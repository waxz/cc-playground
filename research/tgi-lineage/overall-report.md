# From Model-Based Control to Terrain-Grounded Imagination: A Synthesis of the TGI Lineage

This report synthesises the study's 92 paper reports and eight group reports into one argument about how the control of legged robots has evolved and where [Terrain-Grounded Imagination (TGI)](papers/tgi.md) sits in that evolution. It is written for readers who want the cross-group picture; the group reports ([g0](groups/g0-anchor.md) to [g7](groups/g7-humanoid-tracking.md)) carry the detailed chronology, the paper reports carry the evidence, and the [math cookbook](math-cookbook.md) carries the mathematics. The companion files are the [evolution tree](evolution-tree.md), which draws the lineage as a graph and a chronological ladder, and the [bibliography](bibliography.md).

## Abstract

Legged-robot control has passed through three regimes. Classical control reduced the floating-base, contact-switching dynamics to templates and optimised trajectories through explicit models ([g4](groups/g4-classical-control.md)). Model-free reinforcement learning replaced the model with sampled returns and, once GPU simulators made samples cheap, settled on [PPO](papers/ppo.md) in massively parallel simulation as an industrial recipe for both perceptive locomotion ([g5](groups/g5-learned-locomotion.md)) and motion imitation ([g6](groups/g6-character-imitation.md), [g7](groups/g7-humanoid-tracking.md)). A third regime, still forming, returns to differentiating a model, but a learned one, and uses its Jacobians to compute first-order policy gradients over short imagined horizons closed by a critic ([g2](groups/g2-world-models.md), [g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md)). [SuperMimic](papers/supermimic.md) demonstrated this third regime at scale on humanoid motion tracking over flat ground; TGI carries the same training loop to velocity-commanded perceptive locomotion on sparse terrain. The argument of this report is that the thread connecting these regimes is the choice of gradient estimator: zeroth-order (score-function) estimators trade variance for indifference to the environment's structure, first-order (pathwise) estimators trade bias and fragility for low variance, and contact is the point where the second trade turns unfavourable. TGI's components are best read as a set of devices for keeping the first-order trade favourable in the presence of terrain discontinuities. The report also records what the evidence does not yet show: TGI has no paper and reports no quantitative results; several of its central predecessors are preprints; and the decisive question, why backpropagation through learned models failed in [ME-TRPO](papers/me-trpo.md), [PETS](papers/pets.md) and [RWM](papers/rwm.md) yet succeeds in SuperMimic and TGI, has not been tested in a common setting.

## 1. Introduction and scope

TGI is a 2026 software release from ETH Zurich's Robotic Systems Lab that trains a 29-degree-of-freedom Unitree G1 humanoid to walk across stairs, gaps, pits and stepping stones, and an ANYmal-D quadruped to climb boxes and cross stones, without model-free policy-gradient estimation ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §1). Its README names four predecessors, [SuperMimic](papers/supermimic.md), [Watch Your Step](papers/watch-your-step.md), [SHAC](papers/shac.md) and [RWM](papers/rwm.md); the code adds [Success-Guided Sampling](papers/sgs.md) and [AME-2](papers/ame2.md). The study followed the references of those works backwards to a bounded depth (see the [README](README.md) for the construction), and arrived at a corpus of 92 works organised into eight groups.

Two framing corrections are necessary at the outset. First, TGI is not a motion-tracking method. Its G1 task is velocity-commanded perceptive locomotion with a reward taken from Watch Your Step's velocity-tracking set (exponential tracking of planar linear and yaw velocity, stand-still pose and joint-deviation terms), supplemented by foothold-support, edge and impact terms; its ANYmal task is goal reaching with a differentiable transcription of AME-2's reward ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §2.6). The motion-imitation lineage of [g6](groups/g6-character-imitation.md) and [g7](groups/g7-humanoid-tracking.md) reaches TGI only through SuperMimic's training loop, which was developed on, and tuned for, the dense and smooth rewards of tracking ([g7](groups/g7-humanoid-tracking.md), §6). Second, TGI is not a pure first-order method. Its policy loss combines the pathwise gradient of an imagined return with a likelihood-ratio term of fixed weight 0.1 and a mirror-symmetry loss ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §2.5), so it occupies a specific point in the space of estimator mixtures that [g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md) maps.

The report proceeds as follows. Sections 2 and 3 trace the three ancestral lines and the two application lines. Section 4 isolates the gradient-estimator question as the thread that unifies them. Section 5 describes the convergence in SuperMimic, and Section 6 analyses TGI component by component. Section 7 discusses the tensions that cross group boundaries, Section 8 the threats to the validity of the study's conclusions, and Section 9 the open research questions.

## 2. Three ancestral lines

### 2.1 Classical control and trajectory optimisation

A legged robot is an underactuated floating-base system whose only means of moving its centre of mass is a sequence of intermittent, unilateral, friction-limited contacts, governed by $M(q)\ddot q + h(q,\dot q) = S^\top\tau + J_c^\top\lambda$ with complementarity and friction-cone conditions on $\lambda$ ([rigid-body contact](math-cookbook.md#m-rigid-contact)). The classical response, traced in [g4](groups/g4-classical-control.md), was reduction. [Raibert](papers/raibert.md)'s hopping machines established that balance can be maintained dynamically and that foot placement is the principal actuator for it, through the rule $x_f = \dot x T_s/2 + k_{\dot x}(\dot x - \dot x_d)$ that later became the spring-loaded inverted pendulum template ([SLIP and Raibert placement](math-cookbook.md#m-slip)). For walking humanoids the linear inverted pendulum supplied the zero-moment-point relation $p = x - (z_c/g)\ddot x$, which [Kajita et al.](papers/kajita-zmp-preview.md) turned into a linear-quadratic preview controller and from which [Pratt et al.](papers/capture-point.md) isolated the divergent component, the capture point $x_{cp} = x + \dot x/\omega$ ([LIPM, ZMP, capture point](math-cookbook.md#m-lipm-zmp)). Each of these replaced the full dynamics by a model in which a stability criterion could be stated and enforced.

The second classical move was to optimise through a model rather than reason from a template. [Tassa, Erez and Todorov](papers/ilqr-online-to.md) ran iLQG in a receding-horizon loop on a simulated humanoid, with a backward pass $V_x = \ell_x + f_x^\top V'_x$ that is, at first order, exactly the adjoint recursion reverse-mode differentiation computes through a rollout ([LQR and iLQR/DDP](math-cookbook.md#m-lqr-ddp), [BPTT](math-cookbook.md#m-bptt)). [MuJoCo](papers/mujoco.md) was built to make this feasible, with a convex soft-contact model that makes dynamics smooth and invertible. On hardware the full model was too slow, and [convex MPC on Cheetah 3](papers/convex-mpc-cheetah.md) reduced the quadruped to a linearised single rigid body solved as a quadratic programme over ground-reaction forces ([centroidal and SRBD models](math-cookbook.md#m-centroidal-srbd), [MPC](math-cookbook.md#m-mpc)). Perception entered later: [TAMOLS](papers/tamols.md) optimised footholds and base motion jointly over a bilinearly interpolated, smoothed elevation map, and [perceptive NMPC](papers/perceptive-nmpc.md) used multiple-shooting SQP with terrain as convex polygons and a signed distance field, reporting that single-shooting iLQR diverged on stepping stones ([shooting methods](math-cookbook.md#m-shooting)).

Three classical ideas survive in TGI in altered form, and the persistence is not superficial. The finite-horizon MPC cost with terminal cost is formally the [short-horizon objective with terminal critic](math-cookbook.md#m-short-horizon), with the terminal cost learned instead of designed. The friction cone and the per-foot centre-of-pressure constraint migrate from the optimiser's decision variables into the architecture of TGI's world model, which predicts forces inside a friction cone with $\mu = 1$ and keeps the centre of pressure inside the sole ([g4](groups/g4-classical-control.md), §5). And the observation that the raw height map yields no useful gradient beyond the current tread, which forced TAMOLS to build a smoothed "virtual floor" and gradient-norm edge costs, reappears as TGI's Gaussian-blurred support field and edge field ([terrain lookups](math-cookbook.md#m-terrain-lookup)). Perceptive NMPC's divergence of single-shooting rollouts on stepping stones is the optimal-control counterpart of the gradient pathology TGI's event cut addresses (Section 6). Where TGI departs from the classical tradition is in what it differentiates: iLQR differentiates an exact model online, TGI differentiates a learned one offline and amortises the result into a policy.

Two hybrids serve as foils. [DeepGait](papers/deepgait.md) trained a foothold planner by RL against a centroidal feasibility check, and [DTC](papers/dtc.md) had a PPO policy track TAMOLS footholds with 2.3 cm accuracy on hardware. In both the model supplies information while the policy is trained by score-function gradients; TGI has no planner, but a model whose gradients reach the policy directly ([g4](groups/g4-classical-control.md), §5).

### 2.2 Policy-gradient reinforcement learning

[g1](groups/g1-rl-foundations.md) traces the estimator that dominated learned legged control for half a decade. [REINFORCE](papers/reinforce.md) established that $\nabla_\theta J = \mathbb E[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)(R-b)]$ is unbiased for any action-independent baseline, and the [policy gradient theorem](papers/pg-theorem.md) replaced $R$ by $Q^\pi$ without differentiating the state distribution ([score-function gradient](math-cookbook.md#m-pg-theorem)). The estimator needs only $\log\pi$ to be differentiable. Dynamics and reward are black boxes, and that indifference is the property on which everything else in the lineage turns.

The subsequent development never changed the estimator; it refined the weight and the step. [A3C](papers/a3c.md) made the value function the baseline and showed that parallel actors decorrelate data; [GAE](papers/gae.md) turned the advantage into an explicit bias–variance trade-off $\hat A_t = \sum_l(\gamma\lambda)^l\delta_{t+l}$ with an empirical optimum near $\lambda = 0.95$ ([GAE](math-cookbook.md#m-advantage-gae)). The [natural gradient](papers/natural-pg.md) and [TRPO](papers/trpo.md) bounded each step in KL divergence ([trust regions](math-cookbook.md#m-trust-region)); [PPO](papers/ppo.md) replaced the constraint by the clipped ratio objective $\min(r_t\hat A_t,\ \mathrm{clip}(r_t, 1\pm\epsilon)\hat A_t)$, permitting several epochs of first-order minibatch optimisation per batch ([PPO](math-cookbook.md#m-ppo-clip)). [Heess et al.](papers/heess-emergence.md) applied distributed PPO to procedurally generated terrain in the same year. A parallel off-policy branch, [DPG](papers/dpg.md), [DDPG](papers/ddpg.md), [TD3](papers/td3.md) and [SAC](papers/sac.md), differentiated a learned critic with respect to the action ([DPG](math-cookbook.md#m-dpg), [maximum entropy](math-cookbook.md#m-maxent)). This branch is already first-order, but only one step deep: SAC's actor gradient is the SVG(0) member of the family that [SVG](papers/svg.md), [MAAC](papers/maac.md) and SHAC extend through dynamics.

It is important for the chronology that PPO's dominance in legged robotics was not immediate. The founding sim-to-real results, [Hwangbo et al.](papers/hwangbo-anymal.md) (2019) and [Lee et al.](papers/lee-challenging-terrain.md) (2020), both used TRPO. PPO became standard with [RMA](papers/rma.md) and [legged_gym](papers/legged-gym.md) in 2021, and the reason was a change in which resource was scarce rather than algorithmic superiority: TD3 and SAC report better returns at one million steps, but once [Isaac Gym](papers/isaac-gym.md) placed thousands of robots on one GPU, wall-clock time and robustness became binding. PPO's variance falls as $1/N$ in batch size, its synchronous update is cheap, it is indifferent to non-differentiable terminations, actuator networks and domain randomisation, and its hyperparameters transfer ([g1](groups/g1-rl-foundations.md), §5).

### 2.3 Model-based reinforcement learning and learned world models

[g2](groups/g2-world-models.md) asks three questions of each method: how the model is used (planning, data generation, or differentiable imagination), what the model is (deterministic or probabilistic, explicit-state or latent), and how compounding error is controlled. [Dyna](papers/dyna.md) used the model for length-one simulated backups from visited states, which quietly avoided both exploitation and compounding error. [PILCO](papers/pilco.md) differentiated the full-horizon expected cost through a Gaussian process and achieved striking data efficiency in low dimensions, but its kernels neither scaled nor fit contact. The neural-model period then produced the negative results on which this report's central tension rests. [MBMF](papers/nagabandi-mbmf.md) showed that a network predicting normalised state increments supports MPC and also exposed compounding error. [ME-TRPO](papers/me-trpo.md) tried backpropagation through such a model over the full task horizon, found it unstable through exploitation and exploding or vanishing gradients, and replaced it with TRPO on an ensemble. [PETS](papers/pets.md) reported that propagating gradients through its uncertainty-aware models "did not yield an effective algorithm" and planned with CEM instead ([ensembles](math-cookbook.md#m-ensembles)). [World Models](papers/world-models.md) showed how a controller exploits a near-deterministic model.

The constructive responses came from three directions. [MVE](papers/mve.md) isolated the decomposition into an $H$-step model rollout plus a terminal value and showed that the critic must be trained on imagined states; [MBPO](papers/mbpo.md) bounded the return gap of model rollouts and showed that very short branched rollouts from replay states retain most of the benefit ([Dyna and MBPO](math-cookbook.md#m-dyna-mbpo)); [MOPO](papers/mopo.md) and later [RWM-U](papers/rwm-o.md) penalised epistemic uncertainty for the offline case. In parallel, [PlaNet](papers/planet.md) introduced the recurrent state-space model and [Dreamer](papers/dreamer.md) trained an actor by backpropagating λ-returns through it ([RSSM and ELBO](math-cookbook.md#m-rssm-elbo), [λ-returns](math-cookbook.md#m-lambda-return)). Dreamer's objective is the closest ancestor of TGI's, close enough that [g2](groups/g2-world-models.md) (§6) states the relation as an equation: TGI's recursion is Dreamer's with the constant $\lambda$ replaced by a stopping indicator. [DreamerV2](papers/dreamer-v2.md) mixed score-function and pathwise actor gradients and found the pathwise term better for continuous control; [DayDreamer](papers/daydreamer.md) used that pathwise gradient on a real A1 that learned to walk in about an hour; [DreamerV3](papers/dreamer-v3.md), in the arXiv version read for this study, uses the score-function estimator for all actions without stating a reason. The planning branch continued in [TD-MPC](papers/td-mpc.md) and [TD-MPC2](papers/td-mpc2.md) with deterministic latent models.

The robotic culmination of this line is [RWM](papers/rwm.md), which trained a GRU world model on its own 8-step autoregressive predictions, reducing relative 8-step error from 3.99 (teacher forcing) to 0.47, and ran PPO on 100-step imagined rollouts to deploy ANYmal-D and G1 policies zero-shot ([model learning](math-cookbook.md#m-model-learning)). Through the same model, SHAC failed and DreamerV3 only partially converged. TGI takes RWM's multi-step fitting with one crucial modification (terrain re-queried at predicted states) but discards RWM's PPO-in-imagination optimiser in favour of exactly the first-order objective RWM found unworkable. Section 7.1 returns to this.

## 3. Two application lines

### 3.1 Perceptive locomotion: PPO in massively parallel simulation

[g5](groups/g5-learned-locomotion.md) traces the paradigm that TGI is written against. Its first problem was the reality gap: [Hwangbo et al.](papers/hwangbo-anymal.md) showed that for series-elastic actuators the gap is dominated by the actuator, and closed it with a supervised actuator network inside an analytical simulator ([actuators](math-cookbook.md#m-pd-actuator)). Its second was partial observability: [Lee et al.](papers/lee-challenging-terrain.md) trained a privileged teacher and distilled it into a proprioceptive student, and [RMA](papers/rma.md) regressed a latent of privileged parameters from history ([domain randomisation and teacher–student training](math-cookbook.md#m-domain-rand), [distillation](math-cookbook.md#m-distillation)). Both papers ended with the same admission, that a blind robot cannot choose footholds. [legged_gym](papers/legged-gym.md) then fixed the industrial recipe: 4096 robots, about $10^5$ samples per update, time-out bootstrapping ([time-out bootstrap](math-cookbook.md#m-timeout-bootstrap)), height samples around the base, a game-inspired terrain curriculum ([curricula](math-cookbook.md#m-curriculum)) and rough-terrain training in under 20 minutes. [Miki et al.](papers/miki-wild.md) added robust exteroception through a gated belief encoder and a three-scope height-noise model, deployed on a [GPU elevation map](papers/elevation-mapping-gpu.md); [ANYmal parkour](papers/anymal-parkour.md) and [Extreme Parkour](papers/extreme-parkour.md) added agility; [Radosavovic et al.](papers/radosavovic-humanoid.md) and [humanoid parkour](papers/humanoid-parkour.md) carried the recipe to humanoids.

The decisive evidence for this report comes from the sparse-foothold papers, because they show where the recipe strains and allow a diagnosis. [Zhang et al.](papers/risky-terrains.md) produced the first end-to-end RL quadruped on stepping stones and beams but could not train specialists from scratch in 10,000 iterations and needed curiosity, symmetry augmentation and fine-tuned specialists ([intrinsic motivation](math-cookbook.md#m-intrinsic-motivation), [symmetry](math-cookbook.md#m-symmetry)). [Attention-based map encoding](papers/attention-map-encoding.md) obtained one policy across sparse terrains, at several days of training ([attention](math-cookbook.md#m-attention)). [BeamDojo](papers/beamdojo.md) is the closest PPO-based predecessor of TGI's G1 experiment. In its Table II (three seeds × 100 episodes, 8 m terrains, hard difficulty), plain PPO with the foothold reward added reached 0.33 % success on stepping stones and 0.67 % on balance beams, against 91.67 % and 94.33 % for the full method; removing the soft-dynamics first stage gave 42.00 % and 51.00 %, and removing the double critic 55.67 % and 70.33 %. Its remedies, a dense area-based foothold reward, a second critic for that reward ([multiple critics](math-cookbook.md#m-multi-critic)) and a non-terminating first stage, modify the data distribution and the advantage estimator but not the estimator itself.

[g5](groups/g5-learned-locomotion.md) (§5) identifies four mechanisms behind this failure, and they are worth restating because each has a counterpart in TGI. Termination on missteps makes successful trajectories exponentially rare under Gaussian exploration. The return as a function of foot position is a step function, flat on support and discontinuous at the edge, so the correlation between action noise and outcome that the score-function estimator measures is zero in expectation almost everywhere. Foothold credit arrives late and sparsely, after a full lift-and-land sub-process. And variance reduction by parallelism scales as $1/\sqrt N$ while the informative fraction of samples shrinks. The bottleneck is not perception (AME shows the information is extractable) nor hardware (BeamDojo's G1 places feet on 20 cm stones once a policy exists) but a learning signal that says how a small change in action moves a foot relative to an edge. This is precisely the information a pathwise derivative carries, which is why the sparse-terrain problem is the natural test for a first-order method.

The direct perception parent of TGI, [Watch Your Step](papers/watch-your-step.md), sits at the end of this line. It is trained with PPO on 8192 Isaac Lab environments, but replaces dense map encoding by a blind foothold prediction model (FPM) that predicts the next two footholds from proprioception and gates the policy's observation to two 7×7 patches around them. It reached curriculum level 5 3.4–3.5 times faster than full-map methods and 2.7 times faster than an AME-2-style encoder, with zero-shot G1 transfer ([WYS report](papers/watch-your-step.md), §3). The classical foothold planner thus survives as a learned perceptual gate rather than as a command ([g4](groups/g4-classical-control.md), §5).

### 3.2 Motion imitation: from characters to humanoid robots

[g6](groups/g6-character-imitation.md) and [g7](groups/g7-humanoid-tracking.md) trace a second application of the same PPO template. [GAIL](papers/gail.md) supplied the occupancy-matching theory; [DeepMimic](papers/deepmimic.md) replaced it with exponential-kernel tracking rewards, reference state initialisation and early termination, optimised with PPO and GAE ([tracking rewards](math-cookbook.md#m-tracking-reward)), and its ablation is unambiguous (without early termination, backflip return falls from 0.791 to 0.379). [AMP](papers/amp.md) and [ASE](papers/ase.md) returned to adversarial rewards ([adversarial imitation](math-cookbook.md#m-adversarial-imitation)); [PHC](papers/phc.md), [PULSE](papers/pulse.md) and [MaskedMimic](papers/maskedmimic.md) scaled tracking to the AMASS library and distilled trackers into latent spaces ([latent skills](math-cookbook.md#m-latent-skills)). On robots, [peng-animals](papers/peng-animals.md) transferred DeepMimic to a quadruped; [H2O](papers/h2o.md), [ExBody](papers/exbody.md), [OmniH2O](papers/omnih2o.md), [ExBody2](papers/exbody2.md) and [ASAP](papers/asap.md) addressed retargeting, deployable observations, distillation and dynamics alignment ([retargeting](math-cookbook.md#m-retargeting), [residual dynamics](math-cookbook.md#m-residual-dynamics)); [BeyondMimic](papers/beyondmimic.md) compressed the formulation to four Gaussian kernels with correct actuator modelling and minimal randomisation; [SONIC](papers/sonic.md) scaled one PPO tracker to 611 hours of motion at about 21,000 GPU-hours.

Every core paper of g7 uses PPO, and the costs show in the papers' own evidence: OmniH2O's PPO on history-heavy observations reached 47 % success against 94 % for DAgger distillation, SONIC reports that larger batches "improved optimization stability", and the field pays for sample complexity by distillation, brute-force compute or one policy per clip ([g7](groups/g7-humanoid-tracking.md), §5).

A minority strand within imitation never used PPO and is the direct algorithmic ancestry of SuperMimic. [SuperTrack](papers/supertrack.md) (graphics, 2021; secondary-source report) trained a world model on simulator transitions and a tracking policy through short rollouts of it; [ControlVAE](papers/controlvae.md) and [MoConVQ](papers/moconvq.md) maximised reconstruction objectives by backpropagation through a learned model on a single GPU; [DiffMimic](papers/diffmimic.md) backpropagated through a differentiable simulator; [shi-mb-imitation](papers/shi-mb-imitation.md) carried model-based backpropagation to a real quadruped with roughly tenfold fewer samples than PPO. All were critic-free and therefore myopic, bounded by a rollout horizon of about one second, and all assumed flat ground: ControlVAE states that its world model cannot represent environment changes ([g6](groups/g6-character-imitation.md), §6). This strand demonstrates that a learned differentiable surrogate of a contact-rich simulator can support policy learning over a broad motion distribution; it does not demonstrate that it can do so on terrain.

## 4. The gradient-estimator question as the unifying thread

### 4.1 Two estimators, two error profiles

The groups converge on one mathematical question. Policy optimisation maximises $J(\theta) = \mathbb E_{\tau\sim p_\theta}[R(\tau)]$, and two Monte-Carlo estimators of $\nabla_\theta J$ are available ([g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md), §1). The score-function estimator $\hat g_0 = R(\tau)\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\tau)$ treats dynamics and reward as black boxes; it is "zeroth-order" with respect to the environment, using function values only. The pathwise estimator writes $a = \mu_\theta(s) + \sigma\epsilon$ and differentiates the sampled return, $\hat g_1 = \nabla_\theta R(\tau(\theta,\epsilon))$, which requires the Jacobians of dynamics and reward and propagates them through the chain

$$\nabla_\theta R = \sum_t\Big[\frac{\partial r_t}{\partial a_t}\frac{\partial a_t}{\partial\theta} + \frac{\partial r_t}{\partial s_t}\sum_{k<t}\Big(\prod_{i=k+1}^{t}\frac{ds_i}{ds_{i-1}}\Big)\frac{\partial s_k}{\partial\theta}\Big],\qquad \frac{ds_i}{ds_{i-1}} = f_s + f_a\pi_s$$

([reparameterisation](math-cookbook.md#m-reparam), [BPTT](math-cookbook.md#m-bptt)). For Gaussian smoothing with scale $\sigma$, [Suh et al.](papers/suh-diffsim-gradients.md) bound the score-function variance by a quantity proportional to $Hn/(N\sigma^2)$, growing with horizon $H$, action dimension $n$ and $1/\sigma^2$ but independent of how sensitive the dynamics are ([variance scaling](math-cookbook.md#m-variance-scaling)). The pathwise estimator has no $1/\sigma^2$ factor and no dependence on return scale; its variance is governed instead by the conditioning of the Jacobian product. When that product is well conditioned the first-order estimator wins by orders of magnitude, which is the source of every efficiency claim in the lineage: SHAC reports 382 times fewer samples and 17 times less wall-clock time than PPO on its muscle-actuated humanoid, and SuperMimic reaches PPO's best return on the same world model with about 64 times fewer imagined steps.

PPO's position in this space is precise. For a Gaussian policy the score with respect to the mean is $(a-\mu)/\sigma^2$, so PPO's gradient is a correlation between injected noise and the resulting advantage, a randomised-smoothing estimate of the gradient of the noise-smoothed objective ([g1](groups/g1-rl-foundations.md), §6). Massive parallelism made its variance affordable; it did not change its scaling. The sparse-terrain failures of Section 3.1 and the compute bills of Section 3.2 are this scaling made visible.

### 4.2 Horizon and chaos

The first obstacle to the pathwise estimator is the horizon. [PIPPS](papers/pipps.md) found that pathwise gradients through particle models failed not through local minima but through variance from chaos, reaching $10^8$–$10^{15}$ where likelihood-ratio estimates stayed usable; [Metz et al.](papers/metz-gradients.md) generalised the diagnosis, showing that the Jacobian product grows like $\exp(\lambda_{\max}t)$ for positive Lyapunov exponent and that in Brax Ant 400-step backpropagation fails outright while only a narrow band of truncation lengths learns to walk. [MAAC](papers/maac.md) gave the now-standard response, $J_H = \mathbb E[\sum_{t<H}\gamma^t r_t + \gamma^H Q(s_H,a_H)]$, with a gradient-error bound $c_1(H)\epsilon_f + c_2(H)\epsilon_Q$ that makes the horizon a dial between model-Jacobian error and critic-gradient error. [SHAC](papers/shac.md) made this work on contact-rich locomotion in a differentiable simulator with $h = 32$ windows and a TD(λ) critic, and its ablations established both that the critic is indispensable ("No Critic" fails on every task) and that the horizon trade-off is U-shaped ([short horizon](math-cookbook.md#m-short-horizon)). This is the objective SuperMimic and TGI inherit.

### 4.3 Contact: bias rather than variance

The second obstacle is specific to legged robots and is the one TGI is built around. [Suh et al.](papers/suh-diffsim-gradients.md) distinguished three mechanisms. At a true discontinuity, such as a Heaviside step, a Coulomb switch or a geometric corner, every sample gradient is zero almost surely while the smoothed objective's gradient is not: the pathwise estimator is biased, with zero variance, so it is confidently wrong. At a stiff continuous relaxation it is asymptotically unbiased but shows empirical bias: with high probability finite batches cluster at a wrong value. And stiffness and chaos inflate variance ([contact gradients](math-cookbook.md#m-contact-gradients)). [AHAC](papers/ahac.md) turned this into a horizon rule, truncating rollouts at stiff contact, and its single-environment variant beat SHAC; but the vectorised variant had to learn a shared horizon because asynchronous per-environment truncation could not be batched, and the authors report that synchronous truncation within SHAC windows did not help ([g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md), §2). [Song et al.](papers/song-diffsim-quadruped.md) obtained the first real-robot first-order locomotion result by backpropagating through a smooth single-rigid-body surrogate, but had to supply foot placement through the Raibert heuristic because the gradient could not discover it ([g4](groups/g4-classical-control.md), §7). The lesson for terrain is direct: a foot landing at a stone rim is the canonical geometric discontinuity of Suh et al.'s analysis.

### 4.4 Learned models as smoothers

The third strand of [g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md) is the observation that a learned model can give more useful gradients than the true simulator. [PWM](papers/pwm.md) shows that regularised networks smooth a discontinuous objective, yield higher signal-to-noise gradients on a chaotic double pendulum, and are unaffected when dflex contact is stiffened while SHAC loses 48 % of its reward. Three mechanisms are identified: bounded Lipschitz constants damp Jacobian growth; multi-step autoregressive fitting trains the model to be stable along its own rollouts; and a learned model averages over discrete simulator events, producing the gradient of a locally averaged dynamics that is closer to the gradient of the expected return than any single sample path's ([g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md), §4). In effect, analytic smoothing delivers what randomised smoothing promised but the pathwise estimator could not. The counterweight is [DMO](papers/amigo-decoupled.md), which rolls out the true simulator and uses the model only for Jacobians at true states, and nearly doubles asymptotic return relative to evaluating the same model along its own predictions. The two results are reconciled only if one separates where the model is evaluated (true or imagined states) from how smooth it is; they have been compared only on dflex.

### 4.5 Mixing estimators

Because the two estimators fail in different places, several methods combine them ([estimator mixing](math-cookbook.md#m-estimator-mixing)). PIPPS's total propagation weights them by inverse variance per step; Suh et al.'s α-order estimator does the same but caps the first-order weight when it disagrees with the unbiased zeroth-order estimate, because variance estimates cannot detect bias at a discontinuity; DreamerV2 used a fixed ρ-mixture; and [Onoda et al.](papers/onoda-composite-gradients.md) argue that on soft-contact benchmarks per-step inverse-variance weighting suffices because variance, not empirical bias, dominates there. None of these has been tested on terrain with geometric discontinuities. TGI's fixed weight of 0.1 on the likelihood-ratio term is therefore the least adaptive member of this family, applied in the setting where the adaptive members have least evidence.

## 5. Convergence: SuperMimic

[SuperMimic](papers/supermimic.md) is the point where the three ancestral lines meet for the first time at robot scale. From the Dyna and MBPO branch of [g2](groups/g2-world-models.md) it takes replay-seeded imagination; from RWM, multi-step autoregressive model fitting on $k = 8$-step windows; from SHAC, the critic-bootstrapped short-horizon objective,

$$\hat J(\theta) = \mathbb E\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t\hat c_t\hat r_{t+1} + \gamma^H\hat c_H V_\psi(\hat s_H, g_H, \xi)\Big],\qquad H = 32,$$

with soft survival weights $\hat c_t$ in place of hard termination and no target critic; from SuperTrack and ControlVAE, the practice of learning a single differentiable body model across a motion library; and from BeyondMimic, the reward, observation, action parameterisation, termination and randomisation ([SuperMimic report](papers/supermimic.md), §2). Its distinctive modelling decision is determinism: because the simulator's randomness has known sources, the model conditions on the domain-randomisation context $\xi$ (friction, calibration offsets, torso centre of mass) instead of modelling stochasticity. This places SuperMimic and TGI in the deterministic minority of [g2](groups/g2-world-models.md), alongside MBMF, ME-TRPO, MVE and TD-MPC, and is justified only because the setting is simulation with enumerable randomness.

On the 129,514-motion BONES-SEED benchmark, one H100 surpasses SONIC's released checkpoint within 3 hours, a ratio the authors state conservatively as more than 1000 times fewer GPU-hours; but the comparison crosses GPU types, data volumes and evaluation criteria. The more informative evidence is the controlled LAFAN1 comparison (SuperMimic reaches a return of 0.82 in 1.2 hours on one GPU, against 0.60, 0.70 and 0.72 for simulator PPO on 1, 4 and 8 GPUs within 18 hours) and the mechanism ablation in which BPTT reaches PPO's best return on the same world model with about 64 times fewer imagined steps. The critic ablation replicates SHAC's finding: without the critic, training fails at $H \le 16$, and critic-free policies never settle after pushes. Four motions transfer to a physical G1.

What SuperMimic does not show is equally important for TGI. Its reward is dense and smooth at every step; its contact occurs on a flat floor whose geometry is trivial; and its authors removed random pushes from the simulator precisely because a deterministic model cannot represent the resulting state jumps. It does not discuss contact discontinuities as a source of gradient bias, and it lists "no terrain or object interaction" as a limitation ([SuperMimic report](papers/supermimic.md), §4). SuperMimic therefore establishes that first-order optimisation through a learned body model can replace cluster-scale PPO when the dynamics and reward are benign. TGI is the test of whether it survives when they are not.

## 6. What TGI contributes and why each component is needed

TGI's training loop is SuperMimic's almost parameter for parameter: a replay buffer filled by the deployable controller with exploration noise, a deterministic $\xi$-conditioned model fit on $k = 8$ autoregressive windows, $H = 32$ imagined steps of 20 ms, $\lambda = 0.95$, an update-to-data ratio of 0.4 and a critic fit without a target network ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §2.1; [g0](groups/g0-anchor.md), §2). Its contribution lies in four departures, each of which answers a failure mode that sparse terrain introduces and that SuperMimic did not face. The table summarises them.

| Component | Problem it addresses | Principal ancestors | Status of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terrain read from the simulator; analytic rewards and terminations | A learned regressor smooths step discontinuities and invents phantom support that imagination would exploit | [MBPO](papers/mbpo.md), [MOPO](papers/mopo.md) (exploitation); [TAMOLS](papers/tamols.md) (smoothed fields) | Design rationale only |
| Contact-structured world model $s_{t+1} = s_t + f(x) + B(x)J(s_t)^\top W$ with geometry gate and friction cone | No force may act off the ground; contact must respect kinematics | [rigid-body contact](math-cookbook.md#m-rigid-contact), [convex MPC](papers/convex-mpc-cheetah.md), [MBMF](papers/nagabandi-mbmf.md) increments | Design rationale only |
| Autoregressive fit with terrain re-queried at predicted states | The model must learn how its own drift changes contact | [RWM](papers/rwm.md), [PWM](papers/pwm.md) | RWM's ablation (without terrain) |
| Event-gated return: stopping-time truncation at poorly supported touchdowns | Pathwise gradients through foot-support discontinuities are biased | [SHAC](papers/shac.md), [AHAC](papers/ahac.md), [Suh et al.](papers/suh-diffsim-gradients.md), [Dreamer](papers/dreamer.md) | Unpublished gradient-check script |
| Fixed-weight hybrid estimator (pathwise + 0.1 × likelihood ratio) | Supply zeroth-order credit where the pathwise chain is cut or unreliable | [PIPPS](papers/pipps.md), [DreamerV2](papers/dreamer-v2.md), [Suh et al.](papers/suh-diffsim-gradients.md) | Not ablated |
| Model-predicted impact penalty | Gradient must reach actions several steps before a hard landing | [ASAP](papers/asap.md)-style force awareness; TGI-specific | Not ablated |
| FPM-gated perception, continual FPM training, SGS curriculum | Perception cost and exploration on sparse terrain | [Watch Your Step](papers/watch-your-step.md), [SGS](papers/sgs.md) | WYS and SGS papers (PPO setting) |

**Terrain read from the simulator.** A world model that had to predict terrain, or encode it in a latent, would be fit by squared-error regression and would therefore average over the step discontinuities that define a stepping-stone field; in imagination, the policy would find and exploit the blurred edges, the model-exploitation failure that MBPO's short rollouts and MOPO's penalties exist to prevent ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §4). Because the terrain is static and exactly known in simulation, TGI removes it from the learning problem. The model learns robot dynamics conditional on exact local geometry, and rewards and terminations are analytic functions of the state and the true terrain, so there is no learned reward head to be wrong; SuperMimic's model loss included reward, observation and termination heads, TGI's includes none. The lookups are bilinear and differentiable for the G1 heightmap and a column-wise signed distance field for ANYmal ([terrain lookups](math-cookbook.md#m-terrain-lookup)). The price is that an exact step function has no useful derivative, which TGI answers with designed backward surrogates: a straight-through unsupported-sole share whose forward value uses nearest-node heights while the gradient flows through the bilinear interpolant ([straight-through estimators](math-cookbook.md#m-straight-through)), a Gaussian-blurred support field with $\sigma = 5$ cm, and sigmoid gates with 1 cm temperatures. Section 7.2 returns to this "exact forward, surrogate backward" construction.

**Contact-structured world model.** TGI's model predicts free motion and an effective inverse-inertia matrix but computes the contact geometry exactly: sole–terrain gaps, normals, contact points and joint axes come from forward kinematics and the heightmap, forces are gated by $\sigma((0.04-\phi_b)/0.01)$ so that no force acts off the ground, tangential forces are confined to a friction cone with $\mu = 1$, the centre of pressure stays inside the sole, and the generalised wrench $J^\top W$ is assembled exactly ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §2.2). This is a learned analogue of the $J_c^\top\lambda$ term of the rigid-body equation, with the classical constraints of [g4](groups/g4-classical-control.md) imposed as inductive bias on a predictor. Its necessity follows from Section 4.4: learned smoothing of contact, benign on flat ground, becomes phantom support on a stone field. The gate makes "no support where there is no terrain" a structural fact rather than a regression target. The ANYmal variant extends the structure to shank, thigh and belly collision spheres.

**Autoregressive fitting with terrain re-queried.** TGI fits the model as RWM does, rolling it forward eight steps from a logged state on logged actions, but recomputes terrain features and contact geometry from the predicted state at every step ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §2.3). A predicted foot that drifts 2 cm off a stone therefore receives the true terrain at the drifted position, and the model learns the consequences of its own errors near edges. RWM's ablation supports multi-step fitting in general; nothing in the corpus tests the terrain re-query specifically.

**Event-gated return.** This is TGI's most distinctive algorithmic element, and its exact form matters. The gate $\ell_t$ equals 0 at an imagined touchdown whose unsupported sole share exceeds 0.3 and 1 otherwise; it is computed without gradient. The return recursion is

$$G_t = r_t + \gamma(1-d_t)\big[(1-\ell_t)V_\psi(s_{t+1}) + \ell_t\,G_{t+1}\big],\qquad G_{H-1} = r_{H-1} + \gamma(1-d_{H-1})V_\psi(s_H),$$

which unrolls, with $\tau$ the first flagged step (or $H-1$) and $\bar c_t$ the soft survival product, to

$$G_0 = \sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\gamma^t\bar c_t r_t + \gamma^{\tau+1}\bar c_{\tau+1}V_\psi(s_{\tau+1})$$

([stopping-time truncation](math-cookbook.md#m-stopping-time-truncation)). The cut is therefore not a detach of the state or of the gradient: the remainder of the return after the touchdown is replaced by the critic's value of the post-touchdown state, and the policy still receives gradient through the touchdown transition itself, through $r_\tau$ and through $\nabla_s V_\psi(s_{\tau+1})$. What is removed is the chain that would multiply the touchdown Jacobian, evaluated on a smoothed and possibly wrong side of the support cliff, into every later reward. The construction is SHAC's objective with a state-dependent horizon, and Dreamer's λ-return recursion with the constant $\lambda$ replaced by a stopping indicator ([g2](groups/g2-world-models.md), §6). Relative to AHAC it is a vectorisable form of truncate-at-contact-and-bootstrap, since a mask inside a fixed-length recursion keeps the graph length constant and avoids the asynchrony that forced AHAC to a shared horizon; relative to Suh et al. it detects the bias geometrically from simulator terrain at no sampling cost rather than statistically by comparing estimators ([g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md), §5). Its necessity is the argument of Section 4.3; its sufficiency is unproven, because the gate covers only foot-support discontinuities and not knee contacts, self-collisions or base strikes, and because the critic that replaces the model at the cut is trained on ungated λ-returns computed from the same model rollouts.

**Fixed-weight hybrid estimator.** TGI's policy loss is

$$\mathcal L_\pi = -\overline{G_0} - 0.1\,\overline{\bar c_t\log\pi(a_t\mid o_t)\hat A_t} + 2\,\overline{\lVert\pi(\mathcal M o) - \mathcal M\pi(o)\rVert^2},$$

with reparameterised imagination noise of standard deviation 0.2, standardised λ-return advantages, and a mirror-consistency term ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §2.5). The likelihood-ratio term supplies zeroth-order credit, among other places across the cut events where the pathwise chain has been removed; the reparameterised noise simultaneously acts as randomised smoothing of the pathwise gradient. The estimator is thus neither SHAC's nor PPO's but a fixed point in the mixture space of [g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md) §4.5, with no ratio or clip because imagined actions are on-policy. Whether the adaptive alternatives (inverse-variance weighting, α-order guarding) would do better is untested.

**Impact penalty and perception.** Because the model predicts contact forces as differentiable functions of state and action, TGI can penalise predicted forces above 450 N per sole and let the gradient reach actions taken several steps before landing, a training signal that a model-free method could obtain only through the sampled return. The perception stack is Watch Your Step's, unchanged at the interface so that exported policies run in the WYS validation suites, but with the FPM trained continually and the foothold-gated scan recomputed inside imagination. The perception path itself carries no gradient: rounding to grid cells is non-differentiable and the FPM runs under `no_grad`, so the policy gradient ignores how the next observation depends on the next state.

TGI also reintroduces something its parents deliberately removed. Watch Your Step and AME-2 avoid foothold-level reward design; TGI's G1 reward has about 25 terms including explicit foothold-support, edge and flatness shaping, because a pathwise optimiser needs dense differentiable signals at the foot ([g0](groups/g0-anchor.md), §6). This is a coherent consequence of the estimator choice, but it couples the recipe to terrain-specific shaping.

## 7. Cross-group tensions

### 7.1 Backpropagation through learned models: failure, abandonment, and success without a common test

The sharpest tension in the study is recorded in [g2](groups/g2-world-models.md) (§7) and is unresolved. Read chronologically, the evidence on pathwise gradients through learned models is contradictory. [ME-TRPO](papers/me-trpo.md) found full-horizon backpropagation through neural models unstable and replaced it with TRPO. [PETS](papers/pets.md) reported that gradients through its probabilistic ensembles did not yield an effective algorithm. [Dreamer](papers/dreamer.md), [DreamerV2](papers/dreamer-v2.md) (for continuous control) and [DayDreamer](papers/daydreamer.md) used pathwise gradients successfully, and DayDreamer did so on a real quadruped. [DreamerV3](papers/dreamer-v3.md) then dropped them in favour of the score-function estimator for all actions, without explanation in the version read. [RWM](papers/rwm.md) reported SHAC through its own model failing to converge, with "high and fluctuating model error" and chaotic behaviour, on exactly the ANYmal-D and G1 platforms TGI targets; RWM attributes this to first-order gradients being unsuited to discontinuous contact. Yet SuperMimic and TGI succeed, or in TGI's case are claimed to succeed, with essentially SHAC's objective through an RWM-style model.

The candidate explanations are numerous and, crucially, confounded across experiments. They include full versus short horizons (ME-TRPO's full task horizon against $H = 32$ with a critic); one-step versus autoregressive fitting; stochastic versus deterministic models (PETS's probabilistic ensemble and RWM's Gaussian GRU with sampling against SuperMimic's $\xi$-conditioned deterministic MLP); learned versus analytic rewards (RWM learns rewards from predicted observations, TGI computes them from state and terrain); partial versus full observability (RWM's 32-step history compensates for partial observability that TGI's Markovian full-state model does not have); the presence or absence of gradient cutting at contact events; and SHAC hyperparameters that RWM does not report ([RWM report](papers/rwm.md), §4). The [g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md) analysis adds a further axis, whether the model is evaluated along true states (SVG, DMO) or imagined ones (PWM, SuperMimic, TGI).

No study in the corpus holds the task, robot and model fixed while varying these factors. SuperMimic's PPO-on-world-model ablation varies the estimator with the model fixed, but on flat-ground tracking; RWM's SHAC baseline varies the estimator with its own model, but without reporting SHAC's settings; TGI's repository contains a switch (`--pg_event 0`) that recovers the plain SHAC objective and a finite-difference gradient checker (`scripts/grad_check.py`), but publishes no outputs. The conditions under which pathwise gradients through learned contact models are trustworthy therefore remain an open empirical question, and the study's reading, that short critic-closed horizons, autoregressive fitting, determinism given known randomness, analytic rewards and terrain-aware cutting are jointly sufficient, is an inference from design, not a result. A careful reader should hold the RWM failure and the SuperMimic success side by side as the most important pair of observations in the lineage and treat their reconciliation as hypothesis.

### 7.2 Exact forward, surrogate backward

TGI's decision to read terrain exactly makes imagination accurate where a learned model would blur, but every exact forward quantity is paired with a designed backward surrogate whose bandwidth decides how far a gradient can "see" a stone ([g0](groups/g0-anchor.md), §6). PWM's evidence that smoother, less accurate models produce better policies and DMO's evidence that gradients closer to the simulator's produce better policies pull in opposite directions ([g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md), §4); TGI takes a third position, exact forward and hand-smoothed backward, that neither result directly supports. Nothing bounds how far the optimum of the smoothed problem lies from the true one, and the surrogates (σ = 5 cm, 1 cm temperatures, a 0.3 gate threshold) are hidden hyperparameters. The classical tradition met the same problem and answered with graduated optimisation and multiple shooting ([TAMOLS](papers/tamols.md), [perceptive NMPC](papers/perceptive-nmpc.md)); learning pipelines answer with truncation and critic bootstrapping. Neither answer is principled.

### 7.3 The return of reward engineering

Each paradigm shift in this lineage was partly motivated by removing hand design, and each reintroduced it elsewhere. PPO-based sparse-terrain methods added hand-designed geometric rewards (Extreme Parkour's edge penalty, humanoid parkour's footstep reward, BeamDojo's area-based foothold penalty) because the estimator could not recover foothold information from the dynamics ([g5](groups/g5-learned-locomotion.md), §5). Watch Your Step and AME-2 removed them, using observation design or attention instead. TGI reinstates them for the opposite reason: not because the estimator cannot see the dynamics, but because a pathwise estimator needs a dense differentiable signal at the foot. Whether this restricts the gaits TGI can discover, for example near-edge footholds during climbing, is open.

### 7.4 Structure versus generality

[DreamerV3](papers/dreamer-v3.md) and [TD-MPC2](papers/td-mpc2.md) pursue domain-agnostic models with fixed hyperparameters, and RWM explicitly avoids domain-specific inductive bias. TGI moves the other way, hard-coding contact gating, friction cones, kinematic Jacobians and exact terrain lookups ([g2](groups/g2-world-models.md), §7; [g4](groups/g4-classical-control.md), §6). The structure is what makes its model usable on sparse terrain, but it ties the method to simulation where terrain and kinematics are known, and its omission of uncertainty machinery (ensembles, penalties) is justified only while a simulator keeps supplying on-policy data to correct exploitation. Transferring the recipe to real-robot data, in DayDreamer's or RWM-U's regime, would reopen both questions.

### 7.5 Perception versus differentiability, and stochasticity versus determinism

Two smaller tensions complete the picture. Watch Your Step's gate is a rounding operation that TGI leaves non-differentiable, so the FPM's blindness, a virtue for robustness, also means imagination cannot teach the policy to look elsewhere; the policy can learn only to step where the FPM already predicts ([g0](groups/g0-anchor.md), §6). And TGI's determinism inherits SuperMimic's assumption that randomness is enumerable, but where SuperMimic removed pushes from the simulator, TGI keeps them in the simulator and masks them out of model fitting, so the model never learns push recovery and imagination never practises it ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §4). SuperMimic and TGI bet that the stochastic-model lesson of PETS and the Dreamers applies weakly in simulation ([g2](groups/g2-world-models.md), §5).

## 8. Threats to validity

The conclusions of this study rest on a body of evidence with identifiable weaknesses, and a reader should weigh them explicitly.

The first and most serious is that the target of the study has no paper. TGI is an unpublished software release; the repository reports training times (about 7.5 hours for the G1 recipe and 5.6 hours for ANYmal on one RTX PRO 6000) and two qualitative animations, but no success rates, no baselines, no ablations and no hardware results ([TGI report](papers/tgi.md), §3). Every statement in this report about why a TGI component is needed is therefore an argument from design rationale and from the predecessors' evidence, not from TGI's own measurements. The existence of a finite-difference gradient checker indicates that the gradient cut was motivated by measured disagreement between pathwise and finite-difference gradients, but its outputs are not available. The study analysed the training path of the code; behaviour that depends on unread parts of the repository, or on undocumented hyperparameter interactions, may differ from the account given.

The second is the recency and status of the closest predecessors. [Watch Your Step](papers/watch-your-step.md), [RWM](papers/rwm.md), [RWM-U](papers/rwm-o.md) and [AME-2](papers/ame2.md) are arXiv preprints, as are [Metz et al.](papers/metz-gradients.md), [MVE](papers/mve.md), [ExBody2](papers/exbody2.md) and [GMR](papers/gmr.md); [PPO](papers/ppo.md) itself was never formally published, although its use is so widespread that this matters little. [SuperMimic](papers/supermimic.md) and [SGS](papers/sgs.md) are accepted at CoRL 2026 but appeared within weeks of the study, so no independent replication exists; SuperMimic's headline compute ratio crosses hardware, data and evaluation criteria, and seed variation is not reported. Several key results were read in a specific arXiv version (DreamerV3 v2, RWM v5, SuperMimic v3), and later versions may differ; the claim that DreamerV3 dropped pathwise gradients is explicitly a claim about the version read.

The third is the use of secondary sources. Thirteen reports were written without reading the full text: the score-function foundations ([REINFORCE](papers/reinforce.md), [policy gradient theorem](papers/pg-theorem.md), [natural gradient](papers/natural-pg.md), [DPG](papers/dpg.md)), the early model-based works ([Dyna](papers/dyna.md), [PILCO](papers/pilco.md)), most of the classical-control root ([Raibert](papers/raibert.md), [Kajita et al.](papers/kajita-zmp-preview.md), [capture point](papers/capture-point.md), [online iLQG](papers/ilqr-online-to.md), [MuJoCo](papers/mujoco.md), [convex MPC](papers/convex-mpc-cheetah.md)) and [SuperTrack](papers/supertrack.md). For the foundations the risk is low, since their content is textbook material; for SuperTrack, which is a direct algorithmic precursor of SuperMimic's loop, the study's characterisation rests on descriptions in ControlVAE and SuperMimic, and details such as its horizon and loss weighting should be treated as reported rather than verified. The classical group's continuity claims (Section 2.1) are structural and do not depend on numerical detail, which limits the damage.

The fourth concerns comparability. Quantitative comparisons across papers in this lineage almost never share data, robots, success criteria or compute: success thresholds in g7 differ between 0.5 m mean deviation and 0.25 m height or 0.8 rad orientation criteria; Watch Your Step's AME2 baseline is "inspired by" AME-2 and trained in WYS's setting, not AME-2's; SHAC's and SuperMimic's efficiency ratios are measured against PPO under each paper's own tuning. This report has preferred controlled within-paper comparisons (BeamDojo's Table II, SuperMimic's LAFAN1 and world-model ablations, RWM's autoregressive ablation) wherever available, but cross-paper numbers should be read as orders of magnitude.

Finally, the study's construction introduces a selection effect. Because the corpus follows the references of TGI's named predecessors backwards, works that contradict the lineage's assumptions without being cited by it are under-represented; apart from RWM's SHAC baseline, for example, it contains few studies of first-order methods failing on terrain. Errors in a paper report can also propagate into its group synthesis and from there into this report, which is why the corrections listed in Section 1 were verified against the code and the primary papers.

## 9. Open research questions

The analysis leaves several questions that the corpus cannot answer and that would materially change the assessment of TGI and its lineage.

The first is the common-setting ablation that Section 7.1 calls for. On one robot and one terrain distribution, with one simulator data stream, the factors that distinguish the failures of ME-TRPO, PETS and RWM's SHAC baseline from the successes of SuperMimic and TGI (horizon, critic, model determinism, single-step versus autoregressive fit, learned versus analytic reward, true versus imagined evaluation states, gradient cutting) should be varied one at a time. TGI's repository contains most of the switches needed; what is missing is the experiment.

The second is quantitative evaluation of TGI itself against its natural baselines: Watch Your Step's PPO policy on the same validation suites, with which TGI's exported policies are compatible by construction; BeamDojo-style PPO with dense foothold rewards; and SuperMimic's objective without TGI's departures. The relevant metrics are success by terrain type, wall-clock and GPU-hours to a fixed success level, and gradient agreement with finite differences as a function of horizon and support fraction, which `grad_check.py` was written to measure.

The third concerns gradient reliability. The group lacks an estimator of gradient reliability that is cheap, unbiased and usable inside a learned model ([g3](groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md), §6). TGI's geometric trigger is cheap but task-specific; variance-based triggers fail at discontinuities; zeroth-order comparison is expensive. Whether TGI's gate could be generalised to all contact events, including knees, shanks and base, or replaced by an adaptive mixture in the manner of Suh et al. or Onoda et al., is open, as is whether a fixed weight of 0.1 is close to what an adaptive scheme would choose.

The fourth concerns the smoothing bandwidths. The support-field width and sigmoid temperatures determine which footholds are reachable by gradient. A principled account, for instance a graduated schedule that narrows the bandwidth as training proceeds in the manner of TAMOLS's graduated optimisation, would turn a hidden hyperparameter into a controlled one.

The fifth concerns step-size control: zeroth-order methods have KL constraints and clipping, whereas first-order updates through learned models rely on gradient-norm clipping and weight averaging, and whether a trust region would stabilise them is untested ([g1](groups/g1-rl-foundations.md), §7).

The sixth concerns the boundary of the simulation assumption. TGI's exact terrain, deterministic model and omission of uncertainty penalties all depend on a simulator. Real-robot data, deformable or unmapped terrain, and perception errors beyond the noise model would each break one assumption. Whether the recipe degrades gracefully, or whether it needs RWM-U's uncertainty machinery or DMO's true-state Jacobians, is unknown. The absence of any reported hardware result for TGI makes this the most practically important question.

The seventh concerns the meeting of TGI's two parents' tasks. Every humanoid tracking pipeline in [g7](groups/g7-humanoid-tracking.md) discards terrain- and object-interacting clips as infeasible because references are retargeted and tracked on flat ground. A terrain-aware tracker trained by SuperMimic's loop with TGI's terrain grounding would sit between the two works and would reclaim that data; neither addresses it.

## 10. Conclusion

The lineage traced here is a sequence of relocations rather than replacements. Classical control optimised through explicit models online; model-free RL discarded the model's derivatives and paid in samples, which parallel simulation made affordable except on sparse footholds; learned world models restored derivatives at the cost of model bias, which short horizons, critics and autoregressive fitting contain on benign problems. TGI's contribution is to identify what must change for that containment to survive terrain discontinuities, and to make each change concrete: take the terrain out of the learning problem, put the classical contact structure into the model, let the model learn its own drift near edges, stop the pathwise chain at the events where Suh et al.'s analysis says it is biased and let a critic carry the rest, and keep a small zeroth-order term for credit across those events. Each component has a clear rationale in the predecessors' evidence. None has yet been tested on its own terms, and the most important question in the lineage, why first-order optimisation through learned models fails in some hands and succeeds in others, remains open.
