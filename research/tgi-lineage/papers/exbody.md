## 1. Problem and position in the lineage

ExBody is the earliest of the humanoid papers in this group and takes the most conservative answer to the question of how much of a human reference a real humanoid should track. Its authors observe that physics-based character controllers ([PHC](phc.md), [ASE](ase.md)) rely on 69-DoF bodies, actuator gains an order of magnitude beyond current hardware, and privileged observations (global link positions and velocities) that a robot does not have. Rather than track the whole body, ExBody tracks only the upper body's joints and keypoints, and converts the reference's root motion into a velocity, attitude and height command that the legs follow with a locomotion-style reward. The result is a "partial tracker" on the Unitree H1: expressive arms over robust, reference-agnostic legs. Within the lineage it marks one end of a spectrum whose other end is full-body, anchor-relative tracking ([BeyondMimic](beyondmimic.md)), and its successor [ExBody2](exbody2.md) moved to full-body tracking.

## 2. Method

**Goal space.** The policy $\pi:\mathcal G\times\mathcal S\to\mathcal A$ is conditioned on $\mathcal G=\mathcal G^e\times\mathcal G^m$. The expression goal $g^e=\langle q,p\rangle$ holds the nine upper-body joint angles and 18 coordinates of six upper-body keypoints (shoulders, elbows, hands). The root-movement goal $g^m=\langle v,\text{rpy},h\rangle$ holds root linear velocity, roll/pitch/yaw and height. The observation $s_t=[\omega_t,r_t,p_t,\Delta y,q_t,\dot q_t,a_{t-1}]$ omits linear velocity, absolute height and yaw, which are privileged on the real robot; yaw enters only as the error $\Delta y$. Actions are 19 PD targets ([PD set-points](../math-cookbook.md#m-pd-actuator)).

**Data and retargeting.** About 780 CMU mocap clips (3.7 h) are selected by keyword filters on their text descriptions (walk, dance, punch, wave; excluding ladder, stair, backflip, sit). Retargeting copies local joint rotations: each 3-DoF shoulder or hip is treated as a spherical joint whose quaternion is mapped to three revolute angles via the axis–angle representation $m=\theta a$, and 1-DoF joints take the projection of the rotation on their axis ([rotations](../math-cookbook.md#m-rotations)).

**Reward and training.** The tracking terms (Table III) are $\exp(-0.7\lvert q^{\text{ref}}-q\rvert)$ (weight 3) and $\exp(-\lvert p^{\text{ref}}-p\rvert)$ (weight 2) for the upper body, and $\exp(-4\lvert v^{\text{ref}}-v\rvert)$ (weight 6), roll–pitch and yaw kernels for root movement ([tracking reward](../math-cookbook.md#m-tracking-reward)), plus a locomotion-style regularisation set (feet height, air time, drag, contact force, stumble, energy, joint-limit and deviation penalties). Initial states are drawn from the motion dataset, i.e. reference state initialisation. Training uses [PPO](ppo.md) with 4096 Isaac Gym environments over randomised challenging terrain, in the style of [legged_gym](legged-gym.md).

## 3. Evidence

In simulation (Table IV, 10,000 trajectories), ExBody obtains mean episode linear-velocity tracking reward 318.67 against 205.60 with an added [AMP](amp.md) discriminator reward and 87.83 for AMP without the regularisation terms; AMP improves roll–pitch reward slightly (765.85 vs 754.92) at a large cost in velocity tracking. Full-body tracking reduces every metric (episode length 13.28 vs 16.87; key-body reward 397.25 vs 659.78). Removing reference state initialisation collapses training (episode length 0.23). Sampling root commands uniformly instead of from the motion data leads the policy to kneel and ignore $g^m$. On hardware (Table V), mean absolute roll and pitch over 10-second trials averages 0.051 rad for ExBody versus 0.087 rad with AMP across 14 motions, including clips generated by a text-to-motion diffusion model (MDM). Walking with gestures, dancing with a person, high-fives and handshakes are shown on the H1.

## 4. Critical analysis

ExBody's central empirical claim, that relaxing lower-body tracking improves robustness, is supported in simulation but with a confound: the full-body variant shares the same reward weights and regularisers, which were tuned for the relaxed problem, so the comparison does not show that full-body tracking is intrinsically worse on this hardware. Later work showed that it is not: [ExBody2](exbody2.md) on the G1 and [BeyondMimic](beyondmimic.md) track the full body, including steps and jumps. The metrics are reward sums rather than physical errors, which makes comparison with H2O-style MPJPE difficult, and the real-world evaluation (roll/pitch variance) measures stability rather than tracking fidelity.

The design is nonetheless instructive. It isolates two sim-to-real problems that every later humanoid tracker faces: global quantities (root position, linear velocity, yaw) are not observable on hardware, and the lower body is where physical feasibility fails. ExBody avoids the first by commanding velocity and the second by not tracking legs; [OmniH2O](omnih2o.md) addresses the first with history-based distillation; [BeyondMimic](beyondmimic.md) addresses both with an anchor-relative target and an onboard state estimator. ExBody also confirms, as [DeepMimic](deepmimic.md) had, that reference state initialisation is a necessary exploration device for tracking, and its comparison with AMP anticipates [SONIC](sonic.md)'s argument that dense tracking rewards scale better than adversarial objectives.

## 5. Cross-references

- Builds on: [DeepMimic](deepmimic.md) (reference state initialisation, exponential kernels); [legged_gym](legged-gym.md) (massively parallel PPO, terrain randomisation, locomotion regularisers); [AMP](amp.md) as baseline.
- Extended by: [ExBody2](exbody2.md), which tracks the whole body with a teacher–student scheme; [OmniH2O](omnih2o.md), which removes the velocity observation dependence by history.
- Contrasts with: [H2O](h2o.md), concurrent, which tracks whole-body keypoints but needs MoCap for root velocity; [BeyondMimic](beyondmimic.md), which tracks all target bodies relative to an anchor.

## 6. Relevance to TGI

ExBody's design is, perhaps unexpectedly, the member of the group whose objective is closest to TGI's. TGI trains a policy to follow velocity commands over sparse terrain with a [Watch Your Step](watch-your-step.md) reward, i.e. the legs serve a root-movement command and are not tied to reference footsteps, which is ExBody's treatment of the lower body. TGI inherits nothing from ExBody's implementation; it inherits its training loop from [SuperMimic](supermimic.md), which, unlike ExBody, tracks full-body references. The comparison clarifies why TGI dropped the tracking reward when it added terrain: prescribed flat-ground footholds conflict with stepping stones and gaps, whereas a velocity objective lets foothold choice emerge from the terrain.
