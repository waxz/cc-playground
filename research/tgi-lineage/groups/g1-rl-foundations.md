# g1: Model-free policy optimisation, from REINFORCE to PPO and SAC

## 1. The problem

All methods in this group address the same problem: improve a parameterised policy $\pi_\theta$ for an [MDP](../math-cookbook.md#m-mdp) without differentiating the environment. Their only access to the dynamics is through sampled transitions and scalar rewards, so they must estimate $\nabla_\theta J$ from samples, keep the estimate's variance manageable, and limit how far one noisy estimate may move the policy. They fall into two branches. In the *on-policy, score-function* branch, the gradient is a likelihood-ratio estimate weighted by returns or advantages, and stability comes from controlling the size of the step. In the *off-policy, critic-gradient* branch, a learned action-value function is differentiated with respect to the action, and stability comes from controlling the critic's errors. This group sets the baseline that the legged-robot literature of [g5](g5-learned-locomotion.md) adopted, in practice almost always [PPO](../papers/ppo.md), and that the first-order methods of [g3](g3-analytic-gradients.md) and the anchor works [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md) and [TGI](../papers/tgi.md) of [g0](g0-anchor.md) define themselves against.

## 2. The score-function branch: an estimator refined in four steps

**The score-function gradient.** [REINFORCE](../papers/reinforce.md) (Williams, 1992) established that $\Delta w = \alpha(r-b)\nabla_w\ln g$ is, in expectation, a step along the gradient of expected reward for any baseline $b$ that does not depend on the sampled action. In trajectory form, $\hat g = \sum_t\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)(R-b)$. The estimator needs only $\log\pi$ to be differentiable. No derivative of reward or dynamics enters, which is what "zeroth-order" means in the rest of this report: with respect to the environment, the estimator uses function values, not derivatives. The [policy gradient theorem](../papers/pg-theorem.md) (Sutton et al., 1999) showed that the gradient equals $\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^\pi,a\sim\pi}[\nabla\log\pi\,Q^\pi(s,a)]$ with no derivative of the state distribution. This licenses substituting any unbiased estimate of $Q^\pi$, and through compatible function approximation it licenses substituting a learned critic. The background is in [score-function gradients](../math-cookbook.md#m-pg-theorem).

**Baselines and advantages.** Variance grows with the horizon, because every action's score is multiplied by rewards that other actions caused. [A3C](../papers/a3c.md) (2016) made the learned state value the baseline and used n-step bootstrapped advantages $\sum_{i<k}\gamma^ir_{t+i}+\gamma^kV(s_{t+k})-V(s_t)$. It also showed that many parallel actors can replace experience replay as a decorrelator, which made on-policy deep RL practical. [GAE](../papers/gae.md) (2015/16) turned the choice of advantage into an explicit bias–variance trade-off, $\hat A_t=\sum_l(\gamma\lambda)^l\delta_{t+l}$. Here $\gamma<1$ biases the gradient regardless of the critic, whereas $\lambda<1$ biases it only through value error. The empirical optimum it found, $\lambda\approx0.95$–$0.99$, became the near-universal default ([GAE](../math-cookbook.md#m-advantage-gae), [λ-returns](../math-cookbook.md#m-lambda-return)).

**Trust regions.** A good estimate is useless if the step is too large: the surrogate that the gradient differentiates is valid only near the data-collecting policy. The [natural policy gradient](../papers/natural-pg.md) (Kakade, 2001) preconditioned the gradient by the Fisher matrix, so that step length is measured in KL divergence between action distributions rather than in parameter space. [TRPO](../papers/trpo.md) (2015) proved that $\eta(\tilde\pi)\ge L_\pi(\tilde\pi)-C\,D^{\max}_{\rm KL}$ and turned this into a practical algorithm: maximise the importance-weighted surrogate subject to an average-KL constraint $\delta=0.01$, solved with conjugate gradients and a line search. The evidence that mattered was that per-step KL enforcement learned hopper and walker gaits where a fixed-step natural gradient did not ([trust regions](../math-cookbook.md#m-trust-region)).

**Clipping.** [PPO](../papers/ppo.md) (2017) replaced the constraint with the clipped ratio objective $\min(r_t\hat A_t,\mathrm{clip}(r_t,1\pm\epsilon)\hat A_t)$, which removes the incentive to move a sample's probability ratio outside $[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]$. This allows $K$ epochs of minibatch Adam per batch with plain first-order optimisation and shared policy–value networks. On its own benchmark, clipping with $\epsilon=0.2$ scored 0.82 normalised against −0.39 for the unclipped surrogate and 0.62–0.74 for KL penalties. Concurrently, [Heess et al.](../papers/heess-emergence.md) used distributed adaptive-KL PPO (DPPO) to obtain jumping, crouching and gap-crossing from forward-progress rewards on procedurally generated terrain ([PPO](../math-cookbook.md#m-ppo-clip)).

Across these four steps the estimator itself never changed. PPO's gradient is still $\mathbb{E}[\nabla\log\pi\cdot(\text{weight})]$. The weight was refined from $R-b$ to $Q^\pi$, then to n-step $A$, then to GAE. Step control was refined from a hand-set learning rate to a Fisher-metric direction, then a KL constraint, then a clipped surrogate.

## 3. The critic-gradient branch: DPG, DDPG, TD3 and SAC

The [deterministic policy gradient](../papers/dpg.md) (Silver et al., 2014) showed that for $a=\mu_\theta(s)$, $\nabla_\theta J=\mathbb{E}_s[\nabla_\theta\mu_\theta(s)\nabla_aQ^\mu(s,a)|_{a=\mu(s)}]$. This is the zero-variance limit of the stochastic gradient. It integrates over states only, so it can be estimated off-policy without action importance weights. Structurally it is a different estimator: a *pathwise* derivative through a learned critic, which uses $\nabla_aQ$ rather than sampled returns. [DDPG](../papers/ddpg.md) (2015/16) made it work with networks by importing DQN's replay buffer and target networks (soft updates, $\tau=0.001$), batch normalisation and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck exploration. It solved more than 20 MuJoCo tasks with one hyperparameter set within 2.5 M steps, but with large seed variance. [TD3](../papers/td3.md) (2018) proved that an actor ascending an approximate critic induces overestimation. It countered with three changes: clipped double-Q targets $\min_iQ_{\theta'_i}$, delayed actor updates ($d=2$), and target-policy smoothing. TD3 reported returns two to four times higher than PPO's on HalfCheetah and Ant at 1 M steps. [SAC](../papers/sac.md) (2018) replaced the deterministic actor with a reparameterised, tanh-squashed Gaussian trained to maximise $Q-\alpha\log\pi$ under the [maximum-entropy objective](../math-cookbook.md#m-maxent), adopted TD3's twin critics, and was markedly more stable across seeds on the 21-dimensional Humanoid. SAC's actor gradient $(\nabla_a\log\pi-\nabla_aQ)\nabla_\phi f_\phi$ is a one-step [reparameterisation gradient](../math-cookbook.md#m-reparam), the SVG(0) member of the family that [SVG](../papers/svg.md), [MAAC](../papers/maac.md) and [SHAC](../papers/shac.md) extend to multi-step rollouts.

## 5. Why PPO became the default for massively parallel legged-robot training

PPO is not the most sample-efficient method in this group. TD3 and SAC report better returns at 1 M steps, and SAC attributes PPO's slow learning to the large batches it needs. Its dominance in [g5](g5-learned-locomotion.md) follows from a change in which resource was scarce. Once [legged_gym](../papers/legged-gym.md) put thousands of robots on one GPU, samples became cheap and wall-clock time and robustness became the binding constraints. Four properties of PPO fit that regime.

First, variance can be bought with parallelism. The score-function estimator's variance falls as $1/N$ in the batch size. With 4096 robots and batches of about $10^5$ transitions per update, legged_gym trained a rough-terrain ANYmal policy in under 20 minutes. The on-policy requirement costs little when fresh data arrive in milliseconds, and A3C's insight that parallel actors decorrelate data scales without modification.

Second, the update is cheap and synchronous. PPO does a few epochs of large-minibatch first-order steps on GPU-resident tensors, with no replay buffer of millions of transitions and no per-environment-step critic update. Off-policy methods tie the number of gradient steps to the number of environment steps; with thousands of environments they must either take one update per vectorised step, which wastes data, or raise the update-to-data ratio, which reintroduces the critic instabilities TD3 analysed. SHAC's comparison shows the wall-clock gap between SAC and PPO narrowing or reversing on GPU-parallel benchmarks.

Third, PPO is indifferent to how the environment is built. It needs neither differentiable dynamics nor differentiable rewards, so it accommodates rigid-body contact, actuator networks ([Hwangbo et al.](../papers/hwangbo-anymal.md)), non-differentiable terminations, domain randomisation ([RMA](../papers/rma.md)), privileged teacher–student schemes ([Lee et al.](../papers/lee-challenging-terrain.md), [Miki et al.](../papers/miki-wild.md)) and terrain curricula ([curricula](../math-cookbook.md#m-curriculum)) without modification.

Fourth, its hyperparameters transfer. The clip, $\gamma=0.99$, $\lambda=0.95$ and the actor–critic loop work across robots and tasks with modest tuning, the property the PPO paper claimed and [Heess et al.](../papers/heess-emergence.md) first exercised on terrain. The same template carried into character animation ([DeepMimic](../papers/deepmimic.md), [AMP](../papers/amp.md)) and humanoid tracking ([BeyondMimic](../papers/beyondmimic.md), [SONIC](../papers/sonic.md)). It also carried into model-based work that keeps PPO as the optimiser inside a learned model ([RWM](../papers/rwm.md)'s MBPO-PPO), and into [Watch Your Step](../papers/watch-your-step.md), TGI's direct parent, which trains with PPO across 8192 Isaac Lab environments.

## 6. What the zeroth-order estimator costs, and what first-order gradients buy

PPO's robustness has a precise cost. For a Gaussian policy the score with respect to the mean is $(a-\mu)/\sigma^2$, so the gradient estimate is a correlation between injected noise and the resulting advantage. It is a randomised-smoothing estimate of the gradient of the noise-smoothed objective. The method must discover by trial which direction in a 12- to 29-dimensional action space improved the return, and it must disentangle each action's effect from all others over the credit-assignment horizon. Variance therefore grows with action dimension, with horizon and with $1/\sigma^2$, and PPO pays for it in samples: legged_gym needs about $1.5\times10^8$ transitions per policy, and cluster-scale tracking pipelines such as SONIC used 128 GPUs for a week.

A pathwise (first-order) estimator instead computes $\partial r/\partial a$ and propagates it through dynamics Jacobians $\partial s_{t+1}/\partial(s_t,a_t)$ ([reparameterisation](../math-cookbook.md#m-reparam), [BPTT](../math-cookbook.md#m-bptt)). A single rollout then yields a full gradient whose variance does not grow with action dimension. [SHAC](../papers/shac.md) reports 382× fewer samples and 17× less wall-clock time than PPO on its muscle-actuated humanoid. [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md) reports reaching PPO's best return on the same world model with about 64× fewer imagined steps, and more than 1000× fewer GPU-hours than SONIC. The price is bias and fragility, the subject of [g3](g3-analytic-gradients.md). Through stiff or discontinuous contact the pathwise derivative of each sample is not the derivative of the expected return, and [Suh et al.](../papers/suh-diffsim-gradients.md) show first-order estimates can be worse than zeroth-order ones there ([contact gradients](../math-cookbook.md#m-contact-gradients)). Over long horizons, Jacobian products explode, which is why SHAC truncates to short windows and closes them with a critic ([short-horizon objective](../math-cookbook.md#m-short-horizon)). Through a *learned* model the gradient is only as good as the model's Jacobians, and the policy will exploit their errors. This is the model-scale version of the overestimation TD3 diagnosed for actors that ascend a learned critic.

The critic-gradient branch of this group therefore sits between the two extremes. DDPG, TD3 and SAC already use first-order information, but only one step deep and through a learned $Q$. SHAC, SuperMimic and TGI use it $H$ steps deep through a simulator or world model before handing over to a critic.

[TGI](../papers/tgi.md) makes the trade-off explicit in code. Its main term is SHAC's pathwise 32-step return through the world model and differentiable terrain lookups, bootstrapped with a critic fitted to GAE-style λ-returns ($\gamma=0.99$, $\lambda=0.95$, 5 epochs × 4 minibatches, gradient clipping 1.0, all PPO conventions). Wherever an imagined foot lands on poorly supported terrain the pathwise chain is cut and the critic takes over, locally reducing the update to a DDPG-like differentiation through a value function. In addition, TGI injects reparameterised noise ($\sigma=0.2$) and adds a REINFORCE term, weighted 0.1, on the normalised λ-return advantage. Those are PPO's ingredients without ratio or clip, because the imagined actions are on-policy. The zeroth-order term supplies credit across exactly the events where the model's Jacobians are least reliable, in the spirit of the mixtures of [PIPPS](../papers/pipps.md) and Suh et al. Unlike those methods, TGI fixes the mixing weight rather than adapting it to measured variance, and the repository contains no ablation of the weight.

## 7. Tensions and open problems

Several tensions run through the group and remain unresolved in TGI's lineage. The on-policy/off-policy divide was settled for locomotion by hardware rather than by algorithmic merit, and it reopens once a world model supplies cheap imagined data, as in [g2](g2-world-models.md). The theoretical guarantees (TRPO's monotonic improvement, SAC's soft policy iteration, the compatible-function theorems) hold for idealised versions that the practical algorithms abandon. Implementation details such as normalisation, initialisation and learning-rate schedules account for much of the reported differences, as the inconsistent SAC baselines in the TD3 and SAC papers illustrate. Step-size control was solved for zeroth-order gradients (KL constraints, clipping) but has no accepted counterpart for pathwise gradients through learned models. TGI relies on gradient-norm clipping and policy-weight averaging, and whether a KL trust region would stabilise first-order updates is untested. Finally, the bias–variance accounting that GAE made explicit for $\lambda$ has no equally principled analogue for the depth of a pathwise rollout. SHAC chooses it as a fixed hyperparameter, [AHAC](../papers/ahac.md) adapts it, and TGI sets it per sample from a geometric support criterion.

## 8. How this group feeds the others

[g5](g5-learned-locomotion.md) adopts PPO with GAE wholesale. [g6](g6-character-imitation.md) and [g7](g7-humanoid-tracking.md) inherit PPO through [DeepMimic](../papers/deepmimic.md) and its successors, and [GAIL](../papers/gail.md) and AMP feed discriminator rewards into the same optimiser. [g2](g2-world-models.md) reuses these optimisers inside learned models: SAC in [MBPO](../papers/mbpo.md), PPO in RWM, and λ-return critics in [Dreamer](../papers/dreamer.md). [g3](g3-analytic-gradients.md) generalises the DPG/SAC critic-gradient update to multi-step pathwise gradients. In [g0](g0-anchor.md), TGI keeps this group's critic, advantage estimator and score-function term as auxiliaries to a first-order objective, and inherits its tasks, terrain and curriculum from the PPO-trained pipeline of Watch Your Step.
