# g6: Physics-based character animation by imitation

Papers: [GAIL](../papers/gail.md), [DeepMimic](../papers/deepmimic.md), [AMP](../papers/amp.md), [ASE](../papers/ase.md), [ControlVAE](../papers/controlvae.md), [PHC](../papers/phc.md), [PULSE](../papers/pulse.md), [MaskedMimic](../papers/maskedmimic.md); depth-3 additions [NPMP](../papers/npmp.md) and [MoConVQ](../papers/moconvq.md).

## 1. The problem

A physically simulated character, like a robot, can only move by applying joint actuation inside a rigid-body simulator with contact ([rigid-body dynamics](../math-cookbook.md#m-rigid-contact)). Motion capture shows what natural motion looks like but contains no actions, and it was recorded on a different body under slightly different physics. The problem this group addresses is how to turn kinematic examples into a controller that reproduces them under physics, and then how to make that controller reusable: for many clips, for clips it has not seen, for noisy or partial targets, and for tasks that the data never showed.

Three questions organise the decade of work traced here. What signal tells the policy that it is imitating well (an explicit tracking reward, a learned discriminator, or a reconstruction loss)? How is the policy optimised (a score-function estimator such as PPO, or a pathwise gradient through a learned model)? And what interface does the resulting controller expose (a phase-indexed clip, a latent skill space, or arbitrary full or partial motion targets)? The answers changed largely independently, which is why the group cannot be read as a single chain.

## 2. Chronological development

**Adversarial occupancy matching (2016).** [GAIL](../papers/gail.md) did not target characters but supplied the theory used by half of what follows. Ho and Ermon proved that running RL on a cost recovered by maximum-causal-entropy IRL is equivalent to matching the expert's occupancy measure under the conjugate of the cost regulariser, and that a particular regulariser gives the Jensen–Shannon divergence, estimated by a classifier whose log-output is the policy's cost ([adversarial imitation](../math-cookbook.md#m-adversarial-imitation), [occupancy measures](../math-cookbook.md#m-mdp)). The policy was optimised with [TRPO](../papers/trpo.md). Early attempts to apply GAIL to humanoid mocap produced visibly unnatural motion, which is why the next step went the other way.

**Explicit tracking (2018).** [DeepMimic](../papers/deepmimic.md) replaced a learned objective with a hand-designed one: a weighted sum of exponential kernels on joint-rotation, joint-velocity, end-effector and centre-of-mass errors, synchronised to the clip by a phase input, optimised with [PPO](../papers/ppo.md) and [GAE](../papers/gae.md) ([tracking rewards](../math-cookbook.md#m-tracking-reward)). Its durable contributions are episodic rather than architectural. Reference state initialisation (RSI) places the start-state distribution on the reference, so rewards for late phases (a backflip landing) are observed before the policy can reach them; early termination (ET) truncates fallen trajectories, which both shapes the reward and keeps futile states out of the batch. The ablation is unambiguous for dynamic skills: without ET backflip return falls from 0.791 to 0.379, and without RSI the policy hops instead of flipping. DeepMimic's limits were equally clear: one policy per clip, several days of training per skill, a clock-driven phase that cannot re-time motion, and hand-tuned weights. [NPMP](../papers/npmp.md) (2019) addressed the first limit by training 2707 per-snippet experts and cloning them offline into a single latent-variable inverse model with an AR(1) prior, the first reusable motor-primitive space for a humanoid.

**Adversarial style rewards (2021).** [AMP](../papers/amp.md) returned to GAIL with four changes that made it work for motion: discrimination on state transitions rather than state–action pairs, a least-squares loss, a hand-chosen feature map and, decisively, a gradient penalty on real samples. The discriminator becomes a task-agnostic "style" reward added to a simple task reward; clip selection and transitions emerge without a planner or phase. The price is fidelity and coverage: single-clip pose errors are generally higher than DeepMimic's (backflip 0.150 m vs 0.076 m after time warping), the front-flip collapses to shuffling, and on large datasets the policy imitates only a subset of behaviours.

**Latent skill spaces (2022).** Two contemporaneous papers built reusable low-level controllers $\pi(a|s,z)$ on which high-level task policies act, but by opposite means. [ASE](../papers/ase.md) combined AMP's discriminator with a mutual-information skill-discovery reward on a hyperspherical latent and pre-trained for ten billion steps in a GPU simulator; it also introduced random fallen-state initialisation, giving every downstream task free get-up behaviour. [ControlVAE](../papers/controlvae.md) instead maximised a VAE evidence lower bound on state transitions, with a state-conditional prior and a residual posterior, by backpropagating through a learned world model in the manner of [SuperTrack](../papers/supertrack.md). Downstream task policies were then trained the same way, by differentiating task losses through the frozen skill controller and the world model over 16-step rollouts, and a PPO comparison on heading control needed far more transitions and time. ControlVAE is the group's bridge to [g3](../groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md); its successor [MoConVQ](../papers/moconvq.md) (2024) kept the model-based training and scaled it to 23.2 hours of motion by switching to residual vector quantisation.

**Universal trackers (2023).** [PHC](../papers/phc.md) returned to tracking but at dataset scale: one physically honest policy (no residual root forces) that tracks 98.9% of the 11,313 cleaned AMASS clips and 96.4% of held-out ones, follows noisy video pose estimates at 88.7–91.9% success, and recovers from falls without resets. It keeps DeepMimic's exponential rewards, RSI and a relaxed ET, adds the AMP reward and an energy penalty against jitter, and handles capacity with progressively added, frozen primitives combined multiplicatively. PHC is the template for the humanoid-robot trackers of [g7](../groups/g7-humanoid-tracking.md), beginning with [H2O](../papers/h2o.md) and [OmniH2O](../papers/omnih2o.md).

**Distilled latent spaces and unified controllers (2023–2024).** [PULSE](../papers/pulse.md) joined the two branches: an improved tracker (PHC+, 100% training success after data cleaning) is distilled online, DAgger-style, into a variational encoder–decoder with a learned proprioceptive prior, giving a latent space that covers AMASS. Its VR-tracking results (93.4% test success against 37.6% for ASE retrained on AMASS) show that coverage comes from the data the space is distilled from, and its ablations show that mixing RL with distillation degrades the space. [MaskedMimic](../papers/maskedmimic.md) removed the high-level policy altogether. A transformer tracker trained with RL on terrain and objects is distilled into a conditional VAE whose prior sees randomly masked targets, text or object geometry; tasks are then solved by "goal engineering" with no further training, including VR tracking at 98.1% test success and sitting on unseen objects at 96.9%.

## 4. PPO-based versus model-based

The table makes the central division explicit. Seven of the ten methods optimise their RL stages with likelihood-ratio estimators ([policy gradient](../math-cookbook.md#m-pg-theorem), [PPO](../math-cookbook.md#m-ppo-clip)): GAIL (TRPO), DeepMimic, AMP, ASE, PHC, PULSE's teacher and downstream policies, and MaskedMimic's teacher. They treat the simulator as a black box, pay for it with sample budgets that grew from about $10^8$ steps per skill (DeepMimic) to $10^{10}$ (ASE, MaskedMimic), and lean on RSI, ET and prioritised sampling to keep variance tolerable. NPMP's SVG(0) is pathwise but through a learned Q-function rather than through dynamics ([deterministic policy gradient](../math-cookbook.md#m-dpg)), so it belongs with off-policy actor–critics, not with model-based methods; [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md)'s Table 1 label "SVG(0)" should be read with this in mind.

ControlVAE and MoConVQ are the only methods that differentiate through a model of the body. Their pipeline, inherited from [SuperTrack](../papers/supertrack.md), alternates simulator collection into a FIFO buffer, multi-step autoregressive world-model fitting ([model learning](../math-cookbook.md#m-model-learning)) and policy updates by backpropagation through short imagined rollouts ([reparameterisation](../math-cookbook.md#m-reparam), [BPTT](../math-cookbook.md#m-bptt)). Both omit a critic and discount the loss to suppress compounded model error, which limits the effective horizon to roughly one second. [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md) keeps this loop, adds a critic after an $H=32$ step imagination window in the manner of [SHAC](../papers/shac.md) ([short-horizon objective](../math-cookbook.md#m-short-horizon)), and scales it to humanoid-robot tracking on 129,514 motions with real deployment. It explicitly names SuperTrack, ControlVAE and [DiffMimic](../papers/diffmimic.md) as critic-free predecessors whose objective is "more myopic". The model-based branch is thus the direct ancestry of SuperMimic and TGI, while the PPO branch supplies the reward, reset and termination conventions that both use.

## 5. Shared mathematics

Four pieces of mathematics recur. The exponential-kernel tracking reward, $\exp(-k\|\hat x-x\|^2)$ summed over feature groups, appears in DeepMimic, PHC, MaskedMimic's teachers and, through BeyondMimic, in SuperMimic ([tracking rewards](../math-cookbook.md#m-tracking-reward), with rotation errors from [rotations](../math-cookbook.md#m-rotations)). Its boundedness suits PPO's advantage normalisation; its smoothness also makes it a well-conditioned target for analytic gradients. Adversarial objectives (GAIL's JS form, AMP's least squares with gradient penalty, ASE's added mutual information) share [adversarial imitation](../math-cookbook.md#m-adversarial-imitation). Latent-variable models share the ELBO with a learned or conditional prior ([latent skills](../math-cookbook.md#m-latent-skills)): NPMP's AR(1) prior, ControlVAE's residual posterior on a state-conditional prior (reused by MaskedMimic), PULSE's proprioceptive prior with residual high-level actions, and MoConVQ's residual quantisation. Teacher–student training uses [distillation](../math-cookbook.md#m-distillation), offline in NPMP and online (DAgger) in PULSE and MaskedMimic. Finally, every method actuates through PD targets ([PD actuation](../math-cookbook.md#m-pd-actuator)), and every PPO method uses GAE ([advantage estimation](../math-cookbook.md#m-advantage-gae)).

## 6. Tensions and open problems

*Fidelity versus flexibility.* Tracking with a synchronised reference gives dense supervision and high fidelity but needs a reference at run time; adversarial priors free the interface but lose fidelity and coverage. The field resolved this not by choosing but by layering: trackers became teachers (PULSE, MaskedMimic), and adversarial terms survived as regularisers (PHC) or proposed fine-tuning (MaskedMimic).

*Coverage.* Every latent-space method reports that its space does not cover the data: AMP and ASE suffer mode collapse, ControlVAE degrades at 3.3 hours, PULSE loses 2 points of test success to the bottleneck, and MoConVQ and PHC fail on isolated dynamic skills. Coverage was improved most by data cleaning and curricula over hard clips (PHC's hard-negative mining, PHC+'s filtering, MaskedMimic's failure-proportional sampling, ControlVAE's value-weighted starts), all forms of [success-guided sampling](../math-cookbook.md#m-curriculum).

*Compute.* PPO trackers scaled by spending more: a GPU-week for PHC, two weeks on four A100s for MaskedMimic, a cluster for [SONIC](../papers/sonic.md). The model-based branch achieved its results on single consumer GPUs, but with smaller datasets and no robots until SuperMimic. Whether analytic gradients retain their advantage as datasets, terrain and contact complexity grow is the question SuperMimic and TGI test.

*Environment.* All core methods except DeepMimic's terrain experiments, AMP's obstacle tasks and MaskedMimic's training playground assume flat ground, and none conditions motion on footholds. MaskedMimic reports that on rough terrain its controller imitates flat-ground stepping instead of choosing footholds. ControlVAE states that its world model cannot represent environment changes and that model-free RL is still required for them.

*Myopia after perturbation.* Tracking rewards evaluated one step at a time do not value recovery; PHC added a separate recovery primitive, ASE trained from fallen states, and critic-free model-based methods are bounded by their rollout horizon.

## 7. How the group feeds other groups and TGI

Towards [g7](../groups/g7-humanoid-tracking.md), the group supplies the full tracking recipe (exponential rewards, RSI, ET, future-frame goals, hard-motion sampling) and the teacher–student pattern: [peng-animals](../papers/peng-animals.md) transfers DeepMimic to a quadruped; [H2O](../papers/h2o.md), [OmniH2O](../papers/omnih2o.md) and [ExBody](../papers/exbody.md) build on PHC-style trackers; [ASAP](../papers/asap.md), [BeyondMimic](../papers/beyondmimic.md) and [SONIC](../papers/sonic.md) extend tracking to agile and general-purpose humanoid control with multiple goal interfaces, in the spirit of MaskedMimic. Towards [g3](../groups/g3-analytic-gradients.md) and [g2](../groups/g2-world-models.md), ControlVAE and MoConVQ show that a learned differentiable surrogate of a contact-rich simulator can support policy learning over a broad motion distribution, which is the premise of [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md).

[TGI](../papers/tgi.md) inherits from this group through SuperMimic. From the model-based branch it takes the training loop: simulator data into a replay buffer, a world model fit on multi-step autoregressive windows, and policy updates by backpropagating the return through short imagined rollouts seeded from buffer states. From the tracking branch it takes dense, smooth rewards and the treatment of resets and termination as design variables: RSI and hard-motion sampling become SuperMimic's return-based resets and TGI's success-guided terrain curriculum, and early termination becomes a differentiable survival weight. TGI's own additions answer the open problems named above. Its critic addresses myopia; terrain read from the simulator through differentiable lookups ([terrain lookups](../math-cookbook.md#m-terrain-lookup)) addresses the environment gap that ControlVAE and MaskedMimic acknowledge; foothold-gated perception ([Watch Your Step](../papers/watch-your-step.md)) targets the foothold selection MaskedMimic lacks; and the gradient cut at poorly supported touchdowns confronts the unreliability of model Jacobians at contact events ([contact gradients](../math-cookbook.md#m-contact-gradients)), which ControlVAE and MoConVQ never examine.

## Corpus additions

- [NPMP](../papers/npmp.md) (`npmp`, arXiv 1811.11711). Cited directly by SuperMimic (Table 1, first row). It fills the gap between DeepMimic's per-clip experts and the reusable latent spaces of ASE and PULSE, and its SVG(0) training is the necessary contrast for distinguishing critic gradients from model gradients.
- [MoConVQ](../papers/moconvq.md) (`moconvq`, arXiv 2310.10198). Cited directly by SuperMimic as the scaling of learned-model training to tens of hours of data. It is the only evidence between ControlVAE and SuperMimic that analytic gradients through a learned body model scale with dataset size.
- Considered and not added: SAMCON (no arXiv, discussed via DeepMimic), motion VAEs (kinematic, used only as ControlVAE's decoder architecture), PhysicsVAE (Won et al. 2022, no arXiv version located; it is summarised within ControlVAE and SuperMimic's Table 1).
