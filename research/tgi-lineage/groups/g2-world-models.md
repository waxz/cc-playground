# g2: Model-Based RL and Learned World Models

This group traces how learned dynamics models came to be used for training control policies, from [Dyna](../papers/dyna.md) in 1990 to the robotic world models of [RWM](../papers/rwm.md) and [RWM-U](../papers/rwm-o.md). [Terrain-Grounded Imagination](../papers/tgi.md) (TGI) trains a legged-robot policy by backpropagating a 32-step imagined return through a learned world model fit to simulator transitions. It takes its training loop from [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md), and almost every design decision in that loop is an answer to a question this literature posed. Three questions organise the group. How is the model used: for decision-time planning, as a generator of extra training data, or as a differentiable object through which returns are differentiated? What is the model: deterministic or probabilistic, explicit-state or latent, single or ensemble? And how is compounding error handled: short branched rollouts, uncertainty penalties, or multi-step training? The gradient side of TGI's inheritance, meaning when pathwise gradients through a model are trustworthy, belongs to [g3](g3-analytic-gradients.md). This report covers the model side and the points where the two meet.

## 1. The problem

A learned model offers two things to a policy learner: cheap samples and analytic derivatives. Both are corrupted by model error, and the error is not benign. The optimiser seeks out regions where the model is wrong in its favour (model exploitation, documented vividly by [World Models](../papers/world-models.md)), and errors compound along autoregressive rollouts ([m-model-learning](../math-cookbook.md#m-model-learning)). [MBPO](../papers/mbpo.md)'s first bound makes the second point quantitative: with full-length model rollouts the return gap grows with the square of the effective horizon $1/(1-\gamma)$. Every method here trades the information a model provides against the bias it introduces. They differ in where they place that trade.

## 2. Chronological development

**Foundations (1990–2011).** [Dyna](../papers/dyna.md) framed planning and learning as the same Bellman backup applied to real and simulated transitions, with simulated transitions of length one starting from previously visited states. That choice quietly avoided both exploitation and compounding error. [PILCO](../papers/pilco.md) took the opposite route and differentiated the full-horizon expected cost analytically through a Gaussian-process model, propagating uncertainty by moment matching. It reached remarkable data efficiency in low dimension, but GPs did not scale to contact-rich locomotion and their smooth kernels fit contact poorly.

**Neural models for control (2017–2018).** [MBMF](../papers/nagabandi-mbmf.md) showed that a feed-forward network predicting normalised state increments supports random-shooting MPC on MuJoCo locomotion. Its own 100-step open-loop prediction plot exposed compounding error, and it measured but did not train on an $H$-step error. [ME-TRPO](../papers/me-trpo.md) tried BPTT through such a model over the full task horizon and found it unstable, due to exploitation and exploding or vanishing gradients. It replaced BPTT with TRPO on an ensemble. [PETS](../papers/pets.md) attributed the remaining asymptotic gap to unmodelled uncertainty, introduced probabilistic ensembles with trajectory sampling and CEM, and matched model-free asymptotes on four tasks. It also reported that propagating gradients through its uncertainty-aware models "did not yield an effective algorithm". [MVE](../papers/mve.md) isolated the decomposition into an $H$-step model rollout plus a terminal value, and showed that the critic must be trained on imagined states. [World Models](../papers/world-models.md) trained a controller entirely in a learned latent dream from pixels, using evolution strategies, and showed how a controller exploits a near-deterministic model.

**Latent imagination and branched rollouts (2018–2020).** [PlaNet](../papers/planet.md) introduced the RSSM and latent overshooting and planned in latent space with CEM. Its future-work list named the two changes that became [Dreamer](../papers/dreamer.md): a value function beyond the horizon, and gradient-based optimisation. Dreamer trained an actor by backpropagating λ-returns through the latent model, with a critic that made performance robust to horizon length. [MBPO](../papers/mbpo.md) took the Dyna branch to deep RL: an ensemble generates very short rollouts from replay states for SAC. Its experiments showed that one-step rollouts retain most of the benefit and that 500-step rollouts are useless. [MOPO](../papers/mopo.md) turned ensemble variance into a reward penalty with a lower-bound guarantee for offline RL.

**Robustness and scale (2021–2024).** [DreamerV2](../papers/dreamer-v2.md) added categorical latents and KL balancing. It mixed score-function and pathwise actor gradients, finding the former better on Atari and the latter better in continuous control. [DreamerV3](../papers/dreamer-v3.md) reached fixed-hyperparameter generality across more than 150 tasks with free bits, symlog and return normalisation, and in the version read here uses the score-function estimator for all actions. [TD-MPC](../papers/td-mpc.md) and [TD-MPC2](../papers/td-mpc2.md) took the planning branch forward with decoder-free deterministic latent models trained on multi-step reward, value and consistency losses, planning with MPPI and a terminal Q, and scaling to 317M parameters.

**Robots (2022–2025).** [DayDreamer](../papers/daydreamer.md) ran DreamerV2, with a pathwise actor gradient for continuous control, on a physical A1 quadruped that learned to walk in about an hour. [RWM](../papers/rwm.md) trained a GRU world model autoregressively on its own 8-step predictions with a 32-step history. It ran PPO on 100-step imagined rollouts (MBPO-PPO) and deployed ANYmal-D and G1 policies zero-shot. Through the same model, SHAC failed and DreamerV3 only partially converged. [RWM-U](../papers/rwm-o.md) added ensemble uncertainty and a MOPO-style penalty for fully offline learning from mixed simulation and real data.

## 5. Three axes and what TGI inherits

**Model use.** The literature splits three ways. Planning methods (MBMF, PETS, PlaNet, TD-MPC) never learn from the model's derivatives and pay a per-step optimisation cost at deployment. Data-generation methods (Dyna, ME-TRPO, MBPO, MOPO, RWM) treat the model as a sampler for an ordinary RL algorithm. Differentiable-imagination methods (PILCO, Dreamer, DayDreamer, SuperMimic, TGI) differentiate predicted returns through the model. TGI belongs to the third branch and takes its start-state distribution from the second. Imagined rollouts begin at replay-buffer states, as in Dyna, MBPO and Dreamer, and the buffer is refreshed continuously by the deployable policy acting in the simulator. TGI does not plan at deployment.

**Model type.** The dominant trend, through PETS, PlaNet and the Dreamers, was toward stochasticity: probabilistic outputs, ensembles, stochastic latents. PlaNet's ablation, in which the agent "does not learn" without the stochastic path, and World Models' observation that deterministic models are easily exploited were taken as general lessons. A minority line kept deterministic models: MBMF, ME-TRPO, MVE, and the TD-MPC family, whose latent models are entirely deterministic MLPs. SuperMimic and TGI join that minority for a reason specific to simulation. The sources of randomness are known and enumerable (the randomisation vector $\xi$: friction, actuation lag, masses, calibration offsets). Given as model inputs, they leave a nearly deterministic, fully observed transition. PlaNet's two explanations for needing stochastic latents, partial observability and a safety margin against planner exploitation, therefore apply weakly. TGI also models explicit physical state rather than a latent, inheriting MBMF's normalised-increment parameterisation in frame-local coordinates, and it adds structure no method here has: contact forces gated by the exact terrain gap and mapped through the kinematic contact Jacobian. From TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3 it takes network hygiene: LayerNorm MLPs, normalised targets, and a zero-initialised output layer.

**Compounding error.** Three strategies appear. The first is short rollouts from real states: Dyna's length one, MBPO's $k\approx1$–15, and the 12–16-step horizons of PlaNet and the Dreamers. The second is uncertainty: PILCO's moment matching, PETS's particles, MOPO's and RWM-U's penalties. The third is multi-step training: PlaNet's latent overshooting, TD-MPC's multi-step latent loss, and RWM's autoregressive training, which reduced RWM's relative 8-step error from 3.99 (teacher forcing) to 0.47. TGI combines the first and third and omits the second. Its rollouts are 32 steps, longer than MBPO recommends but closed by a critic. Its model is fit on 8-step autoregressive windows from logged states, and the simulator's terrain is re-queried at every predicted state, so the fit sees the true geometry at the model's own drifted positions. It uses no ensemble or penalty. Continual on-policy refitting makes exploitation self-correcting in the online setting, which is what separates TGI's situation from MOPO's and RWM-U's offline one. TGI adds two mechanisms specific to terrain. It takes terrain, rewards and terminations exactly from the simulator, so the model has nothing to hallucinate there. And it cuts the gradient at imagined touchdowns on poorly supported terrain, so error in the model's Jacobians, as distinct from its predictions, does not propagate.

## 6. Dreamer and TGI's objective

[Dreamer](../papers/dreamer.md) is the closest ancestor of TGI's policy objective, and the relation is exact enough to state as an equation. Dreamer's actor maximises λ-returns $V_\lambda(s_\tau)=r_\tau+\gamma[(1-\lambda)v(s_{\tau+1})+\lambda V_\lambda(s_{\tau+1})]$, summed over all imagined states and differentiated through the latent model. TGI maximises $G_0$ with $G_t=r_t+\gamma(1-d_t)[(1-\ell_t)V(s_{t+1})+\ell_t G_{t+1}]$, which is the same recursion with the constant $\lambda=0.95$ replaced by a stopping indicator $\ell_t\in\{0,1\}$. That indicator equals 0 at a poorly supported touchdown and is computed without gradient. Away from such events TGI differentiates the $\lambda=1$ return of SHAC: the whole reward sum through the model, with the critic at the end. Dreamer's uniform λ spreads gradient through the critic at every step, a global bias–variance compromise. TGI's $\ell_t$ is a local compromise placed where contact makes model Jacobians unreliable ([m-contact-gradients](../math-cookbook.md#m-contact-gradients)). Both train the critic on λ-returns over imagined states without a target network, with $\gamma=0.99$ and $\lambda=0.95$, as [MVE](../papers/mve.md) requires. Both freeze the model during the actor update, a rule [DayDreamer](../papers/daydreamer.md) states explicitly. Both weight returns by a soft survival product, which descends from Dreamer's learned discount and becomes analytic sigmoid terminations in TGI. TGI departs from Dreamer by removing the latent state, the learned reward and the learned discount. It also adds [DreamerV2](../papers/dreamer-v2.md)'s ρ-mixture with a small fixed score-function weight of 0.1.

## 7. Tensions and open problems

The sharpest tension concerns the estimator. ME-TRPO and PETS found pathwise gradients through neural models unusable. Dreamer and DayDreamer used them successfully for continuous control. DreamerV3 abandoned them for generality. RWM reported SHAC failing through its own model on exactly the robots and tasks TGI's lineage targets, while SuperMimic and TGI succeed with essentially SHAC's objective through an RWM-style model. The candidate explanations differ across these experiments: full versus short horizons, one-step versus autoregressive fitting, stochastic versus deterministic models, learned versus analytic rewards, and the presence or absence of gradient cutting. None has been ablated in a common setting, and TGI's repository contains a gradient-checking script but no published results. The conditions under which pathwise gradients through learned contact models are trustworthy remain an open empirical question. [g3](g3-analytic-gradients.md) analyses it from the gradient side.

A second tension is between generality and structure. DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 pursue domain-agnostic models with fixed hyperparameters, and RWM explicitly avoids domain-specific inductive biases. TGI moves the other way, hard-coding contact gating, friction cones, kinematic Jacobians and exact terrain lookups. That structure is what makes its model usable on sparse terrain, but it ties the method to simulation, where terrain and kinematics are known. A third tension is between data regimes. The uncertainty machinery of MOPO and RWM-U exists because offline models cannot be corrected. TGI's omission of it is justified only as long as a simulator keeps supplying on-policy data. Transferring TGI's recipe to real-robot data, in DayDreamer's or RWM-U's regime, would reopen that question.

## 8. How the group feeds the others and TGI

The group supplies TGI's training loop in its model-side form: replay buffer, model refit, and replay-seeded imagination ([Dyna](../papers/dyna.md), [MBPO](../papers/mbpo.md)). It supplies the actor–critic in imagination with λ-return targets ([Dreamer](../papers/dreamer.md), [MVE](../papers/mve.md)), the autoregressive model fit ([RWM](../papers/rwm.md)) and the increment parameterisation ([MBMF](../papers/nagabandi-mbmf.md)). Through its negative results ([ME-TRPO](../papers/me-trpo.md), [PETS](../papers/pets.md), RWM's SHAC baseline) it also supplies the failure modes TGI's event gate is designed against. The deterministic-gradient tools come from [g1](g1-rl-foundations.md) ([DDPG](../papers/ddpg.md), [SAC](../papers/sac.md), [PPO](../papers/ppo.md) as RWM's optimiser), and the pathwise-gradient analysis from [g3](g3-analytic-gradients.md) ([SVG](../papers/svg.md), [PIPPS](../papers/pipps.md), [SHAC](../papers/shac.md), [MAAC](../papers/maac.md), [PWM](../papers/pwm.md)). The robotic setting connects to [g5](g5-learned-locomotion.md): RWM and RWM-U reach the zero-shot ANYmal-D and G1 transfer that [legged_gym](../papers/legged-gym.md)-style model-free training achieves, and TGI aims to extend this to sparse terrain. [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md) applies the combined recipe to humanoid motion tracking, and [TGI](../papers/tgi.md) to terrain.

## Corpus additions

- `me-trpo` — Kurutach et al., Model-Ensemble Trust-Region Policy Optimization, ICLR 2018, arXiv [1802.10592](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10592) ([report](../papers/me-trpo.md)). It is the explicit early negative result on BPTT through neural dynamics models, which TGI's short critic-closed horizon, autoregressive fit and gradient cut answer. It is also the origin of on-policy RL on model ensembles, the precursor of MBPO-PPO and SuperMimic's PPO-on-world-model baseline.
- `mve` — Feinberg et al., Model-Based Value Estimation for Efficient Model-Free RL, arXiv 2018, [1803.00101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00101) ([report](../papers/mve.md)). It isolates the "H-step model rollout plus terminal value" decomposition underlying Dreamer's, TD-MPC's, SHAC's and TGI's objectives, and the requirement, which TGI follows, that the critic be trained on imagined states.
