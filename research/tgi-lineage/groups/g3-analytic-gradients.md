# g3: Analytic Gradients: Pathwise Policy Gradients, Differentiable Simulation and Backpropagation Through Learned Models

This group is the mathematical core of [Terrain-Grounded Imagination](../papers/tgi.md). TGI trains a legged policy by differentiating an imagined return through a learned world model and differentiable terrain lookups, closing a 32-step window with a critic and cutting the gradient at imagined touchdowns on poorly supported terrain. Each part of that sentence answers a problem identified by a paper in this group. Read in order, the papers move from an estimator ([SVG](../papers/svg.md), 2015), to a diagnosis of why it fails over long horizons ([PIPPS](../papers/pipps.md), [Metz et al.](../papers/metz-gradients.md)), to a practical fix ([MAAC](../papers/maac.md), [SHAC](../papers/shac.md)), to a precise account of why contact undermines the fix ([Suh et al.](../papers/suh-diffsim-gradients.md), [AHAC](../papers/ahac.md)), and finally to the move from differentiable simulators to differentiable learned models ([SuperTrack](../papers/supertrack.md), [PWM](../papers/pwm.md), [DMO](../papers/amigo-decoupled.md), [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md)). Along the way, [DiffMimic](../papers/diffmimic.md) and [Song et al.](../papers/song-diffsim-quadruped.md) show the approach working on characters and on a real quadruped.

## 1. The problem: two ways to differentiate an expectation

Policy optimisation maximises $J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim p_\theta}[R(\tau)]$. Two unbiased Monte-Carlo estimators of $\nabla_\theta J$ exist under suitable regularity ([m-pg-theorem](../math-cookbook.md#m-pg-theorem), [m-reparam](../math-cookbook.md#m-reparam)). The **score-function** (likelihood-ratio, zeroth-order) estimator $\hat g_0 = R(\tau)\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\tau)$ requires only that the policy density be differentiable. Dynamics and reward are black boxes, which is why [REINFORCE](../papers/reinforce.md), [PPO](../papers/ppo.md) and the whole of [g1](g1-rl-foundations.md) can use it. The **pathwise** (reparameterisation, first-order) estimator writes the action as $a = \mu_\theta(s) + \sigma\epsilon$ and differentiates the sample return, $\hat g_1 = \nabla_\theta R(\tau(\theta,\epsilon))$. This needs the Jacobians of the dynamics and reward.

Their variances behave differently. For Gaussian smoothing with scale $\sigma$, [Suh et al.](../papers/suh-diffsim-gradients.md) (Lemma 3.10) bound $\mathrm{Var}[\hat g_0] \le B_V^2B_\pi^2\,Hn/(N\sigma^2)$. This bound grows linearly with horizon $H$ and action dimension $n$ and with the scale of returns, but does not depend on how sensitive the dynamics are. The pathwise estimator has no such $1/\sigma^2$ factor and no dependence on return scale. Its variance is governed instead by the magnitude of the derivative chain:

$$\nabla_\theta R = \sum_t\Big[\frac{\partial r_t}{\partial a_t}\frac{\partial a_t}{\partial\theta} + \frac{\partial r_t}{\partial s_t}\sum_{k<t}\Big(\prod_{i=k+1}^{t}\frac{ds_i}{ds_{i-1}}\Big)\frac{\partial s_k}{\partial\theta}\Big],\qquad \frac{ds_i}{ds_{i-1}} = f_s + f_a\pi_s .$$

When the closed-loop Jacobians are well conditioned, $\hat g_1$ is far less noisy than $\hat g_0$, often by orders of magnitude in high-dimensional action spaces. This is the source of every efficiency result in the group: [SHAC](../papers/shac.md) reports 382× fewer samples than PPO on a 152-muscle humanoid, and [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md) reaches PPO-on-world-model returns with about 64× fewer imagined steps. Once the Jacobians are ill conditioned, the advantage reverses.

## 2. Chronological development

**Estimator (2015).** [SVG](../papers/svg.md) extended value-gradient methods to stochastic policies and environments by reparameterising both. It produced a spectrum: SVG(∞), which backpropagates through a whole episode; SVG(1), with one model step and a critic; and SVG(0), which is the stochastic form of [DPG](../papers/dpg.md). Two of SVG's findings set the agenda for the next decade. A learned critic makes the method robust to a poor model (SVG(1) degrades much less than SVG(∞) when model capacity shrinks). And "model gradient error can compound during backpropagation". SVG evaluated model Jacobians along *real* trajectories, inferring the noise that explains observed transitions. Most successors abandoned this idea, and DMO revived it.

**Diagnosis (2018–2021).** [PIPPS](../papers/pipps.md) replaced [PILCO](../papers/pilco.md)'s moment matching with particles and found that pathwise gradients failed. The reason was not local minima but variance from chaos: with the random seed fixed, the sample return is a fractal-like function of initial state, and its exact derivative carries no information about the derivative of its smooth expectation. Pathwise variance reached $10^8$–$10^{15}$ where likelihood-ratio estimates remained usable. PIPPS's answer, *total propagation*, mixes the two estimators recursively with inverse-variance weights and so effectively chooses the pathwise depth per step. [Metz et al.](../papers/metz-gradients.md) generalised the diagnosis to differentiable programming. The product $\prod_i ds_i/ds_{i-1}$ grows like $\exp(\lambda_{\max} t)$ when the largest Lyapunov exponent $\lambda_{\max}$ is positive ([m-bptt](../math-cookbook.md#m-bptt)). Randomisation smooths the expected loss but not the per-sample Jacobian products. In Brax Ant, 400-step BPTT fails outright, and only a narrow band of truncation lengths (around 10 steps) learns to walk.

**Short horizon with a terminal critic (2020–2022).** [MAAC](../papers/maac.md) gave the now-standard objective $J = \mathbb{E}[\sum_{t<H}\gamma^t r_t + \gamma^H Q(s_H,a_H)]$ through a learned ensemble, and bounded the *gradient* error by $c_1(H)\epsilon_f + c_2(H)\epsilon_Q$. The horizon thus trades model-Jacobian error, which grows with $H$, against critic-gradient error, which is discounted by $\gamma^H$. [SHAC](../papers/shac.md) applied the same structure to a differentiable simulator (dflex) and made it work on contact-rich locomotion. It used windows of $h=32$ steps, cut gradients between windows, and trained a critic on TD(λ) targets ([m-short-horizon](../math-cookbook.md#m-short-horizon), [m-lambda-return](../math-cookbook.md#m-lambda-return)). The critic does two jobs. It supplies the return beyond the window, so the policy is not myopic, and it acts as a smooth surrogate for the rugged long-horizon landscape, including termination discontinuities. SHAC's horizon sweep is U-shaped: short windows give poor value estimates and use too little of the simulator's sensitivity information, while long windows reintroduce exploding gradients. Its "No Critic" ablation fails on every task. [Song et al.](../papers/song-diffsim-quadruped.md) obtained the first real-robot result (Mini Cheetah), with short windows but no critic. They backpropagated through a smooth single-rigid-body surrogate while aligning its state to IsaacGym in the forward pass. [DiffMimic](../papers/diffmimic.md) applied full-horizon BPTT through Brax to motion imitation. It found that 10-step truncation *hurt* there, and instead replaced drifting rollout states by reference states. That replacement doubles as a gradient cut.

**Contact (2022–2024).** [Suh et al.](../papers/suh-diffsim-gradients.md) explained why gradients through contact are untrustworthy, with three distinct mechanisms. At a true discontinuity (Heaviside, Coulomb friction, a geometric corner) the pathwise estimator is *biased*. Every sample sees zero derivative while the smoothed objective's derivative is non-zero. At a stiff continuous relaxation it is asymptotically unbiased but shows *empirical bias*. With probability $1-\beta$ the samples cluster at a wrong value, so finite batches look precise and are wrong. For a friction ramp of width $\nu$, the bias has size $c_\sigma$ with probability about $c_\sigma\nu$, and the variance scales as $1/\nu$. Stiffness and chaos inflate pathwise variance. The α-order estimator mixes the two estimators by inverse variance but caps the first-order weight when it disagrees with the unbiased zeroth-order estimate. [AHAC](../papers/ahac.md) turned this into a horizon rule. Its bound $B \le HB_rB_\pi(c + B_f^{H-1})$, with contact making $B_f$ large, implies that rollouts should stop at stiff contact. AHAC-1 truncates at $\|\nabla f\| > C$ in a single environment and beats SHAC. The vectorised AHAC instead learns a shared horizon through a Lagrangian constraint on contact stiffness, because asynchronous per-environment truncation could not be batched. The authors also report that synchronous truncation at stiff contacts within SHAC windows did not help.

**Learned models (2021–2026).** In parallel, a line of work replaced the simulator with a learned model in the backward pass, and often in the forward pass too. [SuperTrack](../papers/supertrack.md) (graphics, 2021) trained a world model on simulator transitions and a tracking policy through short rollouts of it, without a critic. [PWM](../papers/pwm.md) made the case that this can be *better* than the true simulator. A regularised model (LayerNorm, SimNorm) smooths a discontinuous objective, so gradient descent finds a much better optimum even though the model is less accurate. On a double pendulum, learned models give higher signal-to-noise gradients than the true chaotic dynamics, especially when trained on long autoregressive windows. When dflex contact is stiffened, SHAC loses 48 % of its reward while PWM is unaffected. [DMO](../papers/amigo-decoupled.md) took the opposite position on the forward pass: roll out the simulator, use the model only for Jacobians at true states. Relative to evaluating the same model's Jacobians along its own predictions, this nearly doubled asymptotic return on dflex. [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md) committed fully to imagination. It used SHAC's critic-bootstrapped objective through one learned humanoid model, with survival weights instead of hard termination, and trained a whole-body tracker on one GPU that transfers to a Unitree G1. [Onoda et al.](../papers/onoda-composite-gradients.md) revisited estimator mixing and found that per-step inverse-variance weighting suffices on stiffened dflex tasks: variance, not empirical bias, dominated there.

## 4. Shared mathematics and its tensions

**The horizon as a bias–variance dial.** Every method after SVG differentiates a truncated objective $J_H = \sum_{t<H}\gamma^t r_t + \gamma^H V(s_H)$ ([m-short-horizon](../math-cookbook.md#m-short-horizon)). Its gradient has two error sources: the critic's gradient error, scaled by $\gamma^H$, and the Jacobian chain, whose variance scales roughly with $\|ds_i/ds_{i-1}\|^{2H}$. That second scaling is exponential if the system is chaotic, and is inflated by stiffness at contact ([m-contact-gradients](../math-cookbook.md#m-contact-gradients)). SHAC, PWM, SuperMimic and TGI fix $H$ near 32 steps, which in TGI is 0.64 s. AHAC learns it. PIPPS chooses it implicitly per step. Song et al. and DMO-BPTT drop the critic entirely, which is only defensible when the reward is so dense that short-horizon optimality approximates long-horizon optimality. SuperMimic shows the cost of that choice under perturbation: critic-free policies never settle after a push.

**Smoothing: randomised or analytic.** Pathwise estimators are unbiased only for Lipschitz dynamics (Suh et al., Lemma 3.2). Two routes restore usefulness. Randomised smoothing (action noise) makes the *expected* objective smooth, but the pathwise estimator still differentiates the unsmoothed sample paths. That is the root of empirical bias and of chaos-induced variance, and it is why zeroth-order estimates sometimes win. Analytic smoothing changes the dynamics themselves: dflex's ramped friction, Song's rigid-body surrogate, PWM's regularised networks, TGI's sigmoid contact gate. It makes per-sample gradients informative at the price of optimising a biased surrogate. Here lies the main unresolved tension of the group. PWM shows that a smoother, less accurate model can produce better policies, but nothing in its method bounds how far the smoothed optimum lies from the true one. DMO shows that gradients closer to the simulator's yield better policies, yet the simulator's own gradients through stiff contact are exactly what Suh et al. distrust. The two results are reconciled if one separates *where* the model is evaluated, along true or along imagined states, from *how smooth* the model is. DMO measures the first, PWM the second.

**Why a learned model can give more useful gradients than the true simulator.** Three mechanisms appear in the papers. First, function approximators with bounded Lipschitz constants (LayerNorm, SimNorm, smooth activations) cannot reproduce the near-infinite local derivatives of stiff contact, so their Jacobian products grow more slowly. PWM measures higher ESNR on the double pendulum. Second, a model fitted on multi-step autoregressive windows ([m-model-learning](../math-cookbook.md#m-model-learning)) is trained to be stable along its own rollouts, which damps the expanding directions of the Jacobian. This is PWM's 16-step versus 3-step result and the rationale in [RWM](../papers/rwm.md) and SuperMimic. Third, a learned model averages over the simulator's discrete events (contact mode switches, solver iterations), producing the gradient of a locally averaged dynamics, which is closer to the gradient of the *expected* return than the gradient of any single sample path. In effect, analytic smoothing approximates what randomised smoothing promised but the pathwise estimator could not deliver. The counterweight is model bias in the forward pass. MAAC's bound and DMO's ablation quantify it, and every imagination-based method limits it by short horizons from real start states.

**Estimator mixing.** PIPPS (total propagation), Suh et al. (α-order with bias guard) and Onoda et al. (per-step IVW, DDCG) all combine $\hat g_0$ and $\hat g_1$. Their disagreement concerns whether variance estimates can be trusted to set the weight. Suh et al. show that they cannot at near-discontinuities. Onoda et al. show that on standard soft-contact benchmarks they suffice. None of these methods has been tested on terrain with geometric discontinuities.

## 5. How TGI uses this group

TGI's policy loss is, in its code (`tgi/training/trainer.py`), the recursion

$$G_t = r_t + \gamma(1-d_t)\big[(1-l_t)\,V(s_{t+1}) + l_t\,G_{t+1}\big],\qquad G_{H-1} = r_{H-1} + \gamma(1-d_{H-1})V(s_H),$$

differentiated through the world model, the differentiable terrain lookups ([m-terrain-lookup](../math-cookbook.md#m-terrain-lookup)) and the critic's input, with $J = G_0$. Here $d_t$ are soft terminations, and $l_t = 0$ exactly at imagined touchdowns whose unsupported sole share exceeds 0.3, $l_t = 1$ otherwise. With $l_t \equiv 1$ this is SHAC's and SuperMimic's objective: [SVG](../papers/svg.md)-style reparameterised actions, [MAAC](../papers/maac.md)/[SHAC](../papers/shac.md) truncation at $H = 32$, and a critic fit on λ-returns ($\lambda = 0.95$). Soft terminations and survival weights avoid the zero-gradient termination penalties that [Song et al.](../papers/song-diffsim-quadruped.md) and [DMO](../papers/amigo-decoupled.md) identify as a limitation of first-order methods.

The **event-gated cut** is a per-sample, per-step variable horizon. At a flagged touchdown, the return beyond that step is replaced by the critic's value of the post-touchdown state. The policy still receives gradient through the touchdown transition (via $r_t$ and $\nabla_s V(s_{t+1})$), but the chain that would multiply the touchdown Jacobian into all later rewards is removed. Its relation to the group can be stated precisely:

- Relative to [AHAC](../papers/ahac.md), it implements AHAC-1's truncate-at-contact-and-bootstrap idea in a vectorisable form. A mask inside a fixed-length recursion gives constant graph length, avoiding the asynchrony that forced AHAC to a shared learned horizon. Unlike AHAC-1, it keeps the touchdown step itself and cuts only what follows.
- Relative to [Suh et al.](../papers/suh-diffsim-gradients.md), its trigger targets the case they identify as worst. A foot landing at a stone edge or gap rim is a geometric discontinuity, and the model's contact gate, a sigmoid in the sole–terrain gap with 1 cm temperature, is a stiff relaxation of it, the setting of empirical bias. Where Suh et al. detect bias statistically by comparing estimators, TGI detects it geometrically from simulator terrain, at no sampling cost.
- Relative to [PWM](../papers/pwm.md), TGI accepts model smoothing at ordinary touchdowns on supported terrain, where it does not cut. But because terrain is read exactly from the simulator rather than learned, edges stay sharp. Gradients near them are therefore exactly the ones a smooth model cannot make reliable, and those are the ones TGI cuts. TGI thus combines analytic smoothing where it is harmless with truncation where it is not.
- Relative to [PIPPS](../papers/pipps.md) and [Onoda et al.](../papers/onoda-composite-gradients.md), TGI adds a likelihood-ratio term with fixed weight 0.1, using imagined exploration noise and normalised λ-return advantages. This is a non-adaptive estimator mixture that supplies zeroth-order credit, among other places across the cut events. Per-step inverse-variance weighting is an untested alternative.
- Relative to [DiffMimic](../papers/diffmimic.md), whose Demonstration Replay also severs the backward chain when a rollout event (tracking failure) occurs, TGI's cut is conditioned on gradient reliability rather than tracking error, and bootstraps from a critic rather than a reference state.

What the group does *not* yet provide is evidence for TGI's specific threshold or trigger. AHAC's negative remark about synchronous truncation, and Onoda et al.'s finding that bias matters little on soft-contact benchmarks, both caution that the benefit of an event cut is an empirical question for sparse terrain. TGI's repository supplies a configuration switch (`--pg_event 0` recovers the plain SHAC objective), but no published ablation.

## 6. Open problems

Four problems remain open. The group lacks any estimator of gradient reliability that is cheap, unbiased and usable inside a learned model. Variance fails at discontinuities, zeroth-order comparison is expensive, and geometric triggers like TGI's are task-specific. No principled rule bounds the bias introduced by model smoothing, so "smooth enough but not too smooth" is set by architecture choice. The forward-pass question, imagined states (PWM, SuperMimic, TGI) or true states (SVG, DMO), has been ablated only on dflex. And the horizon remains a hyperparameter whose best value correlates with gait period (AHAC). Event-based truncation, as in AHAC-1 and TGI, is one attempt to make it state-dependent.

## 7. Links to other groups

The score-function side of every comparison is [g1](g1-rl-foundations.md) ([REINFORCE](../papers/reinforce.md), [PPO](../papers/ppo.md); [DDPG](../papers/ddpg.md) and [SAC](../papers/sac.md) use the same pathwise idea through a learned Q, i.e. SVG(0)). The world models that make imagination possible, and the autoregressive training that keeps them stable, are [g2](g2-world-models.md) ([PILCO](../papers/pilco.md), [PETS](../papers/pets.md), [MBPO](../papers/mbpo.md), [Dreamer](../papers/dreamer.md), [TD-MPC2](../papers/td-mpc2.md), [RWM](../papers/rwm.md)). Dreamer's actor is itself a pathwise λ-return objective in latent space. The physics being differentiated, and the analytic surrogates (single-rigid-body models, PD layers), come from [g4](g4-classical-control.md) ([MuJoCo](../papers/mujoco.md), [convex MPC](../papers/convex-mpc-cheetah.md), [Raibert](../papers/raibert.md)). The tracking objectives of SuperTrack, DiffMimic and SuperMimic come from [g6](g6-character-imitation.md) ([DeepMimic](../papers/deepmimic.md), [ControlVAE](../papers/controlvae.md)). The anchor group [g0](g0-anchor.md) ([SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md), [Watch Your Step](../papers/watch-your-step.md), [TGI](../papers/tgi.md)) is where these components are assembled.
