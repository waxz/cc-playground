# g7: Humanoid Whole-Body Motion Tracking on Real Robots

This group traces how physics-based motion imitation, developed for animated characters in [g6](g6-character-imitation.md), was carried onto physical legged robots and then scaled into the general-purpose humanoid trackers that [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md) takes as its starting point. Its seven core papers are [peng-animals](../papers/peng-animals.md), [ExBody](../papers/exbody.md), [H2O](../papers/h2o.md), [OmniH2O](../papers/omnih2o.md), [ASAP](../papers/asap.md), [BeyondMimic](../papers/beyondmimic.md) and [SONIC](../papers/sonic.md); three depth-3 additions ([shi-mb-imitation](../papers/shi-mb-imitation.md), [ExBody2](../papers/exbody2.md), [GMR](../papers/gmr.md)) fill gaps in the estimator, sim-to-real and retargeting threads. Every core paper optimises its policy with [PPO](../papers/ppo.md). That shared estimator, and the costs it imposes, is the thread that connects this group to SuperMimic and, through SuperMimic's training loop, to TGI.

## 1. The problem

A tracking controller receives a reference motion, usually human or animal mocap retargeted to the robot, and must reproduce it under the robot's real dynamics. In simulation the problem was effectively solved by [DeepMimic](../papers/deepmimic.md) for single clips and by [PHC](../papers/phc.md) for a 40-hour library: a reward made of exponentiated tracking errors, reference state initialisation, early termination and PPO ([tracking reward](../math-cookbook.md#m-tracking-reward)). Transferring it to hardware raises four problems that the graphics setting hides. The source morphology differs from the robot's, so references must be retargeted and some become infeasible. Simulated characters use idealised actuators and unlimited torque, so the policy must learn under realistic PD actuation ([PD actuators](../math-cookbook.md#m-pd-actuator)). Global quantities that character controllers observe freely (root position, linear velocity, per-link velocities) are unobservable or noisy on a robot. And the residual simulator–reality mismatch must be closed by randomisation, adaptation, identification or careful modelling ([domain randomisation](../math-cookbook.md#m-domain-rand)). The papers differ chiefly in which of these they attack and how; the gradient estimator stays fixed.

## 2. Chronological development

**From characters to a quadruped (2020).** [peng-animals](../papers/peng-animals.md) established the pipeline every later paper repeats: keypoint IK retargeting from dog mocap to the Laikago, DeepMimic-style imitation with PPO (about 200 million samples per clip) under randomised mass, inertia, motor strength, friction and latency, and a dedicated sim-to-real stage. Its stage was a latent encoding of the randomised dynamics, regularised by an information bottleneck and searched on the real robot with advantage-weighted regression over about 50 trials. Adaptation lifted Dog Pace from 0.395 to 0.827 normalised return. The latent search did not survive into the humanoid work, which could not afford tens of real trials per clip, but the problem decomposition did.

**First humanoid trackers (early 2024).** Two concurrent papers took opposite positions on how much of a human reference a full-size humanoid (Unitree H1) should track. [ExBody](../papers/exbody.md) tracked only upper-body joints and keypoints and turned the reference's root motion into velocity, attitude and height commands for legs trained with locomotion-style rewards; full-body tracking under the same reward did worse in its simulated ablation. [H2O](../papers/h2o.md) tracked whole-body keypoints, introduced an SMPL-shape-fit retargeter and a "sim-to-data" filter in which a privileged [PULSE](../papers/pulse.md)-style imitator flags clips it cannot track (8.5k of 10k AMASS sequences survived), and restricted the actor to deployable observations, except that it still needed motion capture for root linear velocity. Both relied on heavy randomisation and long regulariser lists (H2O's reward has seventeen terms).

**Distillation as the route to deployability (mid 2024).** [OmniH2O](../papers/omnih2o.md) removed H2O's motion-capture dependency by distilling a privileged PPO teacher into a student that sees 25 steps of proprioceptive history and sparse head/hand goals, using DAgger ([distillation](../math-cookbook.md#m-distillation)), the teacher–student pattern of legged locomotion ([Lee et al.](../papers/lee-challenging-terrain.md), [RMA](../papers/rma.md)). Its key ablation concerns the estimator: PPO trained directly on the history-heavy input reached 47 % success, against 94 % for DAgger. Real-world global error on standing motions fell from 87 to 48 mm relative to H2O. [ExBody2](../papers/exbody2.md) adopted teacher–student distillation on the G1, replaced global keypoints by local ones plus root-velocity tracking, and curated data by thresholding the learner's own lower-body error.

**Agility and dynamics alignment (early 2025).** [ASAP](../papers/asap.md) addressed the regime randomisation handles badly: a few very agile clips on the G1. Per-motion, phase-conditioned PPO trackers are pre-trained in IsaacGym; real trajectories recorded with motion capture then train a PPO "delta action" policy so that $f^{\text{sim}}(s,a+\pi^\Delta(s,a))$ reproduces real transitions; the tracker is fine-tuned in this corrected simulator and deployed without $\pi^\Delta$. A residual state model (DeltaDynamics) compounded error under rollout, while the delta-action correction cut 1-second open-loop error from 81 to 38 mm in sim-to-sim tests. On hardware a 4-DoF ankle model trained on 100 clips reduced error on a kick from 61 to 50 mm. ASAP is the group's most explicit treatment of the simulator as a model to be corrected with data, and its DeltaDynamics result is a small-scale instance of the compounding-error problem of [g2](g2-world-models.md) ([model learning](../math-cookbook.md#m-model-learning)).

**A compact tracking formulation (late 2025).** [BeyondMimic](../papers/beyondmimic.md) argued that the regularisation and randomisation of earlier pipelines were compensating for modelling and implementation errors. It derived PD gains from reflected rotor inertia (armature), removed observation history, eliminated deployment latency with a C++ stack, randomised only friction, joint offsets, torso CoM and pushes, and reduced the reward to four Gaussian kernels over anchor-relative body poses and twists plus three penalties. One PPO configuration trained every clip, including aerial cartwheels and sprinting, and transferred zero-shot. Its ablations (latency of 5–10 ms causing failures, zero armature causing self-collision) are among the clearest sim-to-real evidence in the group. Its second stage distils per-motion experts into a latent state–action diffusion model steered at test time by classifier guidance. The formulation became the default in public tooling, the harness of [GMR](../papers/gmr.md)'s retargeting study, the basis of SONIC's environment and the interface SuperMimic retains.

**Scaling one tracker (late 2025–2026).** [SONIC](../papers/sonic.md) adopted BeyondMimic's settings and asked whether a single PPO policy improves with scale: 1.2M to 42M parameters, 4M to 100M frames (611 h, retargeted with GMR), and 16 to 128 GPUs (about 21k GPU-hours for the full run). Held-out success rose from 98.0 % to 99.6 % and the policy reached 123/124 real-world motions. A quantised multi-encoder token space unified robot, human and VR inputs for teleoperation, a kinematic planner and a VLA. SONIC's own observation that larger batches "improved optimization stability" is the variance of the likelihood-ratio estimator made visible as cost.

| [SONIC](../papers/sonic.md) | G1 | proprietary mocap, 611 h | one for library | PPO at 128 GPUs | BeyondMimic settings, DR incl. command noise | 123/124 clips, MPJPE |
| [shi-mb-imitation](../papers/shi-mb-imitation.md) | Max quadruped | dog mocap | one VAE policy | BPTT through learned model, no critic | real-data model refit, 2 min | velocity/path errors |
| [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md) (g0) | G1 | BONES-SEED, 129k clips, 264 h | one for library | BPTT through learned world model + critic | BeyondMimic DR as model context $\xi$ | diverse clips on G1 |

Three regularities stand out. The estimator column is uniform until the last two rows. The trend in sim-to-real mechanism runs from adaptation on the robot (2020) through heavy randomisation and distillation (2024) and real-data alignment (2025) to correct modelling with minimal randomisation (BeyondMimic). And policy scope oscillates: generalists that look unnatural (H2O, OmniH2O), specialists that need per-clip runs (ASAP, BeyondMimic), and a generalist made natural by scale (SONIC).

## 4. Shared mathematics

All core papers solve a goal-conditioned POMDP ([MDP](../math-cookbook.md#m-mdp)) whose goal is a window of future reference frames and whose reward is a weighted sum of terms $\exp(-\bar e_s/\sigma_s^2)$ over joint, body, root and velocity errors, with orientation errors measured by the log map ([rotations](../math-cookbook.md#m-rotations)); BeyondMimic's anchor-relative transform $\mathcal A(T^{\text{ref}}_b,T_{\text{anchor}})$ is the most refined form. Actions are PD set-points; BeyondMimic's $\theta^{\text{sp}}=\theta_0+\alpha\odot a$ with $k_p=I\omega^2$, $k_d=2I\zeta\omega$ makes them torque proxies. Exploration is aided by reference state initialisation, early termination and failure-weighted reset sampling ([curriculum](../math-cookbook.md#m-curriculum)). Optimisation is PPO's clipped surrogate with GAE ([PPO](../math-cookbook.md#m-ppo-clip), [GAE](../math-cookbook.md#m-advantage-gae)), i.e. the score-function estimator ([policy gradient](../math-cookbook.md#m-pg-theorem)), with asymmetric critics that see privileged state. Deployability is obtained either by restricting the actor's observation, by DAgger distillation from a privileged teacher, or by both. Latent interfaces for downstream use (BeyondMimic's VAE and diffusion, SONIC's FSQ tokens) draw on [latent skills](../math-cookbook.md#m-latent-skills); BeyondMimic's guidance is inference-time optimisation of a diffusion model's score, which the cookbook does not yet cover.

## 5. Tensions and open problems

**What PPO costs and how papers pay.** The group exhibits three ways of paying for PPO's sample complexity on a large library. Distillation trains specialists or privileged teachers and compresses them (OmniH2O, ExBody2, BeyondMimic's diffusion stage); it is lossy and multiplies runs. Brute force trains one policy with cluster-scale compute (SONIC, about 21k GPU-hours). Restriction trains one policy per clip (ASAP, BeyondMimic's tracking stage). OmniH2O's failure of PPO on a history-heavy input and SONIC's batch-size dependence are direct symptoms of estimator variance. [shi-mb-imitation](../papers/shi-mb-imitation.md) offered a fourth way on a quadruped, backpropagating a tracking loss through a learned model with about tenfold fewer samples than PPO, but without a critic and at small scale. None of the core papers questions the estimator itself.

**Specialists versus generalists.** BeyondMimic attributes generalists' unnatural motion to insufficient exploration; SONIC shows a generalist can be natural if large enough; SuperMimic claims a single generalist trained by analytic gradients surpasses SONIC's checkpoint in 3 GPU-hours. These claims are not measured on matched data and evaluations, and the group lacks a controlled study of naturalness versus scope.

**Randomise or model?** H2O and OmniH2O randomised broadly; BeyondMimic shows that correct armature, latency and minimal randomisation suffice on the G1; ASAP shows that residual mismatch at the ankle remains and can be learned from real data. Whether BeyondMimic's recipe holds on less well-characterised hardware is untested.

**Data quality.** Feasibility filters (H2O, OmniH2O, ASAP, ExBody2) conflate infeasible clips with clips the learner failed to master; GMR shows that specific retargeting artefacts are fatal even for a robust formulation. Every pipeline removes terrain- and object-interacting clips (stairs, ladders, chairs) as infeasible, because references are retargeted and tracked on flat ground. Terrain-aware tracking is open.

**Evaluation.** Success criteria differ (0.5 m mean deviation in H2O/OmniH2O/ASAP; 0.25 m height or 0.8 rad orientation thresholds in BeyondMimic, SONIC and SuperMimic), real-world tests are small, and cross-paper comparisons are rarely data-matched.

## 6. How the group feeds the other groups and TGI

Backward, the group depends on [g6](g6-character-imitation.md) for the tracking recipe ([DeepMimic](../papers/deepmimic.md), [PHC](../papers/phc.md), [PULSE](../papers/pulse.md), [MaskedMimic](../papers/maskedmimic.md)) and for the contrast with adversarial imitation ([AMP](../papers/amp.md), [ASE](../papers/ase.md)) that ExBody and SONIC argue against; on [g1](g1-rl-foundations.md) for PPO; and on [g5](g5-learned-locomotion.md) for massively parallel training ([legged_gym](../papers/legged-gym.md)), sim-to-real practice ([Hwangbo et al.](../papers/hwangbo-anymal.md)) and privileged teacher–student learning ([Lee et al.](../papers/lee-challenging-terrain.md), [RMA](../papers/rma.md)).

Forward, the group supplies SuperMimic with everything except its optimiser. SuperMimic keeps BeyondMimic's reward (without the self-collision penalty, which it argues only guards a stochastic policy against an exploit), observation (with a 10-frame history and a 10-frame future reference), action parameterisation, termination (without the end-effector condition) and randomisation (without pushes, which its deterministic model cannot represent), feeds the randomised parameters to the world model as context $\xi$, converts failure-based reset sampling to return-based sampling, trains on BONES-SEED (SONIC's public data, retargeted with GMR), and uses SONIC's checkpoint as its compute baseline. What it changes is precisely what this group leaves fixed: the score-function gradient is replaced by backpropagation through a learned, differentiable world model over 32-step imagined rollouts with a critic bootstrap, in the manner of [SHAC](../papers/shac.md) and the other pathwise methods of [g3](g3-analytic-gradients.md) ([pathwise gradient](../math-cookbook.md#m-reparam), [BPTT](../math-cookbook.md#m-bptt), [short horizon](../math-cookbook.md#m-short-horizon)), and one model is amortised across the whole library.

TGI ([report](../papers/tgi.md)) takes SuperMimic's training loop (replay buffer, deterministic model conditioned on $\xi$, $k$-step autoregressive fitting, critic-bootstrapped BPTT, set-point actions) but not its tracking objective: it trains velocity-commanded perceptive locomotion over sparse terrain with a reward from [Watch Your Step](../papers/watch-your-step.md). This group's influence on TGI is therefore indirect. It produced the problem on which SuperMimic demonstrated that analytic gradients through a learned model can replace PPO for a real humanoid, a dense, smooth tracking reward that suits first-order gradients. TGI's terrain discontinuities remove that smoothness, which is why it adds contact structure and gradient cuts. Two group results bear directly on TGI's design: ASAP's evidence that a naive residual state model compounds error (TGI fits autoregressively and re-queries exact terrain at predicted states), and ASAP's identification of the G1 ankle as the dominant mismatch, relevant to precise foot placement. ExBody's split, where legs serve a root-velocity command rather than reference footsteps, is closer to TGI's objective than any full-body tracker. A terrain-aware tracker, which would reclaim the clips every pipeline here discards, would sit between TGI and SuperMimic and is not addressed by either.

## Corpus additions

- [shi-mb-imitation](../papers/shi-mb-imitation.md) — Shi et al., "An Efficient Model-Based Approach on Learning Agile Motor Skills without Reinforcement", ICRA 2024, arXiv 2403.01962. The only hardware motion-imitation work before SuperMimic that trains the policy by backpropagation through a learned world model (cited by SuperMimic as [16]); it links [peng-animals](../papers/peng-animals.md)'s task to SuperMimic's estimator.
- [GMR](../papers/gmr.md) — Araújo et al., "Retargeting Matters: General Motion Retargeting for Humanoid Motion Tracking", arXiv 2510.02252. Retargeting was otherwise covered only incidentally; GMR evaluates it with BeyondMimic as a fixed harness and is the retargeter of SONIC's dataset, hence upstream of SuperMimic's BONES-SEED training data.
- [ExBody2](../papers/exbody2.md) — Ji et al., "ExBody2: Advanced Expressive Humanoid Whole-Body Control", arXiv 2412.13196. Bridges ExBody/OmniH2O and BeyondMimic: full-body G1 tracking, teacher–student distillation, local keypoint targets and learner-error data curation.
