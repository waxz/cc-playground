# g4-classical-control: the model-based root of legged locomotion

This group collects the "traditional methods" root of the evolution tree: template models and stability criteria ([Raibert](../papers/raibert.md), [ZMP preview control](../papers/kajita-zmp-preview.md), [capture point](../papers/capture-point.md)), trajectory optimisation and model predictive control ([online iLQG-MPC](../papers/ilqr-online-to.md), [convex MPC on Cheetah 3](../papers/convex-mpc-cheetah.md), and the depth-3 additions [TAMOLS](../papers/tamols.md) and [perceptive NMPC](../papers/perceptive-nmpc.md)), the simulator built for model-based control ([MuJoCo](../papers/mujoco.md)), and two hybrids that put RL inside a model-based decomposition ([DeepGait](../papers/deepgait.md), [DTC](../papers/dtc.md)). The report has two aims: to state the classical paradigm precisely, and to establish which of its ideas survive, in altered form, inside learning pipelines and in particular inside [TGI](../papers/tgi.md).

## 1. The problem

A legged robot is an underactuated floating-base system whose only means of influencing its centre of mass is through intermittent, unilateral, friction-limited contacts. Its full equations of motion, $M(q)\ddot q + h(q,\dot q) = S^\top\tau + J_c^\top\lambda$, are high-dimensional, hybrid (the active contact set switches) and non-smooth at impacts (cookbook: [rigid-body dynamics and contact](../math-cookbook.md#m-rigid-contact)). On rough terrain a further discrete choice is added: which surface each foot contacts. The classical answer to this complexity is *reduction*: find a low-dimensional model whose dynamics capture what matters for balance, impose a stability condition on it, plan in the reduced space over a horizon, and map the plan back to joint torques with a whole-body controller. Each paper in the group can be located by which reduction it chooses and which decision it leaves to heuristics.

## 2. Chronological development

**Templates and dynamic balance (1980s–2006).** [Raibert's](../papers/raibert.md) hopping machines established that balance can be maintained dynamically, through a sequence of flight and stance phases, and that foot placement is the principal actuator for it. His decomposition into hopping height, speed (via foot placement $x_f = \dot x T_s/2 + k_{\dot x}(\dot x - \dot x_d)$) and attitude rests on a spring-mass intuition later formalised as the SLIP template. For walking humanoids, the linear inverted pendulum (LIPM) became the template: with constant CoM height $z_c$, the ZMP satisfies $p = x - (z_c/g)\ddot x$ (cookbook: [LIPM, ZMP and capture point](../math-cookbook.md#m-lipm-zmp)). [Kajita et al. (2003)](../papers/kajita-zmp-preview.md) turned this relation into a linear-quadratic preview controller that computes the CoM motion realising a desired ZMP trajectory from planned footsteps, using a window of future references. [Pratt et al. (2006)](../papers/capture-point.md) read the same LIPM as an unstable system and isolated its divergent component, the capture point $x_{cp} = x + \dot x/\omega$, the point where stepping brings the robot to rest. This converted the foot-placement heuristic into a derivation and redefined balance as a property of reachable futures.

**Optimal control with full models (2012).** [Tassa, Erez and Todorov](../papers/ilqr-online-to.md) ran iLQG inside a receding-horizon loop on a simulated humanoid, synthesising behaviours such as getting up from simple costs, with a regularised backward pass (cookbook: [LQR, Riccati, iLQR/DDP](../math-cookbook.md#m-lqr-ddp); [MPC](../math-cookbook.md#m-mpc)). [MuJoCo](../papers/mujoco.md) was built by the same authors to make this feasible: generalised coordinates, a convex soft-contact model that makes forward and inverse dynamics smooth and unique, and fast finite differences. This is the moment when "differentiate the model to improve the controller" became a working method, but only in simulation, where the model is exact.

**Simplified-model MPC on hardware (2018).** [Di Carlo et al.](../papers/convex-mpc-cheetah.md) reduced the quadruped to a single rigid body, linearised orientation about yaw and fixed footholds from a Raibert-type rule, obtaining a convex QP over ground reaction forces with friction-pyramid constraints, solved online on Cheetah 3. Convex MPC is robust on flat ground but blind: footholds and gait are inputs.

**Perceptive trajectory optimisation (2020–2023).** At ETH the terrain decision was brought inside the optimiser. Modular pipelines searched foothold-score maps around nominal footholds; [TAMOLS](../papers/tamols.md) instead jointly optimised footholds and base splines over a bilinearly interpolated elevation map in about 6 ms, with a contact-force-free stability model (the gravito-inertial acceleration cone, which reduces to the ZMP criterion on flat ground), gradient-based edge avoidance on smoothed height layers, and graduated optimisation over a "virtual floor". [Perceptive NMPC](../papers/perceptive-nmpc.md) chose the other numerical route: a kinodynamic model, terrain exposed as convex foothold polygons and a signed distance field, and multiple-shooting SQP at 100 Hz. It also reported that single-shooting iLQR diverged on stepping stones where the robot leaps or nears a singularity.

**Hybrids (2019–2024).** In parallel, learned controllers ([Hwangbo et al.](../papers/hwangbo-anymal.md), [Lee et al.](../papers/lee-challenging-terrain.md), [Miki et al.](../papers/miki-wild.md); [g5](g5-learned-locomotion.md)) overtook model-based controllers in robustness, but struggled where valid footholds are rare. [DeepGait](../papers/deepgait.md) placed RL inside a model-based decomposition: a learned planner proposes support phases from a heightmap, trained in an MDP whose "dynamics" are an LP feasibility check on centroidal dynamics instead of physics, and a learned controller tracks footholds. [DTC](../papers/dtc.md) inverted the roles: TAMOLS plans online during RL training, and a PPO policy learns to track its footholds, with a logarithmic foothold reward, a consistency reward, and heavy map-noise randomisation. On hardware DTC combined TO-level foothold accuracy (2.3 cm on flat ground) with RL-level robustness to slip and map drift.

## 4. Shared mathematics

The group's mathematics is that of [LIPM/ZMP/capture point](../math-cookbook.md#m-lipm-zmp), [LQR and iLQR/DDP](../math-cookbook.md#m-lqr-ddp), [receding-horizon control](../math-cookbook.md#m-mpc), [rigid-body dynamics, friction cones and complementarity](../math-cookbook.md#m-rigid-contact), [heightmaps and differentiable lookups](../math-cookbook.md#m-terrain-lookup) and, for the hybrids, [PD action spaces](../math-cookbook.md#m-pd-actuator), [tracking rewards](../math-cookbook.md#m-tracking-reward), [domain randomisation](../math-cookbook.md#m-domain-rand) and [terrain curricula](../math-cookbook.md#m-curriculum). Three relations are worth stating explicitly because they reappear in modern pipelines. First, the iLQR backward pass propagates $V_x = \ell_x + f_x^\top V'_x$; reverse-mode differentiation of a rollout return propagates exactly the same adjoint at first order ([BPTT through dynamics](../math-cookbook.md#m-bptt)). Second, a finite-horizon MPC cost with a terminal cost $\ell_f$ is formally the [short-horizon objective with a terminal critic](../math-cookbook.md#m-short-horizon), with the critic learned instead of designed. Third, the ZMP-in-support-polygon and friction-cone conditions are convex constraints on contact wrenches, which can be imposed on any predictor of contact forces, not only on an optimiser's decision variables.

## 5. What survives inside learning pipelines

**MPC ↔ imagined rollouts through a model.** Model predictive control predicts the consequences of candidate actions over a horizon with a model and optimises them. Model-based RL ([g2](g2-world-models.md)) keeps the prediction and changes the model and the optimiser: [PETS](../papers/pets.md) and [TD-MPC](../papers/td-mpc.md) plan online over learned models, while [Dreamer](../papers/dreamer.md), [SuperMimic](../papers/supermimic.md) and TGI *amortise* the optimisation into a policy trained on imagined rollouts. TGI's 32-step (0.64 s) horizon from simulator states is the receding horizon of MPC, re-solved not online but by stochastic gradient descent over thousands of start states. What is lost is the per-state optimality and constraint satisfaction of online MPC; what is gained is a controller that runs in one network evaluation and does not depend on an accurate hand-built model.

**DDP/iLQR backward pass ↔ backpropagating returns through dynamics.** This is the deepest continuity, and it links this group to [g3](g3-analytic-gradients.md). [SVG](../papers/svg.md), [SHAC](../papers/shac.md) and TGI compute $\nabla_\theta J$ by reverse-mode differentiation through the dynamics, which is a first-order, policy-parameter version of the iLQR backward pass. The classical literature had already met the main pathology. [Perceptive NMPC](../papers/perceptive-nmpc.md) found that iLQR's full nonlinear rollouts diverge on stepping stones, and abandoned single shooting for multiple shooting; [Tassa et al.](../papers/ilqr-online-to.md) needed regularisation to keep the backward pass well posed. Learning pipelines answer the same instability differently: SHAC truncates the horizon and bootstraps with a critic; TGI additionally re-anchors rollouts at simulator states and *cuts* the backpropagated return at imagined touchdowns on poorly supported terrain, letting the critic take over exactly where contact makes the Jacobians untrustworthy (cookbook: [gradients through contact](../math-cookbook.md#m-contact-gradients)). The difference that matters is that iLQR differentiates the true model, whereas TGI differentiates a learned one, so the cut protects against both contact stiffness and model error.

**Physical structure ↔ structured world models.** The classical constraints did not disappear; in TGI they migrated from the planner into the model. TGI's contact-impulse world model predicts $s_{t+1} = s_t + f(x) + B(x)J(s_t)^\top W$ with the contact Jacobian computed exactly from MuJoCo-derived kinematics, gates contact force by the gap to the terrain, keeps tangential force inside a friction cone, and for the G1 bounds sole moments so that the centre of pressure stays inside the sole. These are the friction-cone constraints of [convex MPC](../papers/convex-mpc-cheetah.md) and the per-foot ZMP condition of [Kajita et al.](../papers/kajita-zmp-preview.md), imposed as inductive bias on a learned predictor.

**Foothold planning ↔ foothold prediction.** From [Raibert](../papers/raibert.md) through [capture point](../papers/capture-point.md) to TAMOLS, the classical tradition treats the next foothold as the key decision. [DeepGait](../papers/deepgait.md) learned it; [DTC](../papers/dtc.md) took it from TO and made it the policy's tracking target and observation. [Watch Your Step](../papers/watch-your-step.md) keeps the foothold as the interface but changes its role from *target* to *attention*: a learned foothold prediction model, fed the same arguments as the Raibert heuristic (proprioception and command), decides where the policy looks, and WYS uses a Raibert-like velocity-shifted crop as a baseline. TGI inherits this, so the classical foothold planner survives as a learned perceptual gate rather than a command. TGI's differentiable terrain terms are also traceable to TAMOLS: both bilinearly query a global height map and both build smoothed auxiliary fields (TAMOLS's virtual floor and gradient-norm edge cost; TGI's Gaussian-blurred support field and edge field) because the raw map yields no useful gradient beyond the current tread.

**Hybrid TO + RL.** DeepGait and DTC show both the promise and the price of combining paradigms through a fixed interface. DeepGait's planner was trained against a cheap feasibility surrogate rather than physics, an early instance of training in a model rather than a simulator, but its conservative centroidal LP limited gaits and it never left simulation. DTC achieved hardware results on sparse terrain that neither paradigm reached alone, yet it reports no gain in sample efficiency and inherits the planner's limits (fixed trot, 0.40 m climb with TAMOLS). In both, the model-based component provides *information* (feasibility labels, targets) while the policy is still trained by score-function gradients ([g1](g1-rl-foundations.md)). TGI is a different kind of hybrid: no planner, but a model whose gradients reach the policy directly.

## 6. Tensions and open problems

The classical paradigm generalises across terrain geometry because it plans against the map, and it is accurate because it reasons with explicit models; it is brittle because every assumption (flat contacts, perfect state estimation, known contact state, fixed gait) is a failure mode on hardware, as the TAMOLS and DTC experiments with hidden gaps and map drift show. Learned controllers invert this profile. The hybrids resolve the tension only partially, at large training cost. Three questions remain open and bear directly on TGI. Can gradients through a model replace a planner on sparse terrain without the planner's assumptions? TGI's design suggests yes, but only with engineered smooth terrain fields, which reintroduces hand design in the form of TAMOLS-like map processing. How should contact events be handled in gradient-based optimisation? Classical practice uses multiple shooting, smoothing and graduated optimisation; TGI uses truncation and critic bootstrapping, and neither is a principled answer. And how much physical structure should a learned model carry? TGI's choice of exact Jacobians and constraint-respecting contact heads is closer to the classical tradition than to the unstructured latent models of [Dreamer](../papers/dreamer-v3.md).

## 7. How the group feeds the others and TGI

This group supplies the templates and stability concepts used to interpret learned gaits, the optimal-control formalism behind [g2](g2-world-models.md) planning and [g3](g3-analytic-gradients.md) pathwise gradients, the simulator infrastructure ([MuJoCo](../papers/mujoco.md)) on which [g1](g1-rl-foundations.md) was benchmarked, the reference controllers against which [g5](g5-learned-locomotion.md) learned locomotion measured itself, and, through DeepGait and DTC, the foothold-centric view that [Watch Your Step](../papers/watch-your-step.md) and [TGI](../papers/tgi.md) adopt. [Song et al.](../papers/song-diffsim-quadruped.md) show the link most bluntly: backpropagation through a simplified rigid-body model could not discover foot placement and had to be given the Raibert heuristic, which is exactly the gap that foothold-gated perception and differentiable support fields are meant to close.
