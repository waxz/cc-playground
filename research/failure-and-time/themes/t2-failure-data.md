# T2: How can failure data be used better?

This report answers the study's second question for action-conditioned world models and for the policies trained or evaluated with them. It is organised by mechanism rather than by paper: (a) what data to compose, (b) which objective to train it with, (c) how to evaluate whether failures were learned, and (d) how the policy-learning literature already analysed in [the TGI study](../../tgi-lineage/README.md) handles the same problem. A mathematical background section precedes the four mechanisms; ranked recommendations close the report.

The corpus consists of the seed [WorldReforge](../papers/worldreforge.md) (its paper report is written separately; its failure-data findings are analysed here), eight failure-aware world-model papers ([FACT](../papers/fact.md), [CureWM](../papers/dream-of-success.md), [HF-WM](../papers/beyond-success-demos.md), [DreamTrue](../papers/dreamtrue.md), [WorldSync](../papers/worldsync.md), [ABot-PhysWorld](../papers/abot-physworld.md), [PhysisForcing](../papers/physisforcing.md), and the representation-alignment pair [REPA](../papers/repa.md) and [VideoREPA](../papers/videorepa.md)), four evaluation papers ([AHA](../papers/aha.md), [WorldFailBench](../papers/worldfailbench.md), [WorldOracle](../papers/worldoracle.md), [Physion](../papers/physion.md)), two policy papers from WorldReforge's reference list ([PerturBot](../papers/perturbot.md), [Perfect-Demo](../papers/perfect-demo.md)) and two corpus additions ([HER](../papers/her.md), [CQL](../papers/cql.md)). With the exceptions of REPA, VideoREPA, Physion, AHA, HER and CQL, every paper is a 2026 preprint; almost none reports variance over training seeds, and WorldReforge states a single training seed (62101). The evidence is therefore best read as a set of consistent directions with uncertain magnitudes.

## 1. The problem: success-dominated data do not identify action effects

WorldReforge's diagnosis frames the problem. On its simulated evaluation, five pretrained action-conditioned models from three families (Cosmos3, DreamDojo, LingBot-VA) achieve object-motion error at most 10.4% below a static baseline that copies the first frame, and two are worse than it; on paired actions, pretrained Cosmos3 Nano's response-difference error (7.35×10⁻³) exceeds the static baseline's (6.06×10⁻³). FACT measures the same bias directly: a success-only world-action model predicts failure-rollout futures at 19.5 dB PSNR against 26.1 dB for success futures, and adding failures lifts the former to 25.9 dB without changing the latter. CureWM finds that 80% of verified failures receive success values above 0.5 after fine-tuning on official LIBERO data, and that 65 of 71 verified failures on a Franka receive success-like predictions.

CureWM's Proposition 1 explains why more successful data cannot fix this. If every observed action is a deterministic function of its context, $a_i=\pi(x_i)$, then for any action-conditioned predictor $f(x,a)$ the state-only predictor $h_f(x)=f(x,\pi(x))$ attains identical empirical risk. Nothing in the loss rewards sensitivity to the action, because no context ever shows two actions. PerturBot states the policy analogue in information-theoretic terms: when a decision never varies in successful demonstrations ($H(d)\approx 0$, e.g. "lift" always follows "close"), no relabelling or reweighting of those demonstrations can teach the missing branch. Both arguments concern *support*, not *weight*, and that distinction organises everything that follows.

## 2. Mathematical background

**Data mixtures and importance weighting.** Post-training samples segments from a mixture $q_S=\sum_k\pi_k D_k$ over sources (successful holding, failed contact with closed or open gripper, translation, rotation or closure-timing perturbations). Changing the proportions $\pi_k$ is equivalent to minimising an importance-weighted risk $\mathbb E_{u\sim q_S}[w(u)\ell(\theta;u)]$ with $w=q_{\text{target}}/q_S$, and is unbiased for the target distribution only where $q_S>0$. Reweighting therefore changes which existing examples dominate the gradient; it cannot add contexts with a second action. [Perfect-Demo](../papers/perfect-demo.md) makes this concrete for policies: upweighting critical frames by $w_t=\lambda\,\mathbb 1[t\in W]$ recovers part of the gain of deliberate demonstrations that contain recovery states, but not all of it.

**Paired counterfactuals.** CureWM's Proposition 2 shows that $m$ matched pairs $(x_j,a_j^+,1),(x_j,a_j^-,0)$ in $n$ examples force any state-only predictor to incur at least $\frac{2m}{n}\log 2$ additional log-loss, whereas an action-conditioned predictor can remove it. Pairs that share a context are thus the minimal data that make action sensitivity identifiable.

**Region-weighted reconstruction.** With flow matching, $z_\sigma=(1-\sigma)z_0+\sigma\epsilon$, $v^*=\epsilon-z_0$ and clean estimate $\hat z_0=z_\sigma-\sigma v_\theta$, WorldReforge's Interaction Alignment (IA) is

$$L_{IA}=\frac{\sum_{t,p}M_{t,p}\,\text{mean}_c(\hat z_{0,t,p,c}-z_{0,t,p,c})^2}{\sum_{t,p}M_{t,p}}=\sigma^2\big\langle(v_\theta-v^*)^2\big\rangle_M .$$

Under a uniform loss, the share of the gradient coming from the interaction region equals its area fraction $|M|/|\Omega|$, often a few percent of a three-view canvas; normalising by $\sum M$ gives the region a fixed share regardless of its size, and the $\sigma^2$ factor emphasises low-noise steps where fine detail is resolved. [PhysisForcing](../papers/physisforcing.md) applies a similar mask to representation losses rather than to reconstruction.

**Correspondence losses.** For a query point with feature $Q(p_i^0)$, similarities $s_i^t(x)$ to every location of frame $t$ are softmax-normalised, the predicted position is $\hat p_i^t=\sum_x\text{softmax}_x(s_i^t(x)/\tau)\,x$, and the loss is a masked squared or root-mean-squared distance to target tracks. WorldReforge's Surface Correspondence (SC) obtains targets by projecting simulator surface points, $p^{GT}_{it}=\pi(K_t(T^c_t)^{-1}T^o_tX_i)$; PhysisForcing uses CoTracker3 tracks on the training video.

**Representation alignment.** REPA maximises patchwise cosine similarity between a projection of an intermediate noisy-input feature and a frozen encoder's clean-image feature, $\mathcal L_{\text{REPA}}=-\mathbb E[\frac1N\sum_n\text{sim}(y^{*[n]},h_\phi(h_t^{[n]}))]$. VideoREPA's Token Relation Distillation instead matches token-similarity (Gram) matrices, $\mathcal L_{\text{TRD}}=\langle|G^{\text{sp}}_\theta-G^{\text{sp}}_{\text{enc}}|\rangle+\langle|G^{\text{tmp}}_\theta-G^{\text{tmp}}_{\text{enc}}|\rangle$, which constrains relations rather than coordinates and is reported to be safer when fine-tuning a pretrained generator.

**Paired-difference losses and metrics.** WorldSync's Intervention-Effect loss evaluates two action branches at the noise endpoint $t=1$ with shared noise, so the targets' noise cancels: $L_{IE}=\lVert(v^A_\theta-v^B_\theta)-(x_0^B-x_0^A)\rVert^2$. WorldReforge's response-difference error is the corresponding metric, $E_\Delta=\text{mean}[((\hat I^B-\hat I^A)-(I^B-I^A))^2]$.

**Outcome-masked multi-task losses.** FACT trains action, progress value and future video with per-modality flow-matching losses and drops the action term on failures: $L=\mathbb 1[\text{success}]\,w_aL_a+w_vL_v+w_IL_I$, with progress target $\text{clip}(p_{t+H}-\lambda_{\text{fail}}\mathbb 1_{\text{fail}},0,1)$.

**Preference and reward objectives.** Diffusion-DPO ([ABot-PhysWorld](../papers/abot-physworld.md)) replaces log-likelihoods by denoising errors $L(z)=\lVert\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,c)-\epsilon\rVert^2$: $\mathcal L=-\log\sigma(-\beta[(L_\theta(z_w)-L_\theta(z_l))-(L_{\text{ref}}(z_w)-L_{\text{ref}}(z_l))])$. Group-relative methods ([HF-WM](../papers/beyond-success-demos.md)'s Flow-GRPO, [DreamTrue](../papers/dreamtrue.md)'s GDPO with DiffusionNFT) sample $K$ futures per condition and use the within-group standardised reward $A_k=(r_k-\mu_r)/(\sigma_r+\delta)$ as advantage. Both optimise a judge's score, not agreement with a recorded outcome.

**Pessimism for policy learning.** MOPO's penalised reward $\tilde r=r-\lambda u(s,a)$ with an uncertainty estimate $u$ yields a lower bound on true return ([uncertainty penalties](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-ensembles)); CQL adds $\alpha(\mathbb E_{a\sim\mu}Q-\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_\beta}Q)$ to the Bellman error so that the policy's value lower-bounds its true value. HER relabels a transition's goal with an achieved state $g'$ and recomputes $r_{g'}$, valid because the learner is off-policy. TGI's event-gated return is a stopped return bootstrapped by a critic ([stopping-time truncation](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-stopping-time-truncation)).

## 3. Mechanism (a): data composition

**Generating failures by perturbing successes.** All methods that obtain ground-truth failure outcomes do so by perturbing successful references. [AHA](../papers/aha.md)'s FailGen perturbs RLBench keyframes along a seven-mode taxonomy (no grasp, slip, translation, rotation, missing rotation, wrong order, wrong object). WorldReforge perturbs cuRobo-planned reference grasps in translation (51,964 trajectories), rotation (5,720) and gripper closure timing (4,356), records each push, tilt, slip or failed pickup with object pose, contact and surface points, and obtains 13,577 failure-related trajectories out of 62,125 simulated ones; its main post-training mixture has about 45% failure-related segments. CureWM perturbs after an intervention time while keeping pre-intervention actions, across a severity grid, executes every candidate, and keeps families whose failure rate rises with severity. The common design has three properties: shared prefixes (paired contexts, by Proposition 2), controlled offsets and timings (so coverage can be designed), and execution-verified labels (so outcomes are physics, not guesses).

Two findings indicate why paired, perturbation-based failures are more useful than failures harvested from policy rollouts. CureWM's matched comparison (140 failures each, identical successful replays and budget) gives a success–failure value gap of 0.124 for counterfactual failures against 0.014 for fresh policy failures. And WorldSync shows across six architectures that expanding training to off-expert actions (cross-state replay, local perturbation, policy rollouts, feasible-space samples) lowers gated error and raises visual validity for every model; in its ablation, expanded coverage alone accounts for a 25% reduction in gated error, more than its two new losses together.

**Failed contact and successful holding teach complementary physics.** WorldReforge's composition study (Table 5; 1,536 segments, 600 steps) is the corpus' only controlled experiment on which failures matter. Starting from 100% successful holding, replacing half the data by contracted-gripper failed contact lowers object ADE on contracted-contact clips from 12.78 to 10.48 px (−18.0%) and also lowers it by 13.0% on open-gripper contact; yet success-only training remains best on holding clips. The interpretation offered, that failed contact teaches how objects respond to forces while holding teaches how a grasped state persists, is plausible: failure data populate the part of state space where the object moves relative to the gripper, success data the part where it does not. A model trained on either alone extrapolates badly to the other.

**Contact-state diversity at fixed failure share.** At an identical 50% failure share, mixing contracted and open contact (25% each) lowers object ADE by 12.2% and 9.9% in the two contact conditions relative to success-only training and gives the best overall gripper ADE (2.64 px) and DROID PSNR. The execution-type study (Table 4; 768 segments, 400 steps) points the same way: translation plus rotation gives the lowest overall object ADE (7.25 px), rotation plus closure is best on closure clips, and including closure timing gives the best gripper ADE (2.68 px).

These results should be weighed with care. The composition runs are very small (768–1,536 segments, 400–600 steps), use a single seed, and the Table 4 differences in overall object ADE span 7.25–7.38 px, a fraction of a pixel. The Table 5 differences (12.78 vs 10.48 px) are larger and more credible. Composition effects are also backbone-dependent: post-training on Reforge *worsened* DreamDojo's object ADE from 9.57 to 11.81 px while improving its PSNR and gripper error, and LingBot-VA's visual pass rate on real failure videos fell from 83% to 58%. Failure-enriched data are not uniformly beneficial; they interact with the action interface and pretraining of each model.

**Real failures.** [WorldOracle](../papers/worldoracle.md) shows that real training data with about 20% failed segments and evaluation data with about 40% are feasible to collect by teleoperation; FACT and CureWM collect tens to hundreds of real failures. Simulated failures transfer partially: WorldReforge gains 1.04 dB PSNR on DROID and 0.68 dB on its 300 real failure trajectories from simulation-only post-training, but these are reconstruction metrics, not outcome correctness.

## 4. Mechanism (b): objectives

**Keeping the generative loss.** CureWM keeps each model's native objective and changes only the data; WorldReforge's "Reforge" condition does the same and already obtains most of its gain (object ADE −24.9% on Cosmos3 Nano, −25.8% on LingBot-VA). This is the strongest single lever in the corpus, and it is cheap. Its limitation is that a uniform reconstruction loss weights interaction regions by their area and receives no signal that two actions from one context should diverge.

**Outcome, progress and reward supervision.** FACT supervises an action-conditioned progress value alongside video, and masks imitation on failures; keeping the imitation loss on failures drops real-world success from 82% to 63%, and value-based candidate scoring helps only after training on failures (79% without, 92% with). CureWM assigns value 0 to failed replays and shows the trap of outcome labels: labelling all perturbed replays as failures lowers optimism but collapses success values (0.147). Outcome signals say *whether* an interaction failed, not *how* the object moved, which is the gap WorldReforge identifies.

**Counterfactual and reward-guided training without ground truth.** HF-WM and DreamTrue generate futures under perturbed actions and optimise judge rewards (a pairwise action-alignment scorer and a VLM physics checklist; a reward model trained on 30.4K human defect labels). DreamTrue's defect reduction is large (interaction defects 48.1% to 6.3%) but is measured against its own Stage I, which had more defects than the GE-Sim 2.0 baseline (7.5%); HF-WM's RL stage adds about 0.3 dB. Neither reward can distinguish a correct failure outcome from a plausible wrong one, and HF-WM reports reward hacking towards static scenes when the plausibility reward is used alone. Preference optimisation in ABot-PhysWorld (VLM-ranked best/worst generations, Diffusion-DPO) raises a VLM-judged physics score from 0.879 to 0.931 but is applied before action conditioning and evaluated by a related VLM protocol.

**Region-weighted and correspondence losses.** WorldReforge's IA and SC lower object ADE by a further 4.4% over Reforge on Cosmos3 Nano, and by 1.6% (ADE) on DreamDojo, with object FDE −5.0%; in a 6.4k-segment ablation joint IA+SC lowers object ADE 3.2% and gripper ADE 6.3% below the stronger single objective. Simulator-ground-truth correspondence beats CoTracker pseudo-labels by 3.96% (object) and 6.78% (gripper). PhysisForcing's ablation independently supports region focus: the same losses applied uniformly give 46.0 on R-Bench versus 47.5 when masked (fine-tuned baseline 44.8). These are consistent but small effects, all single-seed.

**Representation alignment.** REPA's large effects (17.5× faster convergence on ImageNet) concern semantic representations in training from scratch; VideoREPA's relational alignment raises VideoPhy physical commonsense from 32.3 to 40.1 for CogVideoX-5B; PhysisForcing restricts it to physics regions. None of these teachers has seen action-conditioned failure outcomes, so representation alignment improves coherence of whatever outcome the model predicts but cannot correct which outcome it predicts.

**Paired-action losses.** WorldSync's IE loss is the only objective that directly trains the difference between two actions' futures. In combination with its action-forcing expert it reduces gated and raw error by 9.6% and 21.7% relative to IE alone on ten tasks, but its overall margin over the best baseline is 0.0234 vs 0.0237 m. Because it is applied at $t=1$ only, it supervises the coarse conditional mean of the action effect, not contact detail.

## 5. Mechanism (c): evaluation

**Action sensitivity.** Paired actions from a shared context are the evaluation counterpart of Proposition 2. WorldReforge's response-difference error falls by 43.3% relative to the pretrained model but only 3.9% relative to plain Reforge post-training, showing that data, not objectives, produced most of the sensitivity gain. WorldSync's WorldEcho shows that expert-only evaluation understates off-expert error by 0.022–0.048 m across six models. [PerturBot](../papers/perturbot.md)'s GroundFscore is the policy-side analogue: it requires both invariance to null edits and the correct change under causal edits, so that ignoring an input scores zero. CureWM makes the same point for outcome predictors: report optimism together with discrimination (gap, AUROC) and false alarms, since uniform pessimism lowers optimism without repairing anything.

**Failure benchmarks.** WorldFailBench evaluates closed-loop failure types, stages and success-to-failure boundaries. Agreement in success rate (best Pearson 0.597) does not carry over to stages (SPC −0.12 to 0.10, against 0.90 between reference reruns), and 95.2% of model–pair comparisons underestimate the size of reference success drops under small scene changes. WorldOracle compares open-loop replays with real executions containing about 40% failed segments and finds that the model with the best arm alignment (73.5) has physical-state correctness of 60.3; following the action is not predicting the outcome. AHA's taxonomy and Physion's binary contact-prediction task supply the older template: score discrete physical outcomes against a human or reference baseline.

**Overrating of policies.** Three measurements quantify the risk that success-biased world models make policies look better than they are: CureWM's 80% optimism on verified failures; DreamTrue's regression of world-model on real success rates, whose intercept for Ctrl-World is +8.7 points, concentrated in the low-success regime where failed grasps are hallucinated as completions (failure-aware post-training reduces MAE from 12.9 to 7.1 points); and WorldFailBench's compressed failure boundaries, which make policies appear robust to scene changes that break them in reality. This is the world-model version of model exploitation in model-based RL, discussed next.

## 6. Mechanism (d): the policy-learning side

The model-based RL work analysed in the TGI study treats failures and off-distribution states from the policy's point of view: the danger is not that the model is wrong on average but that the policy optimiser seeks out the states where it is wrong.

**Model exploitation and uncertainty penalties.** [PETS](../../tgi-lineage/papers/pets.md) separates aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty with probabilistic ensembles; [ME-TRPO](../../tgi-lineage/papers/me-trpo.md) stops policy improvement when fewer than 70% of ensemble members agree that it improves; [MBPO](../../tgi-lineage/papers/mbpo.md) bounds the return gap by model error and policy shift and limits branched rollouts to a few steps ([branched rollouts](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-dyna-mbpo)); [MOPO](../../tgi-lineage/papers/mopo.md) and [RWM-O](../../tgi-lineage/papers/rwm-o.md) subtract $\lambda u$ from the reward. RWM-O's penalty sweep shows both failure modes: small $\lambda$ yields policies that exploit the model and fall, large $\lambda$ yields policies that stand still. Its real-robot result is directly relevant: adding 200K real transitions to 800K simulated ones helped, but more real data hurt (0.91 to 0.59 normalised performance), attributed to real data lacking risky, informative transitions. Safe, success-dominated data starve a model of exactly the failure transitions it needs to penalise correctly. [CQL](../papers/cql.md) is the model-free counterpart: observed failures with honest rewards anchor values, while unobserved actions are pushed down indiscriminately.

**Training on the model's own rollouts.** [RWM](../../tgi-lineage/papers/rwm.md) fits the dynamics model autoregressively on its own predictions, cutting relative autoregressive error from 3.99 (teacher forcing) to 0.47, so that the model is trained on the error distribution it will encounter ([multi-step model loss](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-model-learning)). This is the dynamics-model analogue of exposing a video world model to off-nominal states: in both cases, the training distribution must include the states the model will be queried in, not only the ones an expert visits.

**On-policy data refresh.** [SuperMimic](../../tgi-lineage/papers/supermimic.md) and [TGI](../../tgi-lineage/papers/tgi.md) continuously collect simulator transitions with the current policy into a replay buffer and refit the model, so the policy's own failures (falls, missteps) enter the model's training set as they arise. This automatic failure harvesting is unavailable to the video world models in this corpus, which are post-trained once on fixed data; WorldSync's iterative VLAW loop and FACT's rollout-failure collection are partial exceptions. CureWM's evidence that on-policy failures teach discrimination less efficiently than paired counterfactuals suggests the two should be combined, not substituted.

**Failure terminations and gradient cuts.** TGI uses soft terminations computed analytically on the true terrain and truncates the differentiable return at the first poorly supported touchdown, bootstrapping with the critic ([stopping-time truncation](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-stopping-time-truncation)). The rationale is that the model's derivatives after a near-failure event are evaluated on the wrong side of a discontinuity and are biased; cutting there trades model bias for critic bias. The general lesson for failure data is that near failure events a learned model is least reliable, so policy optimisation should either stop trusting it there (gradient cut, termination, short horizon) or be penalised for visiting such states.

**Delta-action correction.** [ASAP](../../tgi-lineage/papers/asap.md) uses real trajectories, including ones where the simulated policy fails on hardware, to learn a residual action $\pi^\Delta(s,a)$ such that the simulator reproduces real transitions ([delta-action models](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-residual-dynamics)), then fine-tunes the policy in the corrected simulator. It uses failed real executions as model-correction data rather than as policy-training data, the same separation of roles that FACT enforces with its loss mask.

**Learning from failures in policy data.** [HER](../papers/her.md) relabels failed attempts with achieved goals; [PerturBot](../papers/perturbot.md) relabels failed and random segments with language describing what they did, which cuts the empty-grasp-then-lift error from 82% to 44% on scripted probes and, combined with view and caption interventions, doubles real-robot success (42% to 84%); [Perfect-Demo](../papers/perfect-demo.md) shows that coverage of recovery states beats reweighting of nominal frames.

## 8. Recommendations

### World-model training

1. **Collect or simulate executed failures as paired counterfactuals from shared prefixes, and keep the native generative loss as the base.** Evidence: Proposition 1–2 (CureWM); 25% object-ADE reductions from data alone (WorldReforge); failure-future PSNR from 19.5 to 25.9 dB (FACT); counterfactual over on-policy failures in a matched comparison (CureWM); coverage expansion helps all six architectures (WorldSync). Limitations: sim-to-real transfer is shown only for reconstruction metrics; one backbone (DreamDojo) degraded in object motion; the paired-versus-unpaired comparison is a single experiment with confounds acknowledged by its authors.

2. **Design the failure mixture for contact-state diversity and keep successful holding in it, at roughly half failures.** Evidence: complementary effects of failed contact and holding, and benefits of mixing contracted and open contact at a fixed share (WorldReforge Tab. 5); FACT and WorldReforge both settle at about 45% failure data. Limitations: single seed, 600-step runs, one simulator and gripper; the optimal share is not established, and Table 4's execution-type differences are sub-pixel.

3. **When failures are in the data, separate what is imitated from what is predicted.** For joint world-action models, mask the action loss on failures and supervise video and outcome (FACT). Evidence: 63% vs 89% real success with and without masking; scoring with a value head helps only after failure training. Limitations: 20 trials per cell, no seeds; the uniform progress target is crude.

4. **Add region-focused reconstruction and correspondence losses using physical ground truth where available.** Evidence: IA+SC give consistent 3–5% gains beyond data across three model families; physical labels beat tracker pseudo-labels by about 4–7%; region masking beats uniform application (PhysisForcing). Limitations: small, single-seed effects; require masks and point correspondences, which real data provide only through trackers.

5. **Use judge-based rewards or preferences only as a complement, and audit them for outcome correctness.** Evidence: large reductions in judge-detected defects (DreamTrue) but small reference-metric gains (HF-WM) and reward hacking towards static scenes. Limitations: no paper in the corpus verifies that RL-trained counterfactual outcomes match real executions.

6. **Treat representation alignment as a general regulariser, not a failure remedy.** Evidence: REPA and VideoREPA improve generic quality and physics plausibility; no teacher in these papers encodes action-conditioned failure outcomes.

7. **Evaluate on paired actions and on discrete outcomes, with reference repeatability.** Report response-difference error or paired value gaps, AUROC together with false alarms, physical-state correctness against real executions (WorldOracle), and failure boundaries (WorldFailBench). Limitations: these benchmarks have not yet been applied to failure-aware models; correlations over five or six policies are noisy.

### Policy training

1. **Never imitate failures; route them to the components that can use them.** Use failed executions to train the world model or value (FACT, ASAP's delta-action model, CQL's Bellman targets), or relabel them with what they achieved (HER, PerturBot). Evidence: imitation on failures drops success to 63% (FACT); relabelled segments repair motor shortcuts (82% to 44% error) in PerturBot. Limitation: relabelling adds behavioural support, not recovery; recovery requires recorded corrective continuations.

2. **Penalise model uncertainty in imagined returns and calibrate the penalty on held-out real failures.** Evidence: MOPO and RWM-O's penalty sweep; ME-TRPO's ensemble validation. Limitation: penalties trade exploitation for paralysis, and the right $\lambda$ was found by sweep; video world models in this corpus provide no calibrated epistemic uncertainty, which is an open gap.

3. **Refresh the model's data on-policy and include risky transitions.** Evidence: SuperMimic and TGI's replay loops; RWM-O's degradation with safe real data; WorldSync's iterative improvement. Limitation: on-policy failures alone teach discrimination less efficiently than paired counterfactuals (CureWM), so mix both.

4. **Stop trusting the model at failure events.** Truncate differentiable returns or terminate at predicted near-failures and bootstrap with a critic (TGI). Limitation: TGI's gate covers one event type and its benefit is unverified quantitatively; the critic is itself trained on model rollouts.

5. **Do not accept world-model policy evaluation without failure-sensitive checks.** Before using a world model to rank or approve policies, check its optimism on known failures and its boundary predictions against a reference (CureWM, DreamTrue, WorldFailBench). Limitation: such checks require some reference executions, so they reduce but do not eliminate real testing.
