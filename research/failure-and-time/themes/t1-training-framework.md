# T1 — How action-conditioned world models are trained, and where WorldReforge sits

This report answers the first study question: how action-conditioned (video) world models are trained and post-trained today, from the first pixel predictors to flow-matching transformers such as Cosmos3, and where the objectives of [WorldReforge](../papers/worldreforge.md) fit in that lineage. The short answer is that the field has converged on one recipe: a frozen perceptual video autoencoder; a large transformer pretrained on web video with a denoising objective, now usually rectified-flow velocity regression; an action interface grafted on during an action mid-training or post-training stage; and evaluation dominated by pixel metrics against a single recorded future. The variation that remains is in what data the model sees, how actions enter the network, how the model is made to roll out over long horizons, and how it is evaluated. WorldReforge changes the first and adds two auxiliary losses to the objective; the data change carries most of its measured effect.

## 1. The lineage in four steps

**Pixel predictors (2016–2018).** [Finn et al. 2016](../papers/finn-video-prediction.md) introduced action-conditioned video prediction for robots: a convolutional LSTM receives the next commanded action at each step and predicts how pixels of the previous frame move (DNA, CDNA, STP), trained with an $\ell_2$ loss on multi-step rollouts with scheduled sampling, on 57–59k autonomously collected pushing sequences. Because the loss is a squared error, the model predicts the conditional mean, and uncertain contact outcomes blur. [SV2P](../papers/sv2p.md) added a latent variable trained by an ELBO with a posterior that sees the future, which required a three-phase schedule (deterministic generator, then posterior without KL, then KL annealing) to avoid an ignored or collapsed latent. [Visual Foresight](../papers/visual-foresight.md) closed the loop: models of this family, trained on random-action data, drive CEM planning on a real robot with goal pixels, image registration or success classifiers as costs. Three features of this period recur later: actions enter frame by frame; data come from exploration and contain off-nominal contacts; and evaluation is ultimately task success under closed-loop control.

**Latent-space models (2018–2023).** The model-based RL line moved prediction into a learned latent state, analysed in the TGI study: [World Models](../../tgi-lineage/papers/world-models.md) trained a VAE and a mixture-density RNN separately; [PlaNet](../../tgi-lineage/papers/planet.md) learned a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) by an ELBO with stochastic and deterministic paths and planned in latent space; [DreamerV3](../../tgi-lineage/papers/dreamer-v3.md) scaled the RSSM with symlog targets and KL balancing and learned behaviour in imagination ([m-rssm-elbo](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-rssm-elbo)). These models are trained jointly with the task (reward heads, value learning) and judged by returns, not pixels; their decoders exist mainly as a training signal. Their limitation for open-world manipulation is generative capacity: reconstructions of novel scenes are poor, and they are trained per task. Their lasting contribution to video world models is conceptual: the separation of a compressed state from observation, the need for multi-step training to control compounding error ([m-model-learning](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-model-learning)), and the habit of evaluating a model by the decisions it supports.

**Diffusion and flow-matching generators (2020–2024).** [DDPM](../papers/ddpm.md) showed that regressing the noise added by a fixed Gaussian process, with a simplified re-weighting of the ELBO, gives state-of-the-art samples. [Video Diffusion Models](../papers/video-diffusion.md) extended this to video with a space–time factorised U-Net, joint image–video training and reconstruction-guided extension. [Latent diffusion](../papers/ldm.md) moved generation into the latent of a separately trained perceptual autoencoder, and [DiT](../papers/dit.md) replaced the U-Net with a transformer over latent patches conditioned through adaLN-Zero, with FID improving predictably with compute. [Flow matching](../papers/flow-matching.md) and [rectified flow](../papers/rectified-flow.md) reframed training as regressing the velocity of a probability path, with straight paths that sample in fewer steps, and [SD3](../papers/sd3.md) showed that the timestep density (logit-normal) and a resolution-dependent shift are what make rectified flow outperform tuned diffusion at scale. [Classifier-free guidance](../papers/cfg.md) became the standard sampling-time control of condition strength. [Diffusion Forcing](../papers/diffusion-forcing.md) gave each token its own noise level, combining causal next-token generation with diffusion and stabilising autoregressive video rollouts beyond the training horizon.

**Robot world models on video generators (2023–2026).** [UniSim](../papers/unisim.md) assembled a 5.6B video U-Net "action-in-video-out" simulator from heterogeneous datasets, unifying text and discretised controls and applying classifier-free guidance on actions. [Genie](../papers/genie.md) learned eight discrete latent actions from 30k hours of unlabelled gameplay (and a robot variant from RT-1 video). [RoboDreamer](../papers/robodreamer.md) composed language-conditioned video scores for compositional plans. [IRASim](../papers/irasim.md) showed, in a latent DiT, that injecting each action into its own frame through adaLN beats a single trajectory embedding. [Ctrl-World](../papers/ctrl-world.md) post-trained Stable Video Diffusion into a multi-view, pose-conditioned, memory-augmented simulator usable in closed loop with VLA policies. [DreamDojo](../papers/dreamdojo.md) pretrained on 44.7k hours of human video with continuous latent actions, post-trained on robots with relative, chunked actions and distilled to a 10.8 FPS causal student. [Cosmos3](../papers/cosmos3.md) unified language, image, video, audio and action in a mixture-of-transformers trained by rectified flow, with action mid-training on 61.3k hours. [LingBot-VA](../papers/lingbot-va.md) interleaved video and action tokens in a causal flow-matching model with a KV cache, trained on 16k hours of robot data and used primarily as a policy.

### 2.1 Data: scale and success bias

Scale has grown by three orders of magnitude, from 59k pushes to tens of thousands of hours, but the *outcome mix* has narrowed. Early data were collected by random exploration and therefore full of failed grasps and pushes. Modern corpora are teleoperated demonstrations (OXE, AgiBot, DROID), human activity video, and web video, all dominated by successful, intentional behaviour; Cosmos3's policy post-training even removes DROID's failure demonstrations, and LingBot-VA filters unsuccessful LIBERO demonstrations. The consequences are documented independently by several papers. IRASim states that an evaluator "must be able to simulate both successful and failed rollouts" and trains on policy rollouts for that reason. Ctrl-World calls DROID's ~19k failed trajectories "crucial" for controllability, yet reports that collisions, sliding and policy retries remain poorly modelled. DreamDojo finds its imagined success rates "often higher than their real counterparts". WorldReforge quantifies the effect: under failed contact, five pretrained backbones are at most 10.4% better than copying the first frame. Human video (DreamDojo, Cosmos3) adds diversity of objects and skills but not robot failure dynamics, and latent actions inferred from future frames can absorb consequences into the action ([Genie](../papers/genie.md) analysis). The [t2](t2-failure-data.md) report treats remedies in detail; for training, the lesson is that coverage of *action-dependent outcomes*, not hours of video, limits what a world model can predict off the demonstrated path.

### 2.2 Tokenisation

Since [LDM](../papers/ldm.md), generation happens in the latent of a separately trained, frozen autoencoder. Current robot models use video VAEs with 4× temporal and 8–16× spatial compression (WAN2.2 in DreamDojo, Cosmos3 and LingBot-VA, with patchification on top). Two consequences follow. First, contact-scale detail (fingertips, a small object's edge) occupies a handful of latent cells, so a uniformly averaged loss gives it little weight; this is the stated motivation of WorldReforge's area-normalised Interaction Alignment, whose masks are fractional overlaps on the latent grid. Second, the latent's temporal compression means four control steps map to one latent frame, which is why DreamDojo and LingBot-VA chunk four actions per latent frame. [Genie](../papers/genie.md)'s discrete tokenizer and masked-token dynamics are the main alternative, but none of the 2025–2026 robot models uses it. No paper reports its VAE's own reconstruction error on contact regions, although it bounds the PSNR gains that papers report.

### 2.3 Backbone

The backbone is a transformer over spatio-temporal latent patches, either factorised (IRASim, Ctrl-World) or full 3D attention, initialised from a pretrained video generator (SVD, Cosmos-Predict2.5, Wan2.2) or, in Cosmos3, from a vision-language model whose layers are duplicated into reasoner and generator towers. Mixture-of-transformers designs (Cosmos3, LingBot-VA) give modalities separate weights within shared attention, following [SD3](../papers/sd3.md)'s MM-DiT. Scale helps pretrained quality ([DiT](../papers/dit.md), [Genie](../papers/genie.md), DreamDojo 14B over 2B in human preference), but WorldReforge's pretrained Cosmos3 Edge, Nano and Super have object ADE of 8.40, 9.07 and 8.63 px under failed contact, so physical fidelity off the data distribution does not follow scale.

### 2.4 Action conditioning

Three families exist. *Global conditions* (UniSim's text-plus-discretised-control embedding with action CFG; RoboDreamer's language; IRASim's video-level adaLN) describe a whole clip. *Frame-aligned conditions* inject each action into its own frame: IRASim's frame-level adaLN, Ctrl-World's frame-wise pose cross-attention, DreamDojo's chunked injection into each latent frame, Cosmos3's action tokens placed between the video tokens they connect, and LingBot-VA's interleaved tokens. *Latent actions* infer the conditioning signal from video (Genie's eight codes, DreamDojo's 32-dimensional continuous code) so that unlabelled video can be used, and are replaced by real actions in post-training. The evidence consistently favours frame alignment: IRASim's Frame-Ada beats Video-Ada in metrics and human preference; Ctrl-World's ablation without frame-level conditioning loses control precision; DreamDojo's chunked injection gives the largest single gain in its ablation (GR-1 PSNR 16.52 → 17.63). Representation matters as well: DreamDojo's relative actions and Cosmos3's pose deltas are both re-baselined per step. When post-training introduces a new interface, the new layers are zero-initialised (DreamDojo, following [DiT](../papers/dit.md)'s adaLN-Zero) or re-initialised with scaled pretrained weights (LingBot-VA). WorldReforge's per-backbone adapter $h_b(s^r_t,s^r_{t+1})$ is an instance of the frame-aligned family, and its DreamDojo regression is the clearest warning that interface changes are not free.

### 2.5 Objective

The objective has moved from pixel regression (Finn) and ELBOs (SV2P, RSSMs) to denoising: $\epsilon$-prediction (UniSim, IRASim), $x_0$-prediction (Ctrl-World), $v$-prediction (VDM), and now rectified-flow velocity regression $v^*=\epsilon-z_0$ (DreamDojo, Cosmos3, LingBot-VA, WorldReforge). These are re-parameterisations of one family whose real degree of freedom is the weighting over noise levels, set by a weight $\omega(\sigma)$ and a timestep density (Section 3). Cosmos3 specifies logit-normal sampling for actions, "mode" sampling for video and a resolution-dependent shift; LingBot-VA a uniform-SNR sampler; WorldReforge only a "backbone-specific" $\omega(\sigma)$. Auxiliary losses are appearing on top of the denoising loss: DreamDojo's temporal-difference loss on predicted velocities, WorldReforge's region-weighted reconstruction (IA) and feature-level surface correspondence (SC), and the representation-alignment losses discussed in [t2](t2-failure-data.md) ([REPA](../papers/repa.md), [VideoREPA](../papers/videorepa.md)). In every case the reported gain of the auxiliary loss is small relative to data or interface changes (DreamDojo: +0.004 dB on GR-1 validation, +0.20 dB counterfactual; WorldReforge: −4.4% object ADE on Cosmos3 Nano, 0% on LingBot-VA).

### 2.6 Pretraining, mid-training and post-training

Training is now a curriculum. Pretraining on web-scale video teaches appearance and generic motion (Cosmos3 Nano: 31T tokens). An action mid-training stage teaches the causal interface between interventions and consequences across embodiments (Cosmos3: 8.4M episodes, 61.3k hours, two-thirds egocentric hand motion; DreamDojo's human-video stage plays the same role with latent actions; LingBot-VA's 1.4T-token robot pretraining). Post-training adapts to a target robot and action space with thousands of steps on small data (DreamDojo 25–30k steps on GR-1; LingBot-VA 3k steps on 50 demonstrations; WorldReforge 4,000 steps on 25.6k segments, about ten epochs). The best evidence for the middle stage is Cosmos3's controlled comparison: with equal post-training budgets, initialising from the mid-trained checkpoint raises DROID forward-dynamics PSNR from 23.24 to 25.52 dB (Nano) and policy success at 500 iterations from 0.0% to 24.6%. DreamDojo's latent-action pretraining similarly beats action-free pretraining. Post-training on narrow data carries a risk of forgetting, which Cosmos3 counters by mixing pretraining data into later stages and which WorldReforge does not address; its Reforge-Eval PSNR gain of ~3.9 dB is largely adaptation to the simulator's rendering, since the static baseline already scores 21.45 dB.

### 2.7 Autoregressive long-horizon generation and drift

Most models generate a fixed block (16–49 frames) and extend it by conditioning on their own outputs (VDM, UniSim, IRASim, Ctrl-World). Errors then compound: the model was trained on clean history but sees its own imperfect frames, the exposure-bias form of the compounding-error problem known from model-based RL ([m-model-learning](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-model-learning)). Four remedies appear. Noise on the history ([Diffusion Forcing](../papers/diffusion-forcing.md)'s per-token noise levels; LingBot-VA's noisy history augmentation) teaches the model to treat context as uncertain. Training on own rollouts (Self Forcing in DreamDojo's distillation; scheduled sampling already in Finn 2016) attacks exposure bias directly. Memory (Ctrl-World's pose-indexed retrieval of sparse past frames; LingBot-VA's KV cache) re-anchors predictions to earlier states. Grounding on real observations (LingBot-VA's forward-dynamics re-grounding) only applies in closed-loop control. Evidence is mostly qualitative or within single papers (DF's 1,000-frame Minecraft rollouts; Ctrl-World's memory ablation; DreamDojo's 1-minute student at PSNR 13.15 against 14.09 for the teacher). WorldReforge predicts 16 frames (1.07 s) from one frame and does not test whether its gains survive autoregressive rollout, the regime in which policy evaluation operates.

### 2.8 Evaluation

Three tiers can be distinguished. *Pixel and distribution metrics* (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD) dominate: Cosmos3 calls PSNR "a useful proxy" while acknowledging that a plausible rollout need not match the single recorded future. They reward appearance and penalise legitimate stochasticity, and they barely separate a static copy of the first frame from a pretrained world model (WorldReforge: 21.45 vs 21.99 dB). *Physical and trajectory metrics* measure what moved: Genie's $\Delta_t$PSNR (sensitivity to actions), Cosmos3's camera-pose errors recovered from generated video, WorldReforge's tracker-based object and gripper ADE/FDE, its paired-action response difference error (RDE), and the static baseline that exposes models that ignore actions. *Closed-loop and decision metrics* ask whether the model supports decisions: Visual Foresight's task success, UniSim's policies deployed after training in the model, IRASim's Pearson 0.99 policy-ranking correlation (four checkpoints), DreamDojo's $r=0.995$ (with optimistic absolute rates), Ctrl-World's instruction-following correlation and policy improvement. Only the third tier tests the use for which these models are built, and it is the tier that WorldReforge omits. Across the corpus, statistical practice is weak: single seeds, few evaluation points for correlations, human or VLM judges, and no confidence intervals.

## 3. Mathematical background

**Diffusion ELBO and score matching.** A Gaussian forward process $q(z_t\mid x_0)=\mathcal N(\alpha_tx_0,\sigma_t^2I)$ with decreasing signal-to-noise ratio $\mathrm{SNR}(t)=\alpha_t^2/\sigma_t^2$ defines a latent-variable model whose reverse process $p_\theta$ is trained by the ELBO $-\log p_\theta(x_0)\le\mathbb E_q\big[\sum_t D_{KL}(q(z_{t-1}\mid z_t,x_0)\Vert p_\theta(z_{t-1}\mid z_t))\big]+\text{const}$. With a noise-predicting network, each KL term is a weighted squared error, and the ELBO becomes $\mathbb E_{t,\epsilon}[w(t)\Vert\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t)\Vert^2]$ with a specific $w$; [DDPM](../papers/ddpm.md) set $w\equiv1$ ($L_{\text{simple}}$). Tweedie's formula links the optimal denoiser to the score of the noised marginal: $\mathbb E[x_0\mid z_t]=(z_t+\sigma_t^2\nabla\log p_t(z_t))/\alpha_t$, so $\epsilon^*(z_t)=-\sigma_t\nabla\log p_t(z_t)$, and denoising regression at all noise levels is denoising score matching. The cookbook entry [m-diffusion-guidance](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-diffusion-guidance) gives the discrete DDPM version.

**$\epsilon$, $x_0$ and $v$ prediction.** Given $z_t=\alpha_tx_0+\sigma_t\epsilon$, a network can output $\hat\epsilon$, $\hat x_0$ or $\hat v=\alpha_t\hat\epsilon-\sigma_t\hat x_0$; any one determines the others, e.g. $\hat x_0=(z_t-\sigma_t\hat\epsilon)/\alpha_t$. The losses differ by SNR-dependent factors: $\Vert\hat x_0-x_0\Vert^2=\mathrm{SNR}(t)^{-1}\Vert\hat\epsilon-\epsilon\Vert^2$, and for variance-preserving schedules ($\alpha_t^2+\sigma_t^2=1$) $\Vert\hat v-v\Vert^2=(1+\mathrm{SNR}(t))\Vert\hat x_0-x_0\Vert^2$. Choosing a parameterisation therefore chooses a weighting over noise levels; $\epsilon$-loss emphasises low noise, $x_0$-loss high noise, and $v$ sits between, which is why [VDM](../papers/video-diffusion.md) adopted $v$ to avoid colour drift at high resolution.

**Rectified flow and flow matching.** Rectified flow uses $\alpha=1-\sigma$, $\sigma_t=\sigma$: $z_\sigma=(1-\sigma)z_0+\sigma\epsilon$, $\sigma\in[0,1]$, a straight line from data to noise. Its velocity along $\sigma$ is $dz_\sigma/d\sigma=\epsilon-z_0=:v^*$. [Flow matching](../papers/flow-matching.md) proves that regressing this per-sample (conditional) velocity, $\mathcal L=\mathbb E\,\Vert v_\theta(z_\sigma,\sigma,c)-v^*\Vert^2$, has the same gradient as regressing the intractable marginal velocity field, so the minimiser is $v_\theta^*(z,\sigma)=\mathbb E[\epsilon-z_0\mid z_\sigma=z]$. Sampling integrates $dz/d\sigma=v_\theta$ from $\sigma=1$ (noise) to $0$ (data). In the general notation, $v^*$ relates to the other targets by $\hat z_0=z_\sigma-\sigma v_\theta$ and $\hat\epsilon=z_\sigma+(1-\sigma)v_\theta$, and the losses compare as $\Vert\hat z_0-z_0\Vert^2=\sigma^2\Vert v_\theta-v^*\Vert^2$ and $\Vert\hat\epsilon-\epsilon\Vert^2=(1-\sigma)^2\Vert v_\theta-v^*\Vert^2$. [SD3](../papers/sd3.md) writes all formulations as $\epsilon$-losses with weight $w_t$ (rectified flow corresponds to $w_t=t/(1-t)$) and shows that sampling $\sigma$ from a logit-normal density, $\pi(\sigma)\propto\frac{1}{\sigma(1-\sigma)}\exp(-\frac{(\mathrm{logit}\sigma-m)^2}{2s^2})$, is equivalent to a weight $\omega(\sigma)\propto\pi(\sigma)$ under uniform sampling. For larger inputs a shift $\sigma'=s\sigma/(1+(s-1)\sigma)$, $s\ge1$, moves mass toward high noise, because more tokens make a given noise level less destructive; Cosmos3's $s=1,3,5,10$ and WorldReforge's inference "timestep shift" refer to this map. [Rectified flow](../papers/rectified-flow.md)'s reflow retrains on the model's own noise–data couplings to straighten trajectories, reducing sampling steps.

**Classifier-free guidance.** Training with the condition dropped with probability $p_{\text{uncond}}$ yields both $v_\theta(z,c)$ and $v_\theta(z)$. Sampling with $\tilde v=(1+w)v_\theta(z,c)-wv_\theta(z)$ approximates the score of $p(z\mid c)p(c\mid z)^w$, sharpening toward outcomes strongly associated with $c$ ([CFG](../papers/cfg.md)). With $c$ an action, $w>0$ amplifies typical consequences of that action, which plausibly suppresses rare outcomes such as slips; conventions differ across codebases (WorldReforge reports scales 1, 0 and 5 for its three families).

**Per-token noise.** [Diffusion Forcing](../papers/diffusion-forcing.md) samples an independent noise level $k_t$ for each frame and trains $\mathbb E\sum_t\Vert\epsilon_t-\epsilon_\theta(z_{t-1},x_t^{k_t},k_t)\Vert^2$, a re-weighted ELBO over all noise-level sequences. Because the model has seen noisy history, inference can condition on generated frames at a small noise level, which tolerates their errors.

**The WorldReforge identity.** On the rectified-flow path, the true clean latent satisfies $z_\sigma-\sigma v^*=(1-\sigma)z_0+\sigma\epsilon-\sigma\epsilon+\sigma z_0=z_0$. The model's estimate is $\hat z_0=z_\sigma-\sigma v_\theta$. Subtracting,
$$\hat z_0-z_0=-\sigma(v_\theta-v^*)=\sigma(v^*-v_\theta).$$
Hence Interaction Alignment, $\mathcal L_{IA}=\sum M\,(\hat z_0-z_0)^2/\sum M$, equals $\sigma^2\langle(v_\theta-v^*)^2\rangle_M$: the base velocity loss restricted to the object mask, renormalised by mask area, and re-weighted by $\sigma^2$ toward high noise levels, where layout and motion are decided. At low noise the IA gradient vanishes. Its net effect on the effective objective is a change of $\omega(\sigma)$ inside the mask, by a factor $1+r(s)\lambda_R\sigma^2/(\bar M\,\omega(\sigma))$ where $\bar M$ is the mask's area fraction; for small objects ($\bar M\ll1$) the up-weighting is large.

## 4. Placing WorldReforge

**What it does.** WorldReforge post-trains existing flow-matching world models with $\mathcal L_\theta=\mathbb E[\mathcal L_{FM}+r(s)(\lambda_R\mathcal L^g_{IA}+\lambda_T\mathcal L^g_{SC})]$ on 25.6k segments drawn from 62k simulated grasp perturbations (about half failure-related), with the VAE frozen and the action adapter $h_b$ per backbone. $r(s)$ ramps the auxiliary terms in over the early training steps (its form is not given), $g$ masks valid noise ranges and visibility, and $\lambda_R,\lambda_T$ are backbone-specific (0.172 and 0.339 for Cosmos3 Nano). Surface Correspondence soft-matches intermediate DiT features (block 18 of Nano) of initial-frame surface points to future wrist-view features and penalises the distance of the expected match from the simulator projection $\pi(K_t(T^c_t)^{-1}T^o_tX_i)$.

**What is genuinely new.** In the lineage above, the data idea (systematically perturbing successful executions to generate labelled failures with exact physical state) is new for video world models, and differs from IRASim's on-policy rollouts and from outcome-level failure training ([FACT](../papers/fact.md), [World models dream of success](../papers/dream-of-success.md), [DreamTrue](../papers/dreamtrue.md)). SC is the most novel *objective*: it supervises the internal features of a video generator with ground-truth 3D surface tracks, a geometric counterpart of representation-alignment losses that align features with pretrained encoders ([REPA](../papers/repa.md), [VideoREPA](../papers/videorepa.md)). IA is less new than its presentation suggests: by the identity above it is a spatially masked, area-normalised, $\sigma^2$-weighted velocity loss, close to masked diffusion losses in subject-driven image generation and to the general practice of re-weighting the denoising loss.

**What the evidence shows.** Measured against its own tables, the main effect is the data. On Cosmos3 Nano, object ADE moves 9.07 → 6.81 px with the base objective on Reforge data and → 6.51 px with IA+SC, so 88% of the improvement precedes the new losses. On LingBot-VA, object ADE is 7.12 px with and without them. On DreamDojo, Reforge post-training raises object ADE from 9.57 to 11.81 px (WorldReforge 11.62), worse than the static baseline (9.38 px), and lowers DROID PSNR (17.27 → 17.03 dB); the paper reports only the 1.6% improvement over Reforge. The ablation on a 6.4k subset gives 7.02 → 6.84 (IA), 6.81 (SC), 6.59 (both), with SC from CoTracker pseudo-labels at 6.87; these are 0.2–0.4 px effects from one training seed and one inference seed, with six-digit loss weights whose tuning protocol is unstated. The paired-action RDE improvement (43.3%) is almost entirely relative to the pretrained model, which is *worse* than predicting no difference at all (7.35 vs 6.06 for the static predictor); relative to Reforge it is 3.9%. Real-world evidence is PSNR/SSIM only, with WorldReforge-over-Reforge margins of 0.10 dB (DROID) and 0.17 dB (Reforge-Real), and LingBot-VA's visual pass rate on Reforge-Real falls from 83% to 58%.

**What the metrics can and cannot show.** ADE/FDE track up to ten query points per entity with CoTracker3 on generated videos and compare them with simulator projections. Generated-video artefacts can corrupt tracks, and the static baseline bypasses the tracker, so model–static comparisons are not like for like. IA up-weights reconstruction inside the object boxes in which the metric's points lie, and SC trains features toward the same kind of point correspondence; the objectives are aligned with the metric, which inflates their apparent benefit relative to, say, physical plausibility judged by humans or downstream use. The "unseen object" split shares scenes, renderer and perturbation generator with training, and post-trained error on unseen objects (6.41 px) is below that on seen ones (6.57 px). There is no autoregressive rollout, no long horizon, no policy evaluation or ranking, and no test that the improved model changes a decision, so WorldReforge's contribution to the uses that motivate it (policy evaluation, planning) is not yet demonstrated.

## 5. Recommendations for building or post-training a robot world model

The recommendations are ranked by the strength of corpus evidence and expected effect size.

1. **Fix outcome coverage in the data before changing the objective.** Include perturbed executions, policy rollouts and failures alongside demonstrations, and keep successful holding in the mix. *Evidence:* WorldReforge's base-objective post-training gives −24.9% object ADE on Cosmos3 Nano and −25.8% on LingBot-VA, against −4.4% and 0% from its new losses; its composition study shows failed contact teaches object response while successful holding teaches persistence; IRASim and Ctrl-World needed failure or rollout data for evaluation and controllability; DreamDojo's evaluation is optimistic without it. *Limitations:* WorldReforge's failures are simulated grasp perturbations with one renderer; real-world gains are ≤1 dB PSNR and unmeasured for motion; on DreamDojo the same data hurt.

2. **Start from an action-mid-trained flow-matching backbone and keep the action interface close to what it was trained on.** Use relative end-effector or pose-delta actions, chunked per latent frame, with zero-initialised adapters and an explicit adaptation budget. *Evidence:* Cosmos3 MT-init vs PT-init (23.24 → 25.52 dB DROID PSNR; 0.0% → 24.6% policy success at 500 iterations); DreamDojo's relative and chunked actions give its largest ablation gains. *Limitations:* PSNR-based; the DreamDojo regression in WorldReforge suggests that switching interfaces (latent/joint to Cartesian) on 4,000 steps can fail, and no paper isolates this.

3. **Condition actions frame by frame and predict the views the policy uses, including the wrist camera.** *Evidence:* IRASim Frame-Ada over Video-Ada; Ctrl-World ablations of frame-level conditioning and joint multi-view prediction, and its observation that the wrist view reduces hallucinated contacts. *Limitations:* evidence from DROID-scale data and pixel metrics; benefits for failure prediction specifically are untested.

4. **Train for the rollout regime in which the model will be used.** If the model will be rolled out autoregressively for evaluation or planning, use noisy-history or per-token-noise training, self-rollout distillation and memory. *Evidence:* Diffusion Forcing's stable 1,000-frame rollouts; LingBot-VA's noisy history; DreamDojo's Self-Forcing student; Ctrl-World's memory ablation. *Limitations:* mostly single-paper, qualitative or PSNR evidence; WorldReforge-style gains are only shown at 16 frames.

5. **Treat auxiliary physical losses as cheap refinements that require proper ablation.** Region-weighted reconstruction (IA), temporal-difference losses (DreamDojo) and feature-level correspondence (SC) add no inference cost and can help when ground truth exists, but report their noise-level weighting explicitly (IA is a $\sigma^2$-weighted masked velocity loss), sweep weights on held-out data, and run several seeds. *Evidence:* WorldReforge ablation (−6.1% object ADE on a 6.4k subset, single seed); DreamDojo (+0.20 dB counterfactual PSNR). *Limitations:* small effects, metric-aligned objectives, zero effect on LingBot-VA.

6. **Evaluate in three tiers and include trivial baselines.** Report a static (copy-first-frame) baseline, action-sensitivity metrics (paired-action RDE, Genie's $\Delta_t$PSNR), object/gripper trajectory errors from ground truth where available, and closed-loop policy ranking or decision metrics, with seeds and confidence intervals. Report guidance scale and sampler settings, since CFG changes action-following. *Evidence:* the static baseline and RDE expose pretrained models in WorldReforge; IRASim, DreamDojo and Ctrl-World show closed-loop correlation is measurable. *Limitations:* tracker-based metrics inherit tracker errors on generated video; policy correlations in the corpus rest on four to seven points and can coexist with biased absolute success rates.

7. **Plan for real-time use separately.** Few-step distillation into a causal student (DreamDojo, 4 steps, 10.8 FPS) and partial denoising for action decoding (LingBot-VA) make closed-loop use feasible. *Evidence:* DreamDojo's teacher–student comparison. *Limitations:* the student loses about 0.9 dB PSNR over one minute, and its physical fidelity off-distribution is untested.

## Corpus additions

Four papers outside the seed list were added. [Finn et al. 2016](../papers/finn-video-prediction.md) (1605.07157) is the origin of robot action-conditioned video prediction and the paper WorldReforge cites as [12]; the lineage cannot be traced without it. [Classifier-free guidance](../papers/cfg.md) (2207.12598) is the sampling-time component of action conditioning in UniSim, RoboDreamer, Cosmos3 and LingBot-VA and a hidden variable in WorldReforge's cross-backbone comparison. [Rectified flow](../papers/rectified-flow.md) (2209.03003) defines the exact linear path on which WorldReforge's identity $\hat z_0-z_0=\sigma(v^*-v_\theta)$ holds and which Cosmos3 calls "rectified flow matching". [SD3](../papers/sd3.md) (2403.03206) is the source of logit-normal timestep sampling and the timestep shift used by Cosmos3 and in WorldReforge's inference settings, i.e. of the $\omega(\sigma)$ that WorldReforge leaves unspecified.
