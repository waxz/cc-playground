# t3 — How should time be embedded into a network?

This report answers the study's third question: how elapsed time and irregular sampling should be represented inside a neural network. It organises the corpus into five families, each defined by a governing equation, examines the seed paper [DAWIN](../papers/dawin.md) as a combination of two of them, relates the question to robot world models, and ends with ranked recommendations. Mathematics not covered by the TGI cookbook is collected in the "Mathematical background" section near the end.

## 1. The question and the corpus

A sequence $(x_i,\tau_i)$ with $\Delta t_i=\tau_i-\tau_{i-1}$ can carry time in three places: in the *input* (the network sees $\tau_i$ or $\Delta t_i$ as a number or an embedding), in the *state transition* (how the hidden state evolves over $\Delta t_i$), or in the *interaction* between elements (attention weights that depend on $\tau_m-\tau_n$). The families below differ in which place they use and in how much physical structure they impose.

## 2. Family (1): time as an input feature

The simplest embedding concatenates $\Delta t_i$ (or $\tau_i$, a mask, or the time since a variable was last observed) to the input, $h_i=\mathrm{RNNCell}(h_{i-1},[x_i;m_i;\Delta t_i])$; [GRU-D](../papers/gru-d.md) calls this GRU-simple and [Latent ODE](../papers/latent-ode.md) RNN-Δt. It requires no architectural change and can exploit informative missingness. Its failure modes follow from the absence of any prior on how state should change over a gap. First, the network must learn from data that a long gap implies more change; nothing forces continuity ($\Delta t\to0$ should leave the state unchanged) or consistency (two gaps of $\Delta$ should equal one of $2\Delta$). Second, raw scalars are poorly conditioned: [Time2Vec](../papers/time2vec.md) reports that LSTM+T fails on long N-TIDIGITS sequences, and its ablation attributes the failure of non-periodic maps partly to saturation (tanh, sigmoid) or explosion (ReLU) as $\tau$ grows. Third, the network's behaviour for $\Delta t$ outside the training range is arbitrary. Fourth, timing can leak in through derived features (velocities divided by $\Delta t$), so ablations that remove the explicit $\Delta t$ do not remove timing information, as [WFB](../papers/wfb.md) concedes; its capacity-matched experiment found shuffled intervals to give the best ADE (0.011418 vs 0.011787 with real intervals). The evidence is mixed rather than negative: on PhysioNet-2012 mortality RNN-Δt reaches 0.787 AUROC against 0.833 for ODE-RNN ([Latent ODE](../papers/latent-ode.md), Table 6), but on PhysioNet-2019 sepsis GRU-Δt (0.878) is level with Neural CDE (0.880) ([Neural CDE](../papers/neural-cde.md)), and [ContiFormer](../papers/contiformer.md)'s strongest competitor is TST, a discrete Transformer with time features.

## 3. Family (2): learned decay gates

[GRU-D](../papers/gru-d.md) computes $\gamma_t=\exp\{-\max(0,W_\gamma\delta_t+b_\gamma)\}$ and decays the hidden state, $h_{t-1}\leftarrow\gamma_{h,t}\odot h_{t-1}$, and missing inputs toward their mean. With $W_\gamma\delta+b$ linear in $\delta$, the hidden decay is the exact solution of $\dot h=-\lambda h$ over the gap, i.e. the real, diagonal, input-free special case of family (4). This is the right prior for homeostatic quantities and for "forgetting stale evidence," and it is what makes GRU-D robust in the clinical baselines of every later paper. Its limits are structural: the state can only relax monotonically toward zero, cannot oscillate or grow, and short gaps are ignored when $W\delta+b<0$. [Mamba](../papers/mamba.md)'s Theorem 1 shows that a GRU-style gate is a ZOH-discretised one-dimensional SSM with input-dependent step, which makes decay gates and selective SSMs points on one continuum: the gate is $1-e^{-\Delta}$ with $\Delta=\mathrm{softplus}(\cdot)$.

## 4. Family (3): continuous-time hidden states, CDEs and continuous attention

[Neural ODE](../papers/neural-ode.md) defines $\dot h=f_\theta(h,t)$ and integrates with an adaptive solver, with gradients from the adjoint system. Elapsed time becomes the integration length. Because an ODE trajectory is fixed by its initial condition, sequence models must inject data somehow. [Latent ODE](../papers/latent-ode.md)'s ODE-RNN interrupts the solve, $h_i'=\mathrm{ODESolve}(f,h_{i-1},(t_{i-1},t_i))$, $h_i=\mathrm{RNNCell}(h_i',x_i)$, and contains the decay RNN as the case $f=-\tau h$. [GRU-ODE-Bayes](../papers/gru-ode-bayes.md) uses the continuous GRU $\dot h=(1-z)\odot(g-h)$, which is bounded in $[-1,1]$ and Lipschitz, between Bayesian jumps, giving a learned Kalman-style filter. [LTC](../papers/ltc.md) uses $\dot x=-[1/\tau+f(x,I)]x+f(x,I)A$, whose input-dependent time constant anticipates selective SSMs, integrated by a fused semi-implicit step. [Neural CDE](../papers/neural-cde.md) replaces interruption by a driving path: $z_t=z_{t_0}+\int f_\theta(z_s)\,dX_s$ with $X$ a spline through $(x_i,t_i)$; since CDEs are invariant to reparameterising the path, time must be a channel of $X$. [ContiFormer](../papers/contiformer.md) moves the ODE into attention, with keys and values evolving by ODEs and attention scores given by time-averaged inner products, $\alpha_i(t)=\frac1{t-t_i}\int_{t_i}^tq(\tau)k_i(\tau)^\top d\tau$.

What the evidence shows is narrower than the motivation. Continuous-time state helps most in interpolation and with sparse or small data: latent ODE MSE on Hopper with 10 % of points is 0.360 vs 1.968 for GRU-D, and GRU-ODE-Bayes beats its discretised twin only with 1,000–2,000 patients (MSE 0.77 vs 0.87) and not on the full data (0.48 vs 0.46). For whole-sequence classification the authors of Latent ODE find that "modelling dynamics between time points does not make a difference" (ODE-RNN 0.833, latent ODE 0.829, GRU-D 0.818). Costs are substantial: ODE-RNN is 60 % slower than a GRU, Neural CDEs about five times slower than RNNs, and ContiFormer needs $N^2$ ODE systems. Neural CDE and ContiFormer, as presented, use natural cubic splines that look at future knots and are therefore non-causal.

## 5. Family (4): discretised linear state-space models

Linear dynamics $\dot x=Ax+Bu$ can be integrated exactly over any interval, which removes the solver while keeping the continuous-time semantics. [S4](../papers/s4.md) uses the bilinear rule with a learned per-channel step $\Delta$ and HiPPO initialisation; because its parameters are continuous, halving the audio sampling rate is handled by doubling $\Delta$ (96.3 % vs 98.3 % on SC10, without retraining). [DSS](../papers/dss.md) shows that a diagonal complex $A$ with ZOH, $\bar A=e^{\Lambda\Delta}$, $\bar B=\Lambda^{-1}(e^{\Lambda\Delta}-I)B$, matches S4; its NO-SCALE ablation, which drops the factor $\Lambda^{-1}(e^{\Lambda\Delta}-I)$, falls from 81.18 to 65.03 average LRA accuracy and fails Path-X. [S5](../papers/s5.md) computes a diagonal MIMO SSM with an associative scan, which permits a different $\Delta t_k$ at every step; on irregularly sampled pendulum images it reaches 3.41 ×10⁻³ MSE vs 3.94 for CRU at 86× its speed, and it degrades least of all models from 16 kHz to 8 kHz (96.52 → 94.53 %). [LRU](../papers/lru.md) removes the continuous-time story altogether, parameterising $\lambda=\exp(-e^{\nu}+ie^{\theta})$ directly and normalising inputs by $\sqrt{1-|\lambda|^2}$; it matches S4D and S5 on LRA and shows that replacing the ZOH factor by $\Delta B$ changes little, concluding that "discretisation performs normalisation." [Mamba](../papers/mamba.md) makes $\Delta$, $B$, $C$ input-dependent, which yields content selectivity at the cost of time invariance, and relies on a fused SRAM scan for efficiency.

The oscillatory variants change the generator rather than the discretisation. [UnICORNN](../papers/unicornn.md) discretises undamped nonlinear oscillators $y''=-[\sigma(w\odot y+Vu+b)+\alpha y]$ with symplectic Euler, giving invertible, memory-efficient recurrences with polynomially bounded gradients (EigenWorms 90.3 %). [LinOSS](../papers/linoss.md) uses linear oscillators $y''=-Ay+Bu$, $A\ge0$ diagonal, with implicit or IMEX steps computed by scan; it tops a fair benchmark of six long UEA sets (67.8 % vs 61.7–62.0 % for LRU, S5, S6 and 58.6 % for Mamba). In both, $\Delta t$ is a tuned timescale, and in LinOSS-IM the damping $|\lambda|^2=1/(1+\Delta t^2A)$ is produced by the integrator, so the effective forgetting rate changes with the step: such models are not consistent across sampling rates.

Two properties make exact (ZOH) discretisation the principled choice when $\Delta t$ varies. The transition satisfies the semigroup law $e^{\lambda\Delta_1}e^{\lambda\Delta_2}=e^{\lambda(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)}$, and the ZOH input term is also consistent: $\frac{e^{\lambda\Delta_1}-1}{\lambda}e^{\lambda\Delta_2}+\frac{e^{\lambda\Delta_2}-1}{\lambda}=\frac{e^{\lambda(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)}-1}{\lambda}$. Splitting a gap with a repeated observation therefore leaves the state unchanged, and the stationary response to a held input, $-b/\lambda$, does not depend on the sampling interval.

## 6. Family (5): periodic encodings of time or coordinates

The [Transformer](../papers/transformer.md) encoding $PE_{(pos,2i)}=\sin(pos/10000^{2i/d})$ (with cosines) makes a fixed offset a rotation of the code; learned embeddings performed identically in its ablation (25.7 vs 25.8 BLEU). [Fourier features](../papers/fourier-features.md) explain the effect: $\gamma(v)$ makes the network's NTK stationary with bandwidth set by the frequency scale, overcoming spectral bias (NeRF 26.14 → 34.51 PSNR). [SIREN](../papers/siren.md) puts sines in every layer with an initialisation ($\omega_0=30$, $\mathcal U(\pm\sqrt{6/n})$) that controls frequency growth. [Time2Vec](../papers/time2vec.md) learns frequencies and adds a linear unit, $\mathrm{t2v}(\tau)=[\omega_0\tau+\varphi_0,\sin(\omega_i\tau+\varphi_i)]$; [mTAN](../papers/mtan.md) uses the same form as attention queries and keys to resample irregular series at reference points (PhysioNet-2012 mortality 0.858, the best in the corpus); [RoPE](../papers/rope.md) rotates queries and keys so that logits depend on $m-n$, which with real timestamps gives attention over elapsed time. Diffusion models embed the noise level with the Transformer sinusoid ([DDPM](../papers/ddpm.md)).

These encodings embed the *value* of time, not a dynamics over it. They interpolate well over bounded, densely covered ranges (token positions, diffusion steps, calendar phase) and fail predictably elsewhere: they are periodic, so extrapolation repeats or produces unseen phase combinations; they alias above the sampling density; and their bandwidth must be tuned. The linear term in Time2Vec and mTAN is the only part that extrapolates monotonically.

## 7. DAWIN as a combination of families (4) and (5)

*Equivalence.* Write $\lambda_m=-\gamma_m-i\omega_m$. DAWIN's transition $D_m(\Delta t)=e^{\lambda_m\Delta t}$ is the ZOH transition of a diagonal complex SSM. Its input gain is

$$\alpha_m(\Delta t)=1-e^{-\gamma_m\Delta t}=(-\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_m)\cdot\frac{e^{\mathrm{Re}\lambda_m\,\Delta t}-1}{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_m},$$

i.e. the ZOH input factor of the *real* pole $-\gamma_m$, scaled by $\gamma_m$ so that the DC gain of the real-pole system is one. DAWIN is therefore S5 with per-step $\Delta t$, a diagonal input matrix, the real-pole ZOH numerator in place of the complex factor, and an input $u_{i,m}=A_me^{i(k_m^\top x_i-\theta_m)}$ that is a random Fourier feature of $x_i$. The claimed difference from SSMs, that DAWIN couples spatial projection and interval scaling "atomically" whereas SSMs use a separable $Bx$, does not survive: $\alpha_m(\Delta t)\,e^{ik_m^\top x}$ is a product of a time gain and a spatial feature, exactly the structure of $\bar B(\Delta t)\,x$.

*What the sinusoidal input map adds.* Three things. A bounded nonlinearity at the input, which lets one layer represent nonlinear features of $x$ (a property already exploited by [WFB](../papers/wfb.md)); boundedness of $|u|\le A_m$, which underlies Corollary 1's insensitivity to input outliers, although DAWIN's pre-norm blocks already apply LayerNorm before each layer; and a kernel interpretation: $\psi_{n,m}=\sum_j w_{nj}A_me^{i(k_m^\top x_j-\theta_m)}$ with $w_{nj}=\alpha_m(\Delta t_j)e^{\lambda_m(\tau_n-\tau_j)}$, so inner products of states across units approximate a stationary kernel in $x$ weighted by a damped, rotating temporal kernel. The costs are those of family (5): periodicity in $x$ (inputs differing by a period of every active $k_m$ are confused), a bandwidth fixed by $k_m\sim\mathcal N(0,1/d)$ on normalised inputs (phase standard deviation about 1 rad, far from SIREN's $\omega_0=30$), further lowered by the Laplacian penalty, and no channel mixing within the layer.

*Rate dependence.* Because transition and gain use different poles, the semigroup consistency of §5 fails when $\omega_m\neq0$: $(1-e^{-\gamma\Delta_1})e^{\lambda\Delta_2}+(1-e^{-\gamma\Delta_2})\neq1-e^{-\gamma(\Delta_1+\Delta_2)}$, so inserting a repeated observation changes the state. The stationary response to a held input is $A_m\alpha_m/(1-D_m)$, which equals $A_m\gamma_m/(\gamma_m+i\omega_m)$ as $\Delta t\to0$ and $A_m$ when $\Delta t\gg1/\gamma_m$. At DAWIN's initialisation ($\omega_m$ up to $2\pi$, $\gamma_m\approx0.13$–1.3) the magnitude ratio $\gamma_m/\sqrt{\gamma_m^2+\omega_m^2}$ reaches about 0.02, a fiftyfold change in gain between dense and sparse sampling of the same constant signal. The exact complex factor $(e^{\lambda\Delta t}-1)/\lambda$, rescaled by $|\lambda|$ if unit gain is wanted, would remove both defects at no cost.

*Theoretical weaknesses.* The associativity proof of Proposition 2 writes $(d_2d_1,\,d_2u_1+u_1)$ for $(d_2d_1,\,d_2u_1+u_2)$; the operator is the standard one and the code is correct. Theorem 1 bounds $|\psi_n|\le A_m/(1-e^{-\gamma_m\Delta t_{\min}})$ under $\Delta t_i\ge\Delta t_{\min}>0$, a hypothesis that DAWIN's own convention $\Delta t_1=0$ violates. Keeping $\alpha$ in the sum gives a stronger result: with $T_j=\tau_n-\tau_j$, $\sum_j(1-e^{-\gamma\Delta t_j})e^{-\gamma T_j}=\sum_j(e^{-\gamma T_j}-e^{-\gamma T_{j-1}})\le1$, so $|\psi_n|\le A_m$ for any positive intervals. The genuine gap is at $\Delta t=0$, where $\alpha(0)=D(0)=1$: $k$ simultaneous events add up to $kA_m$ without damping, and $\alpha$ jumps from $\approx\gamma\epsilon$ to 1 as $\Delta t$ goes from $\epsilon$ to 0. Same-timestamp events are common in clinical records and multi-sensor streams. The $\Delta t_{\text{zero}}$ probe announced for RQ3, which would expose this regime, is not reported.

*Empirical weaknesses.* (i) PhysioNet-2012 dense regression: DAWIN's "best overall accuracy" is 0.9531 NRMSE against 0.9553 for Time-Transformer and 0.9620 for S5; if NRMSE is RMSE divided by the target standard deviation, all models explain under 10 % of variance, and the margin is 0.2 %. Every Holm-adjusted $p=0.010$ in Table 2 is the floor attainable with ten seeds and five comparators, and measures seed variance on a single split. (ii) PhysioNet-2012 mortality: the comparison with the cited literature refutes the reported level.

## 8. Robotics: irregular sensing, control rates, and TGI's fixed step

Robot sensors are multi-rate and jittery: joint encoders and IMUs at hundreds of hertz to kilohertz, cameras and depth sensors at 10–60 Hz with variable latency, elevation maps updated asynchronously, event cameras with no frame rate at all. Policies, by contrast, usually run at a fixed rate (50 Hz in the legged-robot work of the TGI study) and send set-points to faster PD loops; between ticks the action is held constant. That hold is exactly the ZOH assumption of family (4), so for *action* inputs ZOH is physically correct, whereas for *sampled observations* it is a modelling assumption whose error grows with $\Delta t$ relative to the signal bandwidth.

The world models of [TGI](../../tgi-lineage/papers/tgi.md) and [RWM](../../tgi-lineage/papers/rwm.md) use a fixed step of 0.02 s, and for their setting this is the right choice. Their data come from a simulator stepped at a fixed decimation, so $\Delta t$ is constant and carries no information; embedding it would add a constant input. TGI's model predicts normalised increments integrated with that step, and its learned contact matrix $B$ is described as "effective inverse inertia times $\Delta t$": the step is folded into the weights. Its policy gradient is BPTT over 32 imagined steps ([m-model-learning](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-model-learning)), which benefits from a Markov, fixed-step transition. Finally, contact makes the dynamics discontinuous, so the exact-in-$\Delta t$ extrapolation of linear SSMs would not transfer to the contact-rich part of the dynamics in any case. Timing irregularities that do matter are handled implicitly: TGI feeds the previous set-point because actuation lag is randomised ([m-domain-rand](../../tgi-lineage/math-cookbook.md#m-domain-rand)).

A $\Delta t$-aware model becomes necessary when the rate is not fixed: deploying a model trained at 50 Hz at a different control rate, compensating variable perception latency, fusing multi-rate sensors, or event-driven control. The least invasive change for a TGI-style model is to make the step explicit, $s'=s+\Delta t\,f_\theta(s,a,\Delta t)$, and train with randomised decimation, which is consistent to first order as $\Delta t\to0$. For the observation history (for instance a recurrent encoder of asynchronous exteroception), an S5-style diagonal SSM with per-step $\Delta t$ gives exact decay and rotation between arrivals and a GRU-ODE-Bayes-style predict–update structure for filtering. Periodic encodings of absolute time are not appropriate for control, except as phase variables for periodic gaits.

## 9. Mathematical background

**ZOH discretisation.** For $\dot x=Ax+Bu$ with $u(t)=u_k$ held on $(t_{k-1},t_k]$ and $\Delta_k=t_k-t_{k-1}$, variation of constants gives

$$x_k=e^{A\Delta_k}x_{k-1}+\int_0^{\Delta_k}e^{A(\Delta_k-s)}ds\,Bu_k=e^{A\Delta_k}x_{k-1}+A^{-1}(e^{A\Delta_k}-I)Bu_k,$$

exact for piecewise-constant input. Since $A^{-1}(e^{A\Delta}-I)=\Delta I+\frac{\Delta^2}2A+\dots$, the input factor is $\approx\Delta B$ for small $\Delta$, which is LRU's first-order replacement, and it scales the input so that the stationary response $-A^{-1}Bu$ is independent of $\Delta$.

**Bilinear discretisation.** Trapezoidal integration of the state with held input gives $x_k-x_{k-1}=\frac{\Delta}2A(x_k+x_{k-1})+\Delta Bu_k$, hence $\bar A=(I-\frac\Delta2A)^{-1}(I+\frac\Delta2A)$, $\bar B=(I-\frac\Delta2A)^{-1}\Delta B$. It corresponds to $s=\frac2\Delta\frac{z-1}{z+1}$, which maps the open left half-plane onto the open unit disk (stability for every $\Delta$) but warps frequencies as $\omega_d=\frac2\Delta\arctan\frac{\omega\Delta}2$; it is second-order accurate but, unlike ZOH, not exact for any input class.

**Matrix exponential of diagonal complex systems.** If $A=V\Lambda V^{-1}$, then $e^{A\Delta}=Ve^{\Lambda\Delta}V^{-1}$ with $e^{\Lambda\Delta}=\mathrm{diag}(e^{\lambda_j\Delta})$, so after the change of basis each mode evolves independently. For $\lambda=-\gamma+i\omega$, $e^{\lambda\Delta}=e^{-\gamma\Delta}(\cos\omega\Delta+i\sin\omega\Delta)$: decay times rotation. In real coordinates the pair $(p,q)$ with generator $\begin{psmallmatrix}-\gamma&-\omega\\\omega&-\gamma\end{psmallmatrix}$ has exponential $e^{-\gamma\Delta}R(\omega\Delta)$, which is DAWIN's Eq. 4 (with $\omega\to-\omega$). Real-valued signals use conjugate pairs and take real parts. The semigroup law makes products over irregular intervals depend only on total elapsed time: $\prod_{r=j+1}^ne^{\lambda\Delta_r}=e^{\lambda(\tau_n-\tau_j)}$.

**Adjoint method.** For $\dot z=f(z,\theta)$ and loss $L(z(t_1))$, define $a(t)=\partial L/\partial z(t)$. Perturbing the trajectory and integrating by parts gives $\dot a=-a^\top\partial f/\partial z$ and $dL/d\theta=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}a^\top\partial f/\partial\theta\,dt$, solved backward from $a(t_1)$ with $z$ reconstructed backward or checkpointed; losses at intermediate times add jumps $\partial L/\partial z(t_i)$ to $a$. For a linear recurrence $\psi_i=d_i\psi_{i-1}+u_i$ the discrete analogue is $\bar\delta_i=\delta_i+d_{i+1}^*\bar\delta_{i+1}$, a reverse linear recurrence that is itself a scan (DAWIN's Eq. 5).

**Associative scan.** The recurrence $x_k=a_kx_{k-1}+b_k$ is the prefix composition of affine maps $x\mapsto a_kx+b_k$. Composition $(a_2,b_2)\circ(a_1,b_1)=(a_2a_1,\,a_2b_1+b_2)$ is associative, because $((a_3,b_3)\circ(a_2,b_2))\circ(a_1,b_1)=(a_3a_2a_1,\,a_3a_2b_1+a_3b_2+b_3)=(a_3,b_3)\circ((a_2,b_2)\circ(a_1,b_1))$, with identity $(1,0)$. Blelloch's algorithm computes all prefixes in $O(\log L)$ parallel depth and $O(L)$ work for scalar or diagonal $a_k$; per-step $\Delta t_k$ only changes the elements, which is why S5, Mamba, LinOSS and DAWIN can handle time-varying transitions, while convolutional S4 cannot.

**NTK and spectral bias.** In the infinite-width limit a network trained by gradient descent on squared loss evolves as $\hat y(t)\approx K_{\text{test}}K^{-1}(I-e^{-\eta Kt})y$ with $K$ the NTK. In the eigenbasis $K=Q\Lambda Q^\top$ the training residual decays as $e^{-\eta\lambda_it}$, so components with small eigenvalues are learned slowly. For a standard MLP on low-dimensional inputs the eigenvalues fall off rapidly with frequency, hence spectral bias. A Fourier map makes the composed kernel stationary, $k(v_1,v_2)=\sum_ja_j^2\cos(2\pi b_j^\top(v_1-v_2))$, with spectrum set by the $b_j$: wider spectra learn high frequencies faster but alias, narrower ones underfit ([Fourier features](../papers/fourier-features.md)).

## 10. Recommendations

Ranked by the strength of evidence and the breadth of the conditions they cover.

1. **Fixed-rate data (simulation, fixed-rate control): do not embed $\Delta t$.** Use a discrete-time model whose step is folded into the weights, as TGI and RWM do, with a discrete recurrence ([LRU](../papers/lru.md)) or a Transformer with [RoPE](../papers/rope.md) if history is needed. Evidence: LRU matches continuous-time SSMs on regular data without any $\Delta$; Latent ODE and GRU-ODE-Bayes find discretised models equal to continuous ones on regular or plentiful data. Limitation: a rate change at deployment requires retraining or the explicit-step form of §8.

2. **Irregular sampling, long sequences or throughput constraints: an S5-style diagonal complex SSM with per-step $\Delta t$ and exact ZOH.** Use $\bar\lambda_k=e^{\lambda\Delta t_k}$ and the complex input factor $(e^{\lambda\Delta t_k}-1)/\lambda$, a dense input matrix, a fused scan, and nonlinear mixing between layers. Evidence: [S5](../papers/s5.md) on the irregular pendulum (3.41 ×10⁻³ MSE, 86× faster than CRU) and on 8 kHz zero-shot audio; the [DSS](../papers/dss.md) NO-SCALE ablation for the input factor. Limitations: ZOH assumes held inputs; the transition is LTI given $\Delta t$; same-timestamp events need an explicit merge rule.

3. **Possible changes of rate or unseen $\Delta t$ at test time: continuous-parameter LTI SSMs with exact discretisation, and avoid embeddings without a dynamical prior.** S4 and S5 lose 2–5 points when the rate halves, whereas CNNs and ConvNets collapse (S5 Table 2: ConvNet 95.51 → 7.26 %). Avoid integrator-dependent damping (LinOSS-IM, LTC's fused step) and mixed-pole gains (DAWIN) when the rate varies. Limitation: exactness holds only for the linear part; DAWIN's oscillator task shows that all models, Mamba and DAWIN included, degrade on out-of-range intervals (validation 0.12 vs test 0.76 NRMSE).

4. **Content-dependent gating on top of irregular time: combine Mamba-style selection with physical time,** $\Delta_k=\Delta t_k\cdot\mathrm{softplus}(w^\top x_k+b)$. This keeps ZOH semantics and the decay-gate behaviour of [GRU-D](../papers/gru-d.md) and [Mamba](../papers/mamba.md)'s Theorem 1. Evidence is indirect (no paper in the corpus tests the combination); exact rate rescaling is lost.

5. **Sparse, small, partially observed data where interpolation or uncertainty matters: filtering ODE models** ([GRU-ODE-Bayes](../papers/gru-ode-bayes.md), [Latent ODE](../papers/latent-ode.md)) or [Neural CDE](../papers/neural-cde.md) with observational-intensity channels. Evidence: small-sample and interpolation results in §4; sepsis AUC 0.880 with intensity vs 0.776 without. Limitations: 1.6–5× cost, non-causal splines unless replaced, and no advantage for sequence-level classification.

6. **Sequence-level classification of irregular clinical series: attention over time embeddings plus missingness masks.** [mTAN](../papers/mtan.md) (0.858 PhysioNet AUROC at 0.2 min per epoch) or a Transformer with continuous-time RoPE; [ContiFormer](../papers/contiformer.md) adds at most a few points over a discrete Transformer at much higher cost. Limitation: kernel smoothers track dynamical state poorly (mTAND 65.64 ×10⁻³ vs S5 3.41 on the pendulum).

7. **Bounded scalar conditioning or calendar periodicity: sinusoidal embeddings with a linear term.** Diffusion timesteps, gait phase, time-of-day: [Transformer](../papers/transformer.md)/[DDPM](../papers/ddpm.md) sinusoids or [Time2Vec](../papers/time2vec.md); [Fourier features](../papers/fourier-features.md) or [SIREN](../papers/siren.md) for coordinates, with bandwidth tuned to the signal. Limitations: aliasing and periodic extrapolation; no state dynamics.

8. **DAWIN: not recommended over S5 on current evidence.** If used, replace $\alpha$ by the exact complex ZOH factor (normalised by $|\lambda|$), define a merge rule or $\alpha(0)=0$ for same-timestamp events, report the $\Delta t=0$ probe, audit the PhysioNet-2012 pipeline for leakage, and benchmark against fused-kernel Mamba and S5 given the same $\Delta t$ inputs.

## Corpus additions

- [Time2Vec](../papers/time2vec.md) (1907.05321): canonical learned periodic-plus-linear time embedding, needed for family (1)/(5).
- [mTAN](../papers/mtan.md) (2101.10318): attention over time embeddings; supplies the strongest PhysioNet-2012 mortality AUROC used to assess DAWIN.
- [RoPE](../papers/rope.md) (2104.09864): multiplicative rotary encoding, the attention counterpart of DAWIN's phase rotation.
- [Transformer](../papers/transformer.md) (1706.03762): original sinusoidal positional encoding, also the diffusion timestep embedding.
