## 1. Problem and position in the lineage

Linear state-space models $\dot x=Ax+Bu$, $y=Cx+Du$ with HiPPO matrices can memorise long histories, but the predecessor LSSL cost $O(N^2L)$ time and $O(NL)$ memory and its fast algorithms were numerically unstable because the HiPPO matrix is highly non-normal. S4 makes the model practical and starts the family of discretised linear SSMs ([DSS](dss.md), [S5](s5.md), [LRU](lru.md), [Mamba](mamba.md), [LinOSS](linoss.md)) on which DAWIN's recurrence is modelled.

## 2. Method

The continuous system is discretised with step $\Delta$ by the bilinear rule

$$\bar A=(I-\tfrac\Delta2A)^{-1}(I+\tfrac\Delta2A),\qquad \bar B=(I-\tfrac\Delta2A)^{-1}\Delta B,$$

giving the recurrence $x_k=\bar Ax_{k-1}+\bar Bu_k$ or, unrolled, the convolution $y=\bar K*u$ with $\bar K=(C\bar B,C\bar A\bar B,\dots,C\bar A^{L-1}\bar B)$. HiPPO-LegS, $A_{nk}=-\sqrt{(2n+1)(2k+1)}$ for $n>k$, $-(n+1)$ on the diagonal, is normal plus low rank; S4 conjugates it to diagonal-plus-low-rank form $\Lambda-PQ^*$, evaluates the kernel's truncated generating function at roots of unity through a Cauchy kernel with a Woodbury correction, and recovers $\bar K$ by inverse FFT, at $\tilde O(N+L)$ cost. $\Delta$ is a learned per-channel timescale; channels are mixed by position-wise linear layers.

## 3. Evidence

Long Range Arena: average 86.09 with Path-X (length 16,384) solved at 96.35 % where all Transformers are at chance (Table 4; the earlier paper version reported 80.48 / 88.10). Raw Speech Commands SC10 (length 16,000): 98.32 %; at test-time half sampling rate, by changing $\Delta$ alone, 96.30 %, while CKConv drops to 65.96 % and NRDE to 15.12 % (Table 5). sCIFAR 91.13 %, WikiText-103 perplexity 20.95 vs Transformer 20.51 with 60× faster generation, CIFAR density 2.85 bits/dim.

## 4. Critical analysis

The sampling-rate experiment is the most relevant evidence in this corpus for "extrapolation in $\Delta t$": because S4 learns continuous parameters, halving the rate is handled by doubling $\Delta$, losing two points of accuracy without retraining. This depends on the discretisation being a consistent approximation of one continuous system; the bilinear rule maps the left half-plane to the unit disk exactly, so stability is preserved for any $\Delta$, but its frequency warping ($\omega_d=\frac2\Delta\arctan\frac{\omega\Delta}{2}$) distorts high frequencies as $\Delta$ grows. S4's convolutional mode requires a fixed $\Delta$ across a sequence, so irregular sampling is not supported, a limitation removed by [S5](s5.md). Later work ([DSS](dss.md), [LRU](lru.md)) showed that the low-rank HiPPO structure and even the continuous-time interpretation are not necessary for LRA performance, and that the discretisation's main function may be normalisation. The NPLR algorithm is complex, and the authors note instability when eigenvalues enter the right half-plane, corrected by $\Lambda-PP^*$.

## 6. Relevance to the study question

Question 3: family (4). It introduces $\Delta$ as a learnable timescale of a continuous system and demonstrates zero-shot resampling, the property that makes discretised continuous parameterisations attractive when the control or sensor rate may change.
