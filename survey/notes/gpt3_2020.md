# Language Models are Few-Shot Learners
**Brown, Mann, Ryder, Subbiah, Kaplan, Dhariwal, Neelakantan, Shyam, Sastry, Askell, et al. (OpenAI); arXiv:2005.14165, 2020 (NeurIPS 2020)** · **Key:** `gpt3_2020` · **OpenAlex citations:** 63280 · **Era:** tf
**Read status:** partial: abstract, Secs. 1-2, 3 (3.1-3.3 and 3.7-3.9 in detail; 3.4-3.6 via tables/greps), 4 (contamination summary), 5, 6.3, App. B and D read; Sec. 6.1-6.2 (misuse, bias), App. A, C, E-H skimmed or located by grep only

## 1. Problem and motivation
Pre-train-then-fine-tune had removed task-specific architectures, but still needed thousands to hundreds of thousands of labelled examples for every new task (Sec. 1). The authors list three issues: labelled data is a practical bottleneck; larger models fine-tuned on narrow data can exploit spurious correlations and generalize poorly out of distribution; and humans learn new language tasks from an instruction or a few examples. GPT-2 had shown task conditioning through text, but its results were far inferior to fine-tuning (e.g. 4% on Natural Questions, Sec. 1).

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Scale an ordinary autoregressive Transformer language model to 175B parameters and use it without any weight updates: describe the task in text, optionally prepend a few worked examples (the "prompt"), and let the model continue the text. The authors call this "in-context learning" and view it as the inner loop of meta-learning during which the pre-trained model recognizes or adapts to the task within a single forward pass (Fig. 1.1). Settings: zero-shot (instruction only), one-shot (one demonstration), few-shot (typically 10-100 demonstrations fitting in the 2048-token context) (Sec. 2, Fig. 2.1). Analogy: a very widely read colleague who, handed a brief and two sample answers, can imitate the pattern without retraining.

## 3. The mathematics
The paper introduces little new mathematics; its content is empirical. I give the formulas the paper states and flag what is assumed.

**(a) Training objective (not written as an equation in the paper; standard autoregressive log-likelihood, simplified background):** $\mathcal L=-\sum_t\log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})$. $x_t$ is the $t$-th token, $\theta$ the model weights. The model is trained to predict the next token on 300B tokens (Table 2.1); the same objective is used for every task at test time, through the prompt. Cross-entropy follows a power law in compute, as in `scaling2020`, extending it by two more orders of magnitude with only slight deviation (Fig. 3.1).

**(b) Multiple-choice scoring (Sec. 2.4):** pick the completion with highest per-token LM likelihood given the context; for ARC, OpenBookQA and RACE use
$$\frac{P(\text{completion}\mid\text{context})}{P(\text{completion}\mid\text{answer context})}$$
where "answer context" is the string "Answer: " or "A: ". This normalizes by the unconditional probability of the completion, so common answers are not unfairly favored.

**(c) Compute estimate (App. D, Table D.1):** training FLOPs $\approx$ parameters $\times$ tokens $\times 6$ (the table's columns give 6 for forward+backward multiply-adds, with factors for active parameters, so this is a simplified reading of the table). For GPT-3 175B: $174{,}600$ M params $\times\ 300$B tokens $\to 3.14\times10^{23}$ FLOPs $=3.64\times10^{3}$ petaflop/s-days (computed from the table's columns, not stated as a formula).

**(d) Optimization details (App. B):** Adam with $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.95$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, global-norm gradient clipping at 1.0, cosine decay to 10% of the learning rate over 260B tokens, linear warmup over 375M tokens, weight decay 0.1, batch size ramped from 32k tokens over the first 4-12B tokens. Final batch size 3.2M tokens, learning rate $0.6\times10^{-4}$ for 175B (Table 2.1).

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Architecture: GPT-2 style decoder-only Transformer (pre-normalization, modified initialization), with alternating dense and locally banded sparse attention layers like Sparse Transformer; context $n_{ctx}=2048$; $d_{ff}=4d_{model}$ (Sec. 2.1).
2. Eight sizes from 125M to 175B parameters. GPT-3 175B: 96 layers, $d_{model}=12288$, 96 heads, $d_{head}=128$ (Table 2.1).
3. Data (Table 2.2): filtered Common Crawl (410B tokens, 60% of mix), WebText2 (22%), Books1 (8%), Books2 (8%), Wikipedia (3%); higher-quality sets sampled more often; Common Crawl filtered by similarity to reference corpora and fuzzy-deduplicated. 300B tokens seen in training.
4. Evaluation: draw $K$ demonstrations from the training set (or dev set when none), concatenate with newlines, and score or sample the completion; beam search of width 4 and $\alpha=0.6$ for free-form answers (Sec. 2.4). No fine-tuning.

## 5. Achievements
- Aggregate: few-shot gains grow faster with model size than zero-shot across 42 benchmarks (Fig. 1.3).
- LAMBADA (Table 3.2): 76.2% zero-shot, 86.4% few-shot vs 68.0% prior SOTA; PTB zero-shot perplexity 20.5 vs 35.8 (Table 3.1).
- TriviaQA closed-book: 64.3 / 68.0 / 71.2% (0/1/few-shot), above fine-tuned T5-11B+SSM at 60.5 (Table 3.3). Natural Questions 29.9% few-shot vs 36.6 for fine-tuned T5-11B+SSM; WebQS 41.5 vs 44.7.
- Translation (Table 3.4): few-shot BLEU 39.2 Fr-En, 40.6 De-En, 39.5 Ro-En, beating prior unsupervised NMT into English; weaker into other languages (En-Fr 32.6, En-Ro 21.0).
- SuperGLUE test (Table 3.8): few-shot average 71.8 vs 69.0 for fine-tuned BERT-Large, vs 89.0 SOTA; COPA 92.0, WiC 49.4 (chance). CoQA 85.0 F1 few-shot (Sec. 1).
- Arithmetic (Table 3.9): few-shot 100.0% 2-digit addition, 80.4% 3-digit addition, 25.5% 4-digit addition, 29.2% 2-digit multiplication; only 17/2000 addition problems found in training data (Sec. 3.9.1).
- Human detection of GPT-3 175B-written ~200-word news articles was 52% (chance 50%) vs 86% for a control model (Table 3.11); about 500-word articles 52% (Table 3.12).

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors (Sec. 5):** repetition, coherence loss and contradictions in long generations; weak "common sense physics"; near chance on comparison tasks such as WiC and ANLI, and weak on some reading comprehension (RACE, QuAC); autoregressive-only design with no bidirectional or denoising objectives may hurt such tasks; objective weights every token equally and is not grounded in other modalities; poor pre-training sample efficiency relative to humans; unclear whether few-shot truly learns tasks or just recognizes them; costly and inconvenient inference; opaque decisions and inherited bias. A filtering bug left some train/test overlap and retraining was infeasible (Sec. 2.2); contamination analysis flagged six benchmark groups and PIQA (29% of examples flagged, about 3 percentage point drop) and Winograd are marked with asterisks (Sec. 4).
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** The model is not instruction-following or aligned: prompts must be engineered and outputs can be unsafe, which motivated later RLHF work (`instructgpt2022`). The token budget (300B for 175B parameters) was later argued to be far too small for the compute spent (`chinchilla2022`). Few-shot accuracy is sensitive to prompt format and example selection.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `transformer2017`, GPT-2 (Radford et al., not in set), `scaling2020` (explicitly motivates scaling and the "many fewer tokens" regime, Fig. 2.2), `bert2018` (the fine-tuning paradigm it contrasts with), `adam2014`, Sparse Transformer.
- **Led to:** `instructgpt2022`, `cot2022` (hindsight, prompting to elicit reasoning), `chinchilla2022`, `llama2023`, `flamingo2022` (few-shot recipe extended to vision-language, hindsight).
- Single idea added: a sufficiently large autoregressive LM acquires task-agnostic in-context learning, so tasks can be specified entirely by a text prompt with no gradient updates.
