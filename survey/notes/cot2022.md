# Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models
**Wei, Wang, Schuurmans, Bosma, Ichter, Xia, Chi, Le, Zhou (Google Research, Brain Team), NeurIPS 2022 (arXiv:2201.11903v6)** · **Key:** `cot2022` · **OpenAlex citations:** 39078 · **Era:** tf
**Read status:** full text for Sections 1-8 and Appendix A-B tables; exemplar prompt appendices (G-H) and error-analysis tables located but not read in full.

## 1. Problem and motivation
Scaling up language models improved many tasks, but "scaling up model size alone has not proved sufficient" for arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic reasoning (Sec. 1). Two existing ideas each had a flaw: training or fine-tuning with natural-language rationales needs costly, high-quality rationale datasets, and standard few-shot prompting (input-output pairs, Brown et al. 2020) "works poorly on tasks that require reasoning abilities" and often has a flat scaling curve (Sec. 1).

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Keep ordinary few-shot prompting, but write each exemplar not as (question, answer) but as (question, chain of thought, answer), where the chain of thought is a short series of natural-language intermediate steps ending in the answer. Shown a handful of such exemplars, a sufficiently large model imitates the style and writes out its own intermediate steps before answering. Analogy: it is the difference between asking a student to blurt the answer and asking them to show their working on scratch paper. No training and no weight changes are involved ("No language models were finetuned", Sec. 6). The authors stress it is an emergent ability: it helps only at roughly 100B parameters and above (Sec. 3.2).

## 3. The mathematics
The paper has no numbered equations; it is an empirical prompting study. The only formal content is the change in prompt format, which I write in simplified notation (not the paper's own):

$$\text{standard: } x_1\,a_1,\dots,x_k\,a_k,\;x \;\Rightarrow\; \hat a \qquad\qquad \text{CoT: } x_1\,r_1\,a_1,\dots,x_k\,r_k\,a_k,\;x \;\Rightarrow\; \hat r\,\hat a$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $x_i, a_i$ | exemplar question and final answer ($k=8$ for most math tasks, Sec. 3.1) |
| $r_i$ | hand-written chain of thought (intermediate natural-language steps) for exemplar $i$ |
| $x$ | test question |
| $\hat r,\hat a$ | model-generated reasoning and answer, decoded greedily (Sec. 3.1) |

Meaning: the answer is read off after the generated reasoning, so the model conditions its answer on its own intermediate tokens. The paper lists the properties this gives (Sec. 2): extra computation can be allocated to harder problems (more steps means more tokens), the reasoning is inspectable, and it applies to any task solvable via language. Evaluation metric is solve rate / accuracy (%). The ablations (Sec. 3.3) test which part of this matters: equation-only, "variable compute only" (dots equal to the equation length), and chain of thought placed after the answer. The latter two perform "about the same as the baseline", so the gain is not just extra tokens or knowledge activation.

## 4. Method / algorithm
1. Hand-write 8 chain-of-thought exemplars for math word problems (Appendix Table 20; 4 exemplars with solutions from the training set for multiple-choice AQuA). They "did not undergo prompt engineering" (Sec. 3.1).
2. Prompt an off-the-shelf model with the exemplars plus the test question; decode greedily. Models: GPT-3 (text-ada/babbage/curie/davinci-002, presumed 350M, 1.3B, 6.7B, 175B), LaMDA (422M to 137B), PaLM (8B, 62B, 540B), UL2 20B, Codex (Sec. 3.1). LaMDA averaged over five exemplar orders.
3. Benchmarks: GSM8K, SVAMP, ASDiv, AQuA, MAWPS (arithmetic); CSQA, StrategyQA, Date and Sports Understanding, SayCan (commonsense); last-letter concatenation and coin flip (symbolic, with out-of-domain longer inputs).
4. Optional post-hoc external calculator on generated equations (Appendix B, Table 1).

## 5. Achievements
- GSM8K: PaLM 540B 56.9% with CoT vs 17.9% standard; prior best (fine-tuned GPT-3 with verifier) 55% (Appendix Table 1; Fig. 2). New state of the art; with calculator 58.6%.
- Other arithmetic with PaLM 540B CoT: SVAMP 79.0, ASDiv 73.9, AQuA 35.8, MAWPS 93.3 (Table 1). Codex code-davinci-002 reaches 63.1% on GSM8K with CoT (Table 1). GPT-3 175B: 15.6 to 46.9 on GSM8K.
- Emergence: no gain for small models; UL2 20B moves GSM8K only 4.1 to 4.4; LaMDA 137B 6.5 to 14.3 (Table 1, Fig. 4). Gains largest on harder problems; tiny or negative on one-step MAWPS subsets (Sec. 3.2).
- Commonsense: PaLM 540B StrategyQA 75.6% vs prior best 69.4%; Sports Understanding 95.4% vs unaided human enthusiast 84%; minimal gain on CSQA (Sec. 4, Fig. 7).
- Symbolic: near 100% in-domain with PaLM 540B; chain of thought gives upward scaling on longer OOD inputs where standard prompting fails (Sec. 5, Fig. 8).
- Robust to annotator, exemplar sets (including three sets drawn from GSM8K training data), order and number of exemplars (Sec. 3.4, Appendix A.2).
- Manual check: of 50 correct LaMDA 137B GSM8K answers, all chains were correct except two coincidental; of 50 wrong ones, 46% were nearly correct (minor mistakes), 54% had major semantic or coherence errors (Sec. 3.2).

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors (Sec. 6):** whether the network is actually "reasoning" is left open; manual exemplar writing is cheap for few-shot but could be prohibitive for fine-tuning; there is no guarantee of correct reasoning paths, so correct and incorrect answers both occur; emergence only at large scale makes it costly to serve. Also: gain minimal on CSQA, and little headroom or benefit on easy one-step tasks (Sec. 3.2, 4); sensitivity to exemplars shows variance (Sec. 3.4).
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** written reasoning is not necessarily a faithful account of how the model computed its answer; greedy single-sample decoding was soon replaced by sampling plus majority vote (self-consistency, which the paper only mentions as follow-up work); benchmarks such as GSM8K have since saturated; prompts depend on hand-written exemplars unless zero-shot variants are used.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `gpt3_2020` (few-shot prompting), Ling et al. 2017 and Cobbe et al. 2021 (natural-language rationales, verifiers), Nye et al. 2021 (scratchpads), `scaling2020` (scaling motivation).
- **Led to:** `r1_2025` (RL training that makes long chains of thought emerge as a learned behaviour rather than a prompt), and the wider test-time-compute / reasoning-model direction (hindsight); self-consistency and zero-shot "think step by step" prompting (hindsight).
- **Single idea added:** put worked intermediate steps in the few-shot exemplars so that a large enough frozen model writes its own reasoning before the answer, unlocking multi-step performance with no training.
