## 1. Problem and motivation
Physical-AI systems (robots, vehicles) need data with interleaved observations and actions, which is scarce and risky to collect. The paper proposes a "world foundation model" (WFM): a general-purpose world model, pretrained on huge video data, that can be post-trained into a customised world model for a specific robot or driving setup. It is a platform paper: data pipeline, tokenizers, two families of pretrained models, post-training examples and a guardrail, released open-weight (Abstract, Sec. 1).

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Define a world model as $\mathcal W$ predicting the next observation $\hat x_{t+1}$ from past observations $x_{0:t}$ and a perturbation $c_t$ (an action, text, camera pose, etc.) (Sec. 2, Fig. 3). Then build it as large-scale video generation: curate about 100M video clips from 20M hours, compress video with causal tokenizers, pretrain a diffusion transformer (continuous tokens) and an autoregressive transformer (discrete tokens), then fine-tune on a small dataset with the control signal of interest. Analogy: a general physics-flavoured video "prior" that you tune like a language model to a particular robot's camera and controls. Note that "world model" here means a video generative model conditioned on a control input; no latent state or planning machinery is part of the platform.

## 3. The mathematics
**(1) Tokenizer (Sec. 4.1).** $\hat x_{0:T}=\mathcal D(\mathcal E(x_{0:T}))$ with $x_{0:T}\in\mathbb R^{(1+T)\times H\times W\times3}$, tokens $z_{0:T'}\in\mathbb R^{(1+T')\times H'\times W'\times C}$, spatial compression $s_{HW}=H/H'=W/W'$ and temporal compression $s_T=T/T'$. The encoder first applies a 2-level Haar wavelet transform (4x downsampling in $x,y,t$), then causal residual blocks with causal spatio-temporal attention. Continuous latents use a plain autoencoder (latent dim 16); discrete use FSQ with levels (8,8,8,5,5,5), vocabulary 64,000. Causality means an image tokenizer is the one-frame special case, enabling joint image and video training.

**(2)-(4) Tokenizer losses (Sec. 4.2).** Stage 1: $\mathcal L_1=\lVert\hat x_{0:T}-x_{0:T}\rVert_1$ plus a VGG-19 perceptual loss $\mathcal L_{Perceptual}=\frac1L\sum_t\sum_{l=1}^L\alpha_l\lVert \mathrm{VGG}_l(\hat x_t)-\mathrm{VGG}_l(x_t)\rVert_1$ ($l$ indexes layers, $\alpha_l$ layer weights). Stage 2 adds an optical-flow loss (temporal smoothness), the Gram-matrix loss $\mathcal L_{Gram}$ (same form with $\mathrm{GM}_l$) for sharpness, and an adversarial loss in fine-tuning. No commitment or KL losses are used.

**(5)-(8) Diffusion WFM (Sec. 5.1.1, EDM-style).**
$$\mathcal L(D_\theta,\sigma)=\mathbb E_{x_0,n}\lVert D_\theta(x_0+n;\sigma)-x_0\rVert_2^2,\quad n\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I)$$
with overall loss a $\sigma$-weighted expectation using $\lambda(\sigma)=(\sigma^2+\sigma_{data}^2)/(\sigma\sigma_{data})^2$, $\ln\sigma\sim\mathcal N(P_{mean},P_{std})$, and an MLP-parameterised uncertainty $u(\sigma)$ that reweights noise levels (the exact placement of $u(\sigma)$ in Eq. (6) is garbled in extraction; the text says high-uncertainty levels are down-weighted and $u$ is penalised). Symbols: $D_\theta$ denoiser, $x_0$ clean latent of an image or video, $n$ noise, $\sigma_{data}$ data standard deviation. It is the same denoising score matching as DIAMOND (`diamond2024`), applied in tokenizer latent space with a DiT-based backbone.

**(9) Autoregressive WFM (Sec. 5.2).** $\mathcal L_{NLL}=\sum_i-\log P(v_i\mid v_1,\dots,v_{i-1};\Theta)$ over discrete video tokens $v_i$ from the discrete tokenizer; a Llama-3-style decoder-only transformer with parameters $\Theta$.

**(10) Sampson error (Sec. 5.3.1), an evaluation metric.** $\epsilon_{samp}=\frac1N\sum_i\frac{|\bar y_i^\top F\bar x_i|}{\sqrt{\lVert SF\bar x_i\rVert^2+\lVert SF^\top\bar y_i\rVert^2}}$ with homogeneous matched points $\bar x_i,\bar y_i$, fundamental matrix $F$, $S=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,0)$; measures epipolar consistency of generated video.

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Data (Sec. 3): about 20M hours of raw video (720p-4k); steps split, filter, annotate (VLM caption per 256 frames), dedup, shard; shot detection chooses TransNetV2 (Table 1). Categories include driving 11%, hand motion/manipulation 16%, navigation 16%, nature dynamics 20%, etc. The text says about 100M clips for pretraining and 10M for fine-tuning (superscripts lost in extraction; the intro says about 100M clips).
2. Models (Table 10): diffusion Cosmos-Predict1-7B and 14B (Text2World, then Video2World); autoregressive 4B and 12B (video prediction) plus 5B and 13B Video2World with T5 cross-attention; prompt upsampler (12B) for diffusion models; a diffusion decoder to fix blurriness of the discrete tokenizer.
3. Diffusion backbone: DiT with 3D patchification, FPS-aware 3D RoPE plus learnable absolute embeddings, T5-XXL cross-attention, QK-norm (RMSNorm), AdaLN-LoRA (7B model: 11B to 7B parameters, "performance parity", Sec. 5.1.2). Progressive training: 512p/57 frames, then 720p/121 frames, then high-quality fine-tuning (Table 12).
4. Compute: all reported WFMs trained on 10,000 H100 GPUs for three months (Sec. 5).
5. Post-training: camera pose (Sec. 6.1), instruction text (Cosmos-1X humanoid data, about 200 hours, about 12,000 episodes), action vectors on Bridge (7-D end-effector deltas), multi-view driving (RDS, 3.6M 20-second clips, about 20,000 hours).

## 5. Achievements
- Tokenizers (Sec. 4.3, Tables 5-6, Fig. 8): claims +4 dB PSNR on DAVIS over prior tokenizers and up to 12x faster; e.g. Cosmos-0.1 CV4x8x8 DAVIS PSNR 32.80 vs CogVideoX 29.29; Cosmos-Tokenize1 CV4x8x8-360p 35.85. Introduces TokenBench (500 videos).
- 3D consistency (Table 19, 500 RealEstate10K videos): Sampson error 0.355 (7B Text2World) vs VideoLDM 0.841 (real videos 0.431); pose-estimation success 62.6% vs 4.4% (real 56.4%); view-synthesis PSNR 33.02 vs 26.23. Static scenes only.
- Physics alignment (Table 20, 800 simulated clips, 8 scenarios): the authors find that "all the WFMs equally struggle with physics adherence" and larger models do not do better. With 9 conditioning frames the 7B diffusion model gets PSNR 21.06 and average object IoU 0.592; with 1 frame, IoU 0.332.
- Robotics post-training (Sec. 6.2): instruction following human preference 78.3% overall for the 7B model vs 13.0% for VideoLDM-Instruction (23 test episodes, 10 evaluators, Fig. 24); Bridge action-conditioned next-frame (Table 23): 7B PSNR 21.14, SSIM 0.82, FVD 190 vs IRASim-Action 19.13, 0.64, 593.
- Autoregressive failure rate (Table 18): "objects appear from below" failure rate with single-frame conditioning 15% (4B), 7% (5B), 2% (12B), 3% (13B); under 2% with 9 frames.
- Driving and camera-control results exist (Sec. 6.1, 6.3) but were not read in detail.

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors (Sec. 5.2.7, 5.3.2, 9):** models "fall short as reliable simulators of the physical world": object permanence failures, inaccurate contact-rich dynamics, inconsistent instruction following, violations of gravity, light and fluid dynamics; "additional steps in data curation where physically implausible videos are removed are required"; evaluation is hard (human rubrics are subjective and may not align with downstream metrics); autoregressive outputs are blurry from tokenizer loss and objects can appear from below; text prompt upsampling does not help the autoregressive models; Sec. 2.1 lists uses (policy evaluation, training, planning, synthetic data) but "this paper does not include empirical results" for them.
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** no closed-loop control or policy-learning evidence appears in this paper; the robotics evaluations are open-loop video prediction metrics and a 23-episode human study. Later work (e.g. `vjepa2_2025`) used a Cosmos model as a robot-planning baseline and reported poor manipulation and 4 minutes per action in its configuration (that paper's setup, not this paper's claim).

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `dit2022` (DiT backbone), `ddpm2020` and `scoresde2020` / EDM (diffusion training), `ldm2021` (latent diffusion), `rope2021`, `transformer2017`, `cfg2022`; world-model context per its Related Work (Sec. 8): `worldmodels2018`, `genie2024` and `diamond2024` (both cited as autoregressive and diffusion world models respectively), Dreamer-line models.
- **Led to:** Cosmos-based action-conditioned simulators and the Cosmos-Predict/Transfer/Reason line (hindsight); used as a baseline in `vjepa2_2025` (stated in that paper).
- **World-model vs generic video generation:** most of the paper (data curation, tokenizers, base model quality, 3D consistency) is general video generation engineering. The world-model-specific parts are the formal definition $\mathcal W(x_{0:t},c_t)\to\hat x_{t+1}$, the physics-alignment benchmark, and action- and instruction-conditioned post-training. The paper's own physics evaluation does not show that the pretrained models respect physics.
- Single idea added: package a general video foundation model (curated data, causal tokenizers, diffusion and autoregressive transformers) as a pretrain-then-post-train "world foundation model" for Physical AI.
