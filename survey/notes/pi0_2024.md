## 1. Problem and motivation
Robot learning needs generality, dexterity and robustness, but earlier robot foundation models mostly handled simple pick-and-place and relied on autoregressive discretised action tokens (RT-2, OpenVLA), which the paper says pose "a major challenge" for high-frequency, highly dexterous tasks (Sec. I). Dexterous tasks had been studied at small scale, with tens or hundreds of trajectories, roughly 10 hours or less. The authors argue that three things are missing: very large scale, an architecture that can use diverse data while representing precise continuous behaviours, and the right training recipe (Sec. I).

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Start from a pre-trained 3B vision-language model (PaliGemma), and add a small "action expert" (about 300M parameters of separate transformer weights) that generates a whole chunk of 50 future continuous actions at once by flow matching, a diffusion-like method that starts from noise and follows a learned vector field to an action chunk. The VLM part reads images and language; the action expert reads robot state and noisy actions; they interact only through attention. Train in two phases, like LLMs: pre-train on about 10,000 hours of diverse multi-robot data, then post-train on a small curated set for each hard task. Analogy: the VLM is the brain that understands the scene and the instruction, and the action expert is a fast, fine-motor cerebellum that sketches a smooth 1-second movement from noise.

## 3. The mathematics
**Modelling target (Sec. IV).** Model $p(A_t\mid o_t)$ where $A_t=[a_t,a_{t+1},\dots,a_{t+H-1}]$ is an action chunk ($H=50$) and $o_t=[I_t^1,\dots,I_t^n,\ell_t,q_t]$ is the observation (2 or 3 RGB images, language tokens, joint angles).

**Flow-matching loss (Sec. IV):**
$$L^{\tau}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{p(A_t|o_t),\,q(A_t^\tau|A_t)}\big\|v_\theta(A_t^\tau,o_t)-u(A_t^\tau|A_t)\big\|^2$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $t$ (subscript) | robot timestep; $\tau\in[0,1]$ (superscript) is the flow-matching time |
| $A_t^\tau$ | noisy action chunk at flow time $\tau$ |
| $q(A_t^\tau|A_t)=\mathcal{N}(\tau A_t,(1-\tau)I)$ | linear-Gaussian (optimal-transport) probability path |
| $v_\theta$ | network (the action expert) predicting a vector field |
| $u(A_t^\tau|A_t)=A_t-\epsilon$ | target denoising vector field |

In practice: sample noise $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, form $A_t^\tau=\tau A_t+(1-\tau)\epsilon$, and regress $v_\theta(A_t^\tau,o_t)$ onto $A_t-\epsilon$. Why this works: along the straight path from noise ($\tau=0$) to data ($\tau=1$) the velocity is the constant $A_t-\epsilon$, so a network trained to predict it can be integrated to turn noise into a sample. Note the paper's time convention: noise at $\tau=0$, data at $\tau=1$.

**Timestep sampling (Appendix B):** $p(\tau)=\mathrm{Beta}\big(\tfrac{s-\tau}{s};1.5,1\big)$ with $s=0.999$, emphasising low $\tau$ (noisy actions) and omitting $\tau>s$. The authors argue predicting the mean action from an informative observation is a harder problem than predicting the mean image from a text label, hence a different distribution from the logit-normal used for image synthesis.

**Inference (Sec. IV):** start from $A_t^0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and apply forward Euler,
$$A_t^{\tau+\delta}=A_t^{\tau}+\delta\,v_\theta(A_t^{\tau},o_t),\qquad \delta=0.1\ (10\text{ steps})$$
with the keys/values of the observation prefix cached, so only the action tokens are recomputed at each step.

**Attention structure:** a blockwise causal mask over three blocks (images+language, robot state, noisy actions); within the action block attention is fully bidirectional (Sec. IV, Appendix B).

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Backbone: PaliGemma (3B; Gemma 2B LM: width 2048, depth 18, MLP 16,384) plus an action expert with width 1024, MLP 4096 (about 300M parameters, initialised from scratch); total 3.3B parameters (Sec. IV, App. B). Tokens are routed to one of two sets of weights (a two-expert mixture-of-experts style), interacting only via self-attention.
2. Inputs and outputs: images and state are linearly projected to token space; each action in the chunk is one action token through the action expert. State and action vectors are padded to the largest robot's dimension (18) and missing camera slots masked (Sec. V-A).
3. Pre-training mixture: 903M timesteps of own data (106M single-arm, 797M dual-arm) on 7 robot configurations and 68 tasks, plus 9.1% open-source data (OXE, Bridge v2, DROID); each task-robot combination is weighted by $n^{0.43}$ (Sec. V-A). About 10,000 hours total; 700k training steps for the main model (Sec. VI-A).
4. Language: labels combine task names and roughly 2-second segment annotations; a high-level VLM can decompose tasks into subtask commands (Sec. V-B).
5. Post-training: fine-tune on 5 to 100+ hours of curated data per task (Sec. V-A).
6. Inference: 73 ms on-board (14 ms image encoders, 32 ms observation pass, 27 ms for 10 flow steps) on an RTX 4090 with 3 images; chunks of 16 (20 Hz robots) or 25 (50 Hz robots) actions are executed open-loop between inferences; temporal ensembling hurt and was dropped (App. D).

## 5. Achievements
- Out-of-box (Sec. VI-A, Fig. 7): on shirt folding, easy and hard bussing, grocery bagging and toast removal, π0 beats OpenVLA and Octo (both trained on the full mixture), π0-small, and even a compute-parity 160k-step π0, "by a large margin", with near-perfect success on shirt folding and easy bussing. Metrics are normalised task-progress scores over 10 episodes (bar charts only, no numbers in text).
- Language (Sec. VI-B, Fig. 9): π0 follows language commands much better than the non-VLM π0-small, and this translates to gains with human and high-level-VLM guidance.
- New dexterous tasks (Sec. VI-C, Fig. 11): fine-tuned π0 generally outperforms OpenVLA, Octo, ACT and Diffusion Policy; pre-training helps, sometimes about 2x over training from scratch.
- Complex multi-stage tasks (Sec. VI-D, Fig. 13): laundry folding (static and mobile), dryer unloading, table bussing, box building, to-go box, egg packing, lasting 5-20 minutes; the full pre-train plus fine-tune recipe scores best and exceeds 50% of the maximum score on all tasks, solving tasks the authors "were not able to solve with other methods".
- Claims the largest robot pre-training mixture used for a manipulation model and the first flow-matching VLA producing high-frequency action chunks (up to 50 Hz).

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors (Sec. VII):** no comprehensive understanding of how to compose or weight the pre-training data; not all evaluation tasks work reliably, and it is unclear how much data is needed for near-perfect performance; unknown how much positive transfer comes from mixing very diverse data, and whether universality extends to domains such as driving, navigation or legged locomotion. The π0 vs π0-small language comparison is confounded by model size (the authors note it is hard to remove).
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** The pre-training dataset is proprietary and the main model was not open-sourced in the paper (open release came later), limiting reproduction. Results are in bar charts with 10 trials per task, so uncertainty is hard to judge. Open-loop chunk execution limits reactivity. The baselines (OpenVLA, Octo) were trained with a reduced schedule and not designed for chunking or this mixture, which the authors acknowledge (Sec. VI-A).

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `rt2_2023` and `openvla2024` (VLA idea; OpenVLA is a baseline), `flowmatching2022` and `rectflow2022` (flow matching), `ddpm2020` / `diffpolicy2023` (diffusion for actions, Diffusion Policy baseline), `dit2022` (the non-VLM baseline's action expert uses DiT with AdaLN-Zero), `sd3_2024` (rectified-flow timestep sampling contrasted in App. B), `vit2020`, `transformer2017`, PaliGemma/Gemma, ACT and action chunking, Transfusion.
- **Led to:** later flow/diffusion-head VLAs and robot foundation models (hindsight), e.g. successors from the same group.
- **Single idea added:** pair a pre-trained VLM with a separate flow-matching action expert that outputs continuous action chunks, trained with an LLM-style pre-train/post-train recipe on 10k+ hours of cross-embodiment data.
