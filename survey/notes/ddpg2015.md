# Continuous control with deep reinforcement learning
**Lillicrap, Hunt, Pritzel, Heess, Erez, Tassa, Silver, Wierstra (Google DeepMind), arXiv:1509.02971 (v6 text); published at ICLR 2016** · **Key:** `ddpg2015` · **OpenAlex citations:** 18036 · **Era:** rl
**Read status:** full text (main body, Table 1, supplementary experiment details; environment descriptions skimmed).

## 1. Problem and motivation
DQN solved high-dimensional observation spaces but only for discrete, low-dimensional actions, because it needs $\arg\max_a Q(s,a)$ at every step, which in continuous spaces is an optimisation per step (Sec. 1). Discretising actions blows up exponentially (a 7-DoF arm with 3 values per joint gives $3^7=2187$ actions) and discards action structure. The existing deterministic policy gradient (DPG) with neural networks was reported to be unstable on hard problems (Sec. 1).

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Keep an actor network that maps a state directly to a continuous action, and a critic network that scores state-action pairs. Train the critic with Q-learning-style Bellman targets, and train the actor by pushing its output uphill on the critic's value (chain rule through the critic). To make this stable with deep networks, borrow DQN's two tricks, a replay buffer and target networks, but update the target networks softly (slow exponential tracking), and add batch normalisation so one set of hyperparameters works across tasks with different units and scales. Exploration is handled separately by adding temporally correlated noise to the actor's actions.

## 3. The mathematics
**Bellman equation for a deterministic policy (Eq. 3):**
$$Q^\mu(s_t,a_t)=\mathbb{E}_{r_t,s_{t+1}\sim E}\big[r(s_t,a_t)+\gamma Q^\mu(s_{t+1},\mu(s_{t+1}))\big]$$
$\mu:\mathcal S\to\mathcal A$ deterministic policy, $E$ environment, $\gamma\in[0,1]$ discount. Since the inner expectation over the next action vanishes, $Q^\mu$ can be learned off-policy from data generated by another behaviour policy $\beta$.

**Critic loss (Eqs. 4-5):**
$$L(\theta^Q)=\mathbb{E}_{s_t\sim\rho^\beta,a_t\sim\beta,r_t\sim E}\big[(Q(s_t,a_t\mid\theta^Q)-y_t)^2\big],\qquad y_t=r(s_t,a_t)+\gamma Q(s_{t+1},\mu(s_{t+1})\mid\theta^Q)$$
$\theta^Q$ critic weights, $\rho^\beta$ state distribution under $\beta$, $y_t$ target. The dependence of $y_t$ on $\theta^Q$ is ignored when differentiating (the paper says so). In Algorithm 1 the target uses the target networks: $y_i=r_i+\gamma Q'(s_{i+1},\mu'(s_{i+1}\mid\theta^{\mu'})\mid\theta^{Q'})$.

**Actor update, deterministic policy gradient (Eq. 6):**
$$\nabla_{\theta^\mu}J\approx\mathbb{E}_{s_t\sim\rho^\beta}\big[\nabla_aQ(s,a\mid\theta^Q)\big|_{s=s_t,a=\mu(s_t)}\;\nabla_{\theta^\mu}\mu(s\mid\theta^\mu)\big|_{s=s_t}\big]$$
$J$ expected return from the start distribution, $\theta^\mu$ actor weights, $\nabla_aQ$ gradient of the critic with respect to the action. Intuition: move the action in the direction that increases $Q$, then move the actor weights so the actor produces that action. Silver et al. (2014) proved this is the policy gradient (the paper ignores the discount in the state-visitation distribution in practice).

**Soft target update (Sec. 3):** $\theta'\leftarrow\tau\theta+(1-\tau)\theta'$ with $\tau\ll1$, applied to both actor and critic targets. Targets change slowly, making critic learning closer to supervised regression.

**Exploration policy (Eq. 7):** $\mu'(s_t)=\mu(s_t\mid\theta^\mu_t)+\mathcal N$, with $\mathcal N$ an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process ($\theta=0.15$, $\sigma=0.2$, supplementary Sec. 7).

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
Algorithm 1: initialise critic $Q$, actor $\mu$ and targets $Q',\mu'$; replay buffer $R$. Each step: act with $\mu(s_t)+\mathcal N_t$; store $(s_t,a_t,r_t,s_{t+1})$; sample a minibatch; compute $y_i$ from target nets; minimise the critic MSE; apply the sampled policy gradient to the actor; soft-update targets.
Details (supplementary Sec. 7): Adam, learning rates $10^{-4}$ (actor) and $10^{-3}$ (critic); critic L2 weight decay $10^{-2}$; $\gamma=0.99$; $\tau=0.001$; ReLU hidden layers and tanh actor output; low-dimensional nets with two hidden layers of 400 and 300 units (about 130,000 parameters), actions enter Q at the second hidden layer; pixel nets with 3 conv layers (32 filters, no pooling) then two FC layers of 200 units (about 430,000 parameters); minibatch 64 (low-d) or 16 (pixels); replay size $10^6$. Batch normalisation on the state input and all layers of $\mu$ and the pre-action layers of $Q$. Pixels: 64x64 frames, action repeat of 3, giving 9 feature maps.

## 5. Achievements
- One algorithm, architecture and hyperparameter set solves more than 20 simulated MuJoCo tasks (cartpole swing-up, gripper, locomotion, monoped balancing, Torcs driving) from low-dimensional state, and many from pixels (Abstract, Sec. 4, Table 1, results over 5 replicas, at most 2.5 million steps).
- Scores normalised so random = 0 and the iLQG planner (full model access) = 1. Example average / best low-d: cartpole 0.844/1.115, pendulum 0.946/1.021, cheetah 0.903/1.206, hardCheetah 1.311/1.990; from pixels hardCheetah 1.204/1.431 and blockworld3da 0.889/2.225. Some replicas exceed the planner, even from pixels.
- Ablation (Fig. 2): target networks are crucial; the original DPG with replay and batch norm ("cntrl" in Table 1) is much worse on average (e.g. cheetah -0.008, cartpoleBalance -0.468).
- Solved within 2.5 million steps, which the authors say is about a factor of 20 fewer than DQN needs on Atari (Sec. 6).
- Fig. 3: Q estimates track true returns well on simple tasks (pendulum, cartpole), worse on harder ones.

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors:** no convergence guarantees with non-linear approximators; as with most model-free methods, DDPG needs a large number of training episodes (Sec. 6); Q estimates are inaccurate in harder tasks (Fig. 3); in Torcs some replicas failed to learn a sensible policy (Sec. 4).
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** high sensitivity to hyperparameters and random seeds; value overestimation from the single critic (later addressed by TD3 with twin critics and target smoothing); deterministic policy plus hand-set noise limits exploration; evaluated only in simulation on 5 replicas; later `sac2018` offers a more stable maximum-entropy alternative.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `dqn2013` (replay and target networks), deterministic policy gradient (Silver et al. 2014), NFQCA, `batchnorm2015`, Adam (`adam2014`).
- **Led to (hindsight):** `sac2018`, TD3, D4PG, and off-policy model-based methods such as `mbpo2019`; the actor-critic template for continuous robotic control.
- **One idea:** make DQN-style off-policy learning work for continuous actions by learning a deterministic actor through the critic's action-gradient, stabilised with soft target networks.
