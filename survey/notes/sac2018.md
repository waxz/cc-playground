# Soft Actor-Critic: Off-Policy Maximum Entropy Deep Reinforcement Learning with a Stochastic Actor
**Haarnoja, Zhou, Abbeel, Levine (UC Berkeley), arXiv:1801.01290v2 (ICML 2018), 2018** · **Key:** `sac2018` · **OpenAlex citations:** 13864 · **Era:** rl
**Read status:** full text (two-column layout was interleaved by extraction but equations, tables and text were readable; proofs in Appendix B not read in detail)

## 1. Problem and motivation
Model-free deep RL suffers from (i) high sample complexity, partly because on-policy methods (TRPO, PPO, A3C) need fresh samples for every gradient step, and (ii) brittleness to hyperparameters. The off-policy alternative DDPG is sample efficient but "extremely brittle" and hyperparameter sensitive (Sec. 1). Earlier maximum-entropy methods were either on-policy (poor sample complexity) or off-policy but needed complex approximate inference in continuous actions (soft Q-learning).

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Train a stochastic actor and a critic off-policy from a replay buffer, but change the objective: instead of only maximizing reward, also maximize the policy's entropy, "to succeed at the task while acting as randomly as possible". The critic evaluates the soft value (reward plus entropy), and the actor is pushed toward a Boltzmann distribution over the soft Q-values. Entropy keeps exploration alive and stops the policy from collapsing early; the off-policy replay gives sample efficiency. The analogy: a player who keeps all equally good options open rather than committing to one rigid line.

## 3. The mathematics
**(a) Maximum entropy objective (Eq. 1).**
$$J(\pi)=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim\rho_\pi}\big[r(s_t,a_t)+\alpha\,\mathcal H(\pi(\cdot|s_t))\big]$$
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\rho_\pi$ | state-action marginal of the trajectory distribution induced by $\pi$ |
| $r$ | bounded reward |
| $\mathcal H$ | entropy of the policy at $s_t$ |
| $\alpha$ | temperature trading off entropy vs reward |

The standard objective is recovered as $\alpha\to0$. The paper then omits $\alpha$ by absorbing it into the reward scale (reward scaled by $\alpha^{-1}$); an infinite-horizon discounted form is in Appendix A.

**(b) Soft Bellman backup and soft value (Eq. 2-3).**
$$\mathcal T^\pi Q(s_t,a_t)\triangleq r(s_t,a_t)+\gamma\,\mathbb E_{s_{t+1}\sim p}[V(s_{t+1})],\qquad V(s_t)=\mathbb E_{a_t\sim\pi}[Q(s_t,a_t)-\log\pi(a_t|s_t)]$$
$\gamma$ is the discount, $p$ the transition density. The $-\log\pi$ term inside $V$ is the entropy bonus. Lemma 1: repeated application of $\mathcal T^\pi$ converges to the soft Q-value of $\pi$ (tabular, $|\mathcal A|<\infty$).

**(c) Soft policy improvement (Eq. 4).**
$$\pi_{new}=\arg\min_{\pi'\in\Pi}D_{KL}\Big(\pi'(\cdot|s_t)\ \Big\|\ \frac{\exp(Q^{\pi_{old}}(s_t,\cdot))}{Z^{\pi_{old}}(s_t)}\Big)$$
$\Pi$ is a tractable policy family (e.g. Gaussians), $Z$ a normalizer that does not affect gradients. Lemma 2 shows $Q^{\pi_{new}}\ge Q^{\pi_{old}}$, and Theorem 1 shows soft policy iteration converges to the best policy in $\Pi$ (tabular, finite actions).

**(d) Practical losses (Eq. 5, 7, 8, 12).**
$$J_V(\psi)=\mathbb E_{s_t\sim\mathcal D}\Big[\tfrac12\big(V_\psi(s_t)-\mathbb E_{a_t\sim\pi_\phi}[Q_\theta(s_t,a_t)-\log\pi_\phi(a_t|s_t)]\big)^2\Big]$$
$$J_Q(\theta)=\mathbb E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim\mathcal D}\Big[\tfrac12\big(Q_\theta(s_t,a_t)-\hat Q(s_t,a_t)\big)^2\Big],\quad \hat Q=r(s_t,a_t)+\gamma\,\mathbb E_{s_{t+1}}[V_{\bar\psi}(s_{t+1})]$$
$$J_\pi(\phi)=\mathbb E_{s_t\sim\mathcal D,\epsilon_t\sim\mathcal N}\big[\log\pi_\phi(f_\phi(\epsilon_t;s_t)|s_t)-Q_\theta(s_t,f_\phi(\epsilon_t;s_t))\big]$$
$\psi,\theta,\phi$ are parameters of the value, soft-Q and policy networks; $\bar\psi$ is a slowly moving target value network; $\mathcal D$ is the replay buffer; $a_t=f_\phi(\epsilon_t;s_t)$ (Eq. 11) is the reparameterized action with input noise $\epsilon_t$. Using the reparameterization trick gives a lower-variance gradient than the likelihood-ratio estimator and extends DDPG-style policy gradients to any tractable stochastic policy. Two Q-functions are trained and the minimum is used in the value and policy gradients to reduce positive bias (following TD3, Sec. 4.2).

## 4. Method / algorithm
Algorithm 1: each iteration, (1) sample $a_t\sim\pi_\phi$, step the environment, append the transition to $\mathcal D$; (2) for each gradient step, update $\psi$ (value), $\theta_1,\theta_2$ (two soft Q-functions), $\phi$ (policy), then $\bar\psi\leftarrow\tau\psi+(1-\tau)\bar\psi$.
Hyperparameters (App. D, Table 1): Adam, lr $3\times10^{-4}$, $\gamma=0.99$, replay size $10^6$, 2 hidden layers of 256 ReLU units, minibatch 256, $\tau=0.005$, one gradient step per environment step. Actions are a Gaussian passed through tanh (App. C). Reward scale per environment (Table 2): 5 for Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant; 20 for Humanoid-v1; 10 for Humanoid (rllab). Evaluation uses the mean action.

## 5. Achievements
- Fig. 1 (five seeds, six MuJoCo tasks incl. Humanoid-v1 and the 21-dim Humanoid (rllab)): SAC is comparable to baselines on easy tasks and outperforms DDPG, PPO, SQL and concurrent TD3 on harder ones "by a large margin" in speed and final performance. DDPG fails to progress on Ant-v1, Humanoid-v1 and Humanoid (rllab) (Sec. 5.1).
- Ablation (Fig. 2): the deterministic variant has far higher variability across seeds on Humanoid (rllab), so stochasticity and entropy stabilize training. Evaluating with the mean action usually gives higher returns (Fig. 3a).
- Sensitivity (Fig. 3): reward scale is "the only hyperparameter that requires tuning"; $\tau$ in a wide range works and $\tau=0.005$ used on all tasks.
- First off-policy actor-critic in the maximum entropy framework, with a convergence proof for soft policy iteration.

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors:** performance is sensitive to reward scale, which "should be tuned for each task separately" (Fig. 3b, Table 2); theory is for the tabular, finite-action case ($|\mathcal A|<\infty$) and the practical algorithm is an approximation; the authors list second-order (trust region) and more expressive policy classes as future work (Sec. 6). Training curves report sum of rewards, not the entropy-augmented objective SAC optimizes.
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** the per-task reward scale is effectively a hand-tuned temperature (later fixed by learning $\alpha$ automatically in a follow-up version); this paper's version uses a separate value network that later became unnecessary; evaluation limited to low-dimensional MuJoCo proprioception, no pixel observations; Gaussian policies cannot represent strongly multimodal behavior.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `ddpg2015` (off-policy actor-critic), `dqn2013` (target networks, replay), `trpo2015` and `ppo2017` (as on-policy baselines), soft Q-learning and the maximum entropy framework (Ziebart et al.; Haarnoja et al. 2017), TD3 clipped double-Q trick (Fujimoto et al. 2018, concurrent).
- **Led to:** `mbpo2019` (uses SAC as its policy optimizer on model-generated data); (hindsight) became a default off-policy algorithm in robotics and continuous control, and is used inside later model-based and world-model pipelines.
- **Single idea added:** stable off-policy actor-critic by maximizing reward plus policy entropy with a stochastic reparameterized actor.
