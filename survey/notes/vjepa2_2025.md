# V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning
**Authors, venue, year:** Assran, Bardes, Fan, Garrido, Howes, Komeili, Muckley, Rizvi, Roberts, Sinha, Zholus, Arnaud, Gejji, Martin, Hogan, Dugas, Bojanowski, Khalidov, Labatut, Massa, Szafraniec, Krishnakumar, Li, Ma, Chandar, Meier, LeCun, Rabbat, Ballas (FAIR at Meta, Mila); arXiv:2506.09985v1, 11 June 2025 (no venue stated in the text) · **Key:** `vjepa2_2025` · **OpenAlex citations:** 1360 · **Era:** wm

## 1. Problem and motivation
Learning world models from robot interaction data is limited by how little such data exists. Action-conditioned video generators trained on web video have shown only limited robot-control results, and papers emphasise visual fidelity over planning, partly because planning by generating video is expensive (Sec. 1). V-JEPA 2 asks whether latent prediction pretrained on over 1 million hours of unlabelled web video, plus a small amount of robot data, can give a model that understands, predicts and plans.

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Two stages. Stage 1 (V-JEPA 2): an action-free video encoder is pretrained by masked prediction in representation space (the I-JEPA recipe applied to video, scaled up). Stage 2 (V-JEPA 2-AC): freeze that encoder and train a new 300M-parameter predictor that, given the current frame features, the robot's end-effector state and a candidate action, predicts the next frame's features. To act, give the robot a goal image, encode it, and search (cross-entropy method) for the action sequence whose predicted future features are closest to the goal features, executing the first action and repeating (model-predictive control). The "world model" lives entirely in feature space, so nothing is rendered.

## 3. The mathematics
**Eq. (1), pretraining objective (Sec. 2.1).**
$$\min_{\theta,\phi,\Delta_y}\ \big\lVert P_\phi(\Delta_y,E_\theta(x))-\mathrm{sg}(E_{\bar\theta}(y))\big\rVert_1$$
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $E_\theta$ | video encoder (ViT) |
| $E_{\bar\theta}$ | EMA copy of the encoder (the text writes an overbar on $\theta$; the bar is lost in extraction) |
| $P_\phi$ | predictor (ViT, similar to ViT-small) |
| $x$ | masked view of the clip; $y$ the full clip |
| $\Delta_y$ | learnable mask token marking dropped patches |
| $\mathrm{sg}$ | stop-gradient |
Meaning: predict the target encoder's features of masked tubelets (2x16x16) from visible ones; loss only on masked patches; EMA plus stop-gradient is what the authors say prevents collapse. It differs from I-JEPA (`ijepa2023`) by using an $L_1$ rather than $L_2$ loss and by operating on video with 3D-RoPE.

**Eq. (2)-(4), action-conditioned training (Sec. 3.1).**
$$\mathcal L_{\text{teacher-forcing}}(\phi)=\frac1T\sum_{k=1}^{T}\lVert \hat z_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\rVert_1,\quad \mathcal L_{\text{rollout}}(\phi)=\lVert P_\phi(a_{1:T},s_1,z_1)-z_{T+1}\rVert_1,\quad \mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\text{tf}}+\mathcal L_{\text{rollout}}$$
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $z_k=E(x_k)$ | frozen-encoder feature map of frame $k$ (16x16x1408 for ViT-g) |
| $s_k$ | 7-D end-effector state (3 position, 3 Euler angles, 1 gripper) |
| $a_k$ | 7-D action = change in end-effector state between frames $k$ and $k+1$ |
| $\hat z_{k+1}$ | predictor's output given $(a,s,z)$ up to $k$ (block-causal attention) |
| $T$ | 15 for teacher forcing; 2 for the rollout loss (gradient through one recurrent step) |
Meaning: next-feature prediction conditioned on action, plus a short rollout term to reduce error accumulation at inference.

**Eq. (5), planning energy (Sec. 3.2).**
$$\mathcal E(\hat a_{1:T};z_k,s_k,z_g)=\lVert P(\hat a_{1:T};s_k,z_k)-z_g\rVert_1,\qquad a^\star=\arg\min_{\hat a_{1:T}}\mathcal E$$
$z_g$ is the encoded goal image, $z_k$ the encoded current frame; the minimisation uses the cross-entropy method (sample Gaussian action sequences, refit to the top-$k$, iterate); the first action is executed, then re-plan. Intuition: the best action sequence is the one whose imagined end state looks (in feature space) most like the goal.

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Data: VideoMix22M (22M samples: SSv2, Kinetics 400/600/700, HowTo100M, curated YT-Temporal-1B, ImageNet images repeated as 16-frame clips) (Sec. 2.3, Table 1); abstract says over 1M hours.
2. Scaling ingredients (Sec. 2.1-2.4): data 2M to 22M videos; encoder ViT-L (300M) to ViT-g (1B+); training 90K to 252K iterations with warmup-constant-decay; progressive resolution (16 frames at 256x256 up to 64 frames at 384x384 in a 12K-iteration cooldown). Reported effects: data +1.0, curation +1.4, model +1.5, longer training +0.8 average points; cumulative +4.0 over the ViT-L/16 baseline.
3. V-JEPA 2-AC: about 62 hours of Droid video (4 s clips, 4 fps, 256x256), only video and end-effector states (no rewards, task labels or success flags). Predictor: about 300M parameters, 24 layers, 16 heads, hidden dim 1024.
4. Deployment: zero-shot on Franka arms in two labs not in Droid, monocular uncalibrated RGB camera; actions constrained to an $L_1$ ball of radius 0.075 (about 13 cm per step); pick-and-place uses three image sub-goals (4, 10 and 4 steps).
5. Understanding / VidQA: attentive probes on the frozen encoder; an MLLM trained with the encoder in LLaVA-style early fusion on 88.5M image/video-text pairs (Sec. 7).

## 5. Achievements
- Probe classification (Table 4): ViT-g 87.5 average over six tasks; ViT-g384 88.2; SSv2 75.3 (ViT-g) and 77.3 (ViT-g384); ImageNet 84.6. Strongest on motion tasks, "competitive" on appearance: it trails PEcore G on K400/COIN/ImageNet (e.g. COIN 90.7 vs 95.3). Baselines are trained on different data and are "not directly comparable" (authors).
- Action anticipation, Epic-Kitchens-100 (Table 5): ViT-g384 action recall@5 39.7, versus 27.6 for PlausiVL (8B): the paper states +12.1 points, 44% relative; frozen backbone plus attentive probe on encoder and predictor outputs.
- VidQA (abstract): 84.0 PerceptionTest, 76.9 TempCompass, 44.5 MVP, 36.7 TemporalBench, 40.3 TOMATO, state of the art at the 8B scale; they claim to be the first to use a video encoder pretrained without language supervision for this (Sec. 7).
- Robot planning (Table 2, 10 trials per cell): V-JEPA 2-AC averaged over two labs: Reach 100%, Grasp cup 65% / box 25%, Reach-with-object 75% / 75%, Pick-and-place cup 80% / box 65%. Octo (behaviour-cloning baseline) averaged: 100%, 15%, 0%, 15%, 70%, 15%, 10%.
- Versus Cosmos world model (Table 3, Lab 2): Cosmos 4 min per action (80 samples), reach 80%, grasp 0%/20%, pick-and-place 0%/0%; V-JEPA 2-AC 16 s per action (800 samples), reach 100%, grasp 60%/20%, pick-and-place 80%/50%.
- Single-goal reach: end effector within 4 cm of the goal with monotonically decreasing error (Fig. 8); energy landscape smooth and locally convex (Fig. 9).

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors:** sensitivity to camera position (the model must infer the action axes implicitly; they hand-tuned camera placement, Sec. 4.3); error accumulation and exponential action-search growth limit long-horizon planning, so pick-and-place needs image sub-goals; goals must be images, not language; scaling stopped at about 1B parameters; EK100 limits (1 s anticipation, kitchens only, closed vocabulary; accuracy degrades at longer horizons, Sec. 6). Future work includes hierarchical models.
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** manipulation results are 10 trials per cell and grasp success is modest for the box (25%); planning needs a goal image and an unspecified number of CEM samples; the Cosmos comparison fine-tuned a pretrained video model with changed settings (lower learning rate, noise factor), which the authors disclose but which makes it a particular configuration rather than a definitive comparison. The planning stage uses actions and proprioception, so only stage 1 is action-free.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `ijepa2023` (masked latent prediction), V-JEPA (Bardes et al. 2024), `vit2020`, `rope2021` (3D-RoPE); LeCun's JEPA proposal; Droid data.
- **Led to:** later JEPA-style latent world models and planning work (hindsight).
- **World-model vs general representation learning:** Sections 2, 5, 6 and 7 (pretraining, probes, EK100 anticipation, VidQA) are general video representation learning. The world-model claim rests on Sections 3-4: an action-conditioned latent predictor and zero-shot robot planning, trained on 62 hours of robot video. The anticipation probe uses the pretrained predictor but is a classification task, not dynamics modelling with actions.
- Single idea added: scale JEPA video pretraining, then bolt a small action-conditioned latent predictor onto the frozen encoder and plan by feature-space goal matching.
