# BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding
**Devlin, Chang, Lee, Toutanova (Google AI Language); arXiv:1810.04805v2, 2019 (NAACL 2019 version)** · **Key:** `bert2018` · **OpenAlex citations:** 83883 · **Era:** tf
**Read status:** full text (main body and Appendix A, C; Appendix B GLUE details only skimmed)

## 1. Problem and motivation
Pre-trained language representations helped many NLP tasks, but the two dominant recipes were limited to one direction of context. OpenAI GPT used a left-to-right Transformer (each token attends only to the left), and ELMo used a shallow concatenation of independently trained left-to-right and right-to-left LSTMs (Sec. 1, Fig. 3). The authors argue this unidirectionality is "sub-optimal for sentence-level tasks, and could be very harmful" for token-level tasks like question answering, where both sides matter.

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Train a Transformer encoder so that every layer sees both left and right context. A normal language model cannot do this because each word would trivially "see itself" through the stack. BERT sidesteps this with a fill-in-the-blank (Cloze) game: hide 15% of the tokens and predict them from the rest. A second auxiliary task asks whether sentence B really follows sentence A. After pre-training once on unlabeled text, the same network is fine-tuned end-to-end with one small output layer per task. Analogy: instead of predicting the next word, a student reads a passage with blanks and fills them in using the whole page.

## 3. The mathematics
The paper has few numbered equations; these are the defining formulas stated in the text.

**(a) Masked-LM objective (described in Sec. 3.1; the loss is stated in words, so this form is simplified):**
$$\mathcal{L}_{MLM}=-\frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{i\in M}\log p\big(x_i\mid T_i\big),\quad p(\cdot\mid T_i)=\mathrm{softmax}(\cdot)\ \text{over the vocabulary}$$
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $M$ | the 15% of token positions chosen for prediction |
| $T_i\in\mathbb{R}^H$ | final hidden vector of token $i$ |
| $x_i$ | original token id |

Of the chosen positions: 80% are replaced by [MASK], 10% by a random token, 10% left unchanged (Sec. 3.1). The mix reduces the pre-train/fine-tune mismatch since [MASK] never appears at fine-tuning. Pre-training loss is the sum of mean MLM likelihood and mean NSP likelihood (App. A.2).

**(b) Classification fine-tuning loss (Sec. 4.1):** $\log(\mathrm{softmax}(CW^{T}))$, where $C\in\mathbb{R}^H$ is the final hidden vector of [CLS], $W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times H}$ is the new classifier layer, $K$ is the number of labels. (The paper writes the log-softmax; training minimizes its negative cross-entropy.) The only new parameters are $W$.

**(c) SQuAD span prediction (Sec. 4.2):**
$$P_i=\frac{e^{S\cdot T_i}}{\sum_j e^{S\cdot T_j}},\qquad \text{score}(i,j)=S\cdot T_i+E\cdot T_j,\ j\ge i$$
$S,E\in\mathbb{R}^H$ are learned start and end vectors; $T_i$ token states. An analogous softmax gives the end probability. Training maximizes log-likelihood of the true start and end. For SQuAD 2.0, no-answer is scored as $s_{null}=S\cdot C+E\cdot C$ and predicted when $\hat s_{i,j}>s_{null}+\tau$, with $\tau$ tuned on dev.

**(d) Input representation (Sec. 3, Fig. 2):** the embedding of each token is the sum of token, segment (A/B), and position embeddings.

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Architecture: multi-layer bidirectional Transformer encoder (as in `transformer2017`). BERT-BASE: L=12, H=768, A=12, 110M params; BERT-LARGE: L=24, H=1024, A=16, 340M params; feed-forward size 4H (Sec. 3, fn. 3).
2. Input: WordPiece, 30,000 vocabulary, [CLS] first, [SEP] between segments.
3. Pre-training data: BooksCorpus (800M words) + English Wikipedia (2,500M words) (Sec. 3.1).
4. Pre-training: batch 256 sequences, 1,000,000 steps (about 40 epochs of the 3.3B-word corpus), Adam lr 1e-4, warmup 10,000 steps, linear decay, dropout 0.1, GELU; length 128 for 90% of steps then 512 (App. A.2). BASE on 16 TPU chips, LARGE on 64 TPU chips, 4 days each.
5. NSP: 50% true next sentence, 50% random (IsNext/NotNext); the final model gets 97-98% NSP accuracy (fn. 5).
6. Fine-tuning: all parameters updated; batch 16 or 32, lr in {5e-5, 3e-5, 2e-5}, 2-4 epochs (App. A.3). BERT-LARGE sometimes unstable on small data; they used random restarts.

## 5. Achievements
- GLUE test (Table 1): BERT-LARGE average 82.1 vs OpenAI GPT 75.1 and pre-OpenAI SOTA 74.0; MNLI 86.7/85.9; official leaderboard score 80.5 vs GPT 72.8 (Sec. 4.1).
- SQuAD v1.1 test (Table 2): ensemble + TriviaQA 87.4 EM / 93.2 F1; single + TriviaQA 85.1 / 91.8; +1.5 F1 over top ensemble leaderboard entry.
- SQuAD v2.0 test (Table 3): 80.0 EM / 83.1 F1, +5.1 F1 over previous best.
- SWAG (Table 4): BERT-LARGE 86.3 test, +8.3% over GPT.
- CoNLL-2003 NER (Table 7): BERT-LARGE 92.8 test F1 fine-tuned; feature-based (concat last four layers) is 0.3 F1 behind fine-tuning on dev.
- Ablations (Table 5, BASE): removing NSP drops QNLI 88.4 to 84.9 and SQuAD F1 88.5 to 87.9; left-to-right without NSP drops MRPC to 77.5 and SQuAD F1 to 77.8. Size (Table 6): monotonic gains from 3-layer to 24-layer, e.g. MNLI-m 77.9 to 86.6, even on small MRPC (3.6k examples).

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors:** MLM creates a pre-train/fine-tune mismatch since [MASK] never appears downstream (mitigated by the 80/10/10 scheme); MLM converges slightly slower than left-to-right since only 15% of tokens are predicted per batch (App. C.1); BERT-LARGE fine-tuning is unstable on small datasets; fine-tuned per task, with one model per task; the SQuAD systems use TriviaQA data augmentation; [CLS] vector is not a meaningful sentence representation without fine-tuning (fn. 6).
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** Masked tokens are predicted independently, so joint dependencies among masked tokens are ignored. An encoder-only model is poorly suited to open-ended generation. NSP was later found largely unnecessary in follow-up work. Fixed 512-token context limits long documents.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `transformer2017` (encoder stack), OpenAI GPT and ELMo (the two strategies it contrasts), `word2vec2013`-style pretrained embeddings, `layernorm2016`, `adam2014`.
- **Led to:** `gpt3_2020` (contrast: decoder-only few-shot rather than fine-tuning), `scaling2020`, `vit2020` (pre-train then fine-tune on a Transformer), `clip2021`. Many encoder variants followed.
- Single idea added: deep bidirectional pre-training via masked-token prediction, making one pre-trained network fine-tunable for nearly any task with a single added output layer.
