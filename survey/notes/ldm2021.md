# High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models
**Authors, venue, year:** Robin Rombach, Andreas Blattmann, Dominik Lorenz, Patrick Esser, Bjorn Ommer (LMU Munich and IWR Heidelberg; Runway ML); arXiv 2112.10752 (v2, 13 Apr 2022); CVPR 2022 (venue from background knowledge, not stated in the extracted text) · **Key:** `ldm2021` · **OpenAlex citations:** 37803 · **Era:** df
**Read status:** full main text (Sections 1-6) plus the appendix material visible in extraction (tables, Fig. 15 discussion); appendix derivations and hyperparameter tables were only skimmed.

## 1. Problem and motivation
Diffusion models gave state-of-the-art images, but pixel-space training "often consumes hundreds of GPU days" (the paper cites 150-1000 V100 days for ADM) and producing 50k samples takes about 5 days on a single A100 (Sec. 1). Likelihood-based models also waste capacity on imperceptible high-frequency detail. Earlier two-stage methods (VQ-VAE, VQGAN) compressed images aggressively and then modeled the latent autoregressively, which limited fidelity.

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Split learning into two stages. First train an autoencoder once (perceptual loss plus patch-based adversarial loss) that removes imperceptible detail and gives a latent image $z=E(x)$ at spatial downsampling factor $f$ (mild, e.g. 4 or 8). Then train the diffusion model in that latent space, with a standard time-conditional U-Net; the U-Net's 2-D convolutional bias means the latent can stay a spatial grid, so compression need not be extreme. Conditioning (text, layouts, low-resolution images) is added by cross-attention from a domain-specific encoder into the U-Net. Analogy: sketch on a compact thumbnail canvas, and have a trained decoder paint in the fine texture afterwards.

## 3. The mathematics
**(a) Pixel-space diffusion objective (Eq. 1)**
$$L_{DM}=\mathbb E_{x,\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1),t}\big[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|_2^2\big]$$
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $x$, $x_t$ | image and its noised version at step $t$ |
| $\epsilon$ | Gaussian noise that was added |
| $\epsilon_\theta(\cdot,t)$ | time-conditional denoising U-Net |
| $t$ | step, uniform in $\{1..T\}$ |

Meaning: the reweighted variational bound as in `ddpm2020`; a sequence of equally weighted denoising autoencoders.

**(b) Latent diffusion objective (Eq. 2)**
$$L_{LDM}:=\mathbb E_{E(x),\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1),t}\big[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t)\|_2^2\big]$$
Symbols as above, plus $E$ the frozen encoder, $z_t$ the noised latent. Meaning: the identical loss applied to latents $z=E(x)\in\mathbb R^{h\times w\times c}$ with $f=H/h=W/w$; samples are decoded with one pass of $D$. The forward process is fixed, so $z_t$ is cheap to get during training.

**(c) Conditional objective with cross-attention (Eq. 3 and Sec. 3.3)**
$$L_{LDM}:=\mathbb E_{E(x),y,\epsilon,t}\big[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,\tau_\theta(y))\|_2^2\big],\quad \mathrm{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\mathrm{softmax}\!\Big(\tfrac{QK^\top}{\sqrt d}\Big)V$$
with $Q=W_Q^{(i)}\varphi_i(z_t)$, $K=W_K^{(i)}\tau_\theta(y)$, $V=W_V^{(i)}\tau_\theta(y)$.
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $y$ | conditioning input (text, class, layout, ...) |
| $\tau_\theta(y)\in\mathbb R^{M\times d_\tau}$ | domain-specific encoder output (e.g. a transformer for text) |
| $\varphi_i(z_t)$ | flattened intermediate U-Net features at layer $i$ |
| $W_Q,W_K,W_V$ | learnable projections |

Meaning: U-Net feature tokens query the conditioning tokens; $\tau_\theta$ and $\epsilon_\theta$ are optimized jointly. For spatially aligned conditions (super-resolution, semantic maps) the paper instead concatenates the condition to the U-Net input.

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Stage 1 (Sec. 3.1): autoencoder $E,D$ with perceptual + patch-adversarial loss; two regularizers, KL-reg (slight KL penalty toward a standard normal) or VQ-reg (a vector-quantization layer absorbed in the decoder). Downsampling $f=2^m$.
2. Stage 2: time-conditional U-Net diffusion in latent space (Eq. 2), conditioning via cross-attention (Eq. 3) or concatenation.
3. Sampling: DDIM (250 steps for most tables; 100 for the compression study) then decode with $D$; classifier-free guidance (`cfg2022`) for text-to-image and ImageNet.
4. Key choices: $f\in\{4,8\}$ is the sweet spot (Sec. 4.1); rescaling the latent by its component-wise standard deviation affects the signal-to-noise ratio for convolutional sampling beyond 256x256 (Sec. 4.3.2, App. D.1); the text model uses a BERT tokenizer plus a transformer as $\tau_\theta$ on LAION-400M, 1.45B parameters (Sec. 4.3.1).

## 5. Achievements
- Compression trade-off (Sec. 4.1, Figs. 6-7): LDM-4 to LDM-16 balance efficiency and quality; there is a FID gap of 38 between pixel-based LDM-1 and LDM-8 after 2M ImageNet steps.
- Unconditional (Table 1): CelebA-HQ FID 5.11 (new state of the art, beating LSGM 7.22 and UDM 7.16); FFHQ 4.98; LSUN-Churches 4.02; LSUN-Bedrooms 2.95 (ADM 1.90, "close to ADM despite half its parameters and 4x less training resources").
- Class-conditional ImageNet 256x256 (Table 3): LDM-4-G with classifier-free guidance FID 3.60, IS 247.67 with 400M parameters vs ADM-G 4.59 with 608M; LDM-4 without guidance FID 10.56.
- Text-to-image on MS-COCO (Table 2): 1.45B LDM-KL-8 FID 23.31; with guidance (s=1.5) 12.63, comparable to GLIDE (12.24, 6B) and Make-A-Scene (11.84, 4B).
- Inpainting (Table 6-7): at least 2.7x speed-up and at least 1.6x better FID than pixel-based LDM-1; big model with fine-tuning FID 9.39 vs LaMa 12.0-12.31 on Places; user study favors LDM (Table 4).
- Super-resolution 4x ImageNet (Table 5): FID 2.8/4.8 (val/train features) vs SR3 5.2, with 169M parameters vs 625M.
- Convolutional sampling to roughly 1024x1024 (Sec. 4.3.2, Figs. 9, 13).

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors:** sequential sampling is still slower than GANs; the autoencoder's reconstruction ability can bottleneck tasks needing pixel-level precision (super-resolution is "already somewhat limited"); societal risks (deepfakes, training-data leakage, bias, double-edged sword) in Sec. 5. The quality discrepancy of the attention-containing inpainting model at 256 vs 512 resolution was only fixed by fine-tuning (Sec. 4.5).
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** the two-stage design means the generative quality is capped by the autoencoder and its latent scale factor; pixel-level failure modes (small text, fine detail) are tied to the first stage; the open release of such models raised copyright and misuse debates.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `ddpm2020` (objective), `scoresde2020`, `cfg2022` (guidance), `vae2013` (KL-regularized latent), `gan2014` (adversarial/patch first-stage loss), `transformer2017` (cross-attention), VQGAN/VQ-VAE two-stage models, U-Net.
- **Led to:** `dit2022` (replaces the U-Net, reusing this VAE), `sd3_2024`, Stable Diffusion-style systems and latent-space video/world models (`diamond2024`, `cosmos2025`) (hindsight).
- **Single idea added:** run diffusion in the latent space of a perceptual autoencoder with mild compression, plus cross-attention conditioning, making high-resolution diffusion affordable.
