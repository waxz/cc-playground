# Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision
**Authors, venue, year:** Radford, Kim, Hallacy, Ramesh, Goh, Agarwal, Sastry, Askell, Mishkin, Clark, Krueger, Sutskever (OpenAI); arXiv 2103.00020 / ICML 2021 · **Key:** `clip2021` · **OpenAlex citations:** 62735 · **Era:** tf
**Read status:** full text for Sections 1-3, 5-6 (main body, in the two-column extraction); Section 4 (human comparison), Section 7 (broader impacts) and appendices only skimmed or located by search

## 1. Problem and motivation
State-of-the-art vision models were trained to predict a fixed set of crowd-labelled categories (e.g. ImageNet's 1000 classes), so any new concept needed new labelled data (Abstract). In NLP, web-scale pre-training with task-agnostic objectives gave zero-shot transfer (GPT-3), but earlier attempts to learn from image-text pairs performed poorly: Visual N-Grams reached only 11.5% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy (Sec. 1) and the paper's own captioning baseline scaled badly (Sec. 2.3).

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Collect 400 million (image, text) pairs from the internet, and train an image encoder and a text encoder so that, within a batch, each image's embedding is closest to its own caption's embedding and far from every other caption's. Do not try to generate the caption word by word; only decide which caption goes with which image. After training, a new classification task is specified by writing the class names as text ("A photo of a {label}."); the text encoder turns them into the weights of a linear classifier, with no extra training. Analogy: a matching game between photos and captions, played so many times that the player can later match a photo to any description you hand it.

## 3. The mathematics
The paper gives the objective as pseudocode (Fig. 3) rather than a numbered equation; the following is my rendering of it (simplified, standard InfoNCE notation, not the paper's):

$$\mathcal{L}=\tfrac12\Big(\tfrac1N\sum_{i}-\log\frac{e^{\,s_{ii}}}{\sum_j e^{\,s_{ij}}}+\tfrac1N\sum_{j}-\log\frac{e^{\,s_{jj}}}{\sum_i e^{\,s_{ij}}}\Big),\qquad s_{ij}=e^{t}\,\hat I_i\!\cdot\!\hat T_j$$

| Symbol | Meaning (from Fig. 3 / Sec. 2.3) |
|---|---|
| $N$ | batch size (32,768 in training, Sec. 2.5) |
| $\hat I_i=\ell_2(I_f W_i)$, $\hat T_j=\ell_2(T_f W_t)$ | L2-normalised image and text embeddings in the shared space of dimension $d_e$; $W_i,W_t$ are the learned linear projections |
| $t$ | learned log-temperature; logits are multiplied by $e^t$; $\tau$ initialised to the equivalent of 0.07 and clipped so logits are scaled by at most 100 (Sec. 2.5) |
| $s_{ij}$ | scaled cosine similarity of image $i$ and text $j$ |

Meaning: the $N\times N$ similarity matrix gets a cross-entropy loss along rows (image to text) and columns (text to image) with the diagonal as the correct label, i.e. maximise similarity of the $N$ true pairs and minimise it for the $N^2-N$ wrong ones (Sec. 2.3). The paper credits the form to multi-class N-pair loss and InfoNCE. Why a contrastive target: it is an easier proxy than predicting exact words; Fig. 2 shows a transformer caption model learning ImageNet zero-shot 3x slower than a bag-of-words predictor, and the contrastive swap adds another 4x efficiency.

**Zero-shot classifier (Sec. 3.1.2):** for image embedding $\hat I$ and class-name text embeddings $\hat T_c$, predict $\arg\max_c \mathrm{softmax}_c(e^t\,\hat I\cdot\hat T_c)$. The paper observes this is a multinomial logistic regression with L2-normalised inputs and weights, no bias and temperature scaling, where the text encoder acts as a hypernetwork generating the weights. Prompt ensembling is done in embedding space (averaging text embeddings), so the cost is amortised (Sec. 3.1.4).

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Build WIT: 400M pairs, from a base query list of 500,000 queries (words occurring at least 100 times in English Wikipedia, plus bigrams, article names, WordNet synsets), up to 20,000 pairs per query (Sec. 2.2).
2. Image encoder: modified ResNet-50 family (ResNet-D, antialiased blur pooling, attention pooling in place of global average) or a ViT (`vit2020`, with an extra LayerNorm); text encoder: 12-layer, 512-wide, 8-head, 63M-parameter Transformer, BPE vocab 49,152, max length 76, feature at the [EOS] token (Sec. 2.4).
3. Train from scratch, no non-linear projection head, only random square crop as augmentation; Adam with decoupled weight decay, cosine schedule, 32 epochs, batch 32,768 (Sec. 2.3, 2.5).
4. Eight models: RN50, RN101, RN50x4/x16/x64 (4x/16x/64x RN50 compute), ViT-B/32, B/16, L/14. RN50x64 took 18 days on 592 V100s; the largest ViT took 12 days on 256 V100s; ViT-L/14 got one extra epoch at 336px, and this ViT-L/14@336px is "CLIP" in the paper (Sec. 2.5).
5. Inference: embed class prompts once, cache, take the nearest.

## 5. Achievements
- Table 1: zero-shot ImageNet 76.2% (vs Visual N-Grams 11.5%), matching the original ResNet-50 without using its 1.28M labelled images; aYahoo 98.4 vs 72.4; SUN 58.5 vs 23.0. Top-5 is 95%, matching Inception-V4 (Sec. 3.1.3).
- Prompt engineering plus ensembling improves ImageNet by almost 5%: the default prompt gives +1.3%, an 80-prompt ensemble another 3.5% (Sec. 3.1.4; Fig. 4).
- Fig. 5: zero-shot CLIP beats a fully supervised linear probe on ResNet-50 features on 16 of 27 datasets (e.g. +28.9 Stanford Cars, +22.5 Food101, +14.5 Kinetics700), and loses badly on EuroSAT (-37.1), KITTI Distance (-34.0), and others.
- Fig. 6/7: zero-shot matches a 4-shot linear probe on average; on ImageNet it matches 16-shot.
- Fig. 9: zero-shot error follows a smooth log-log trend over a 44x compute range.
- Sec. 3.2 / Fig. 10: best CLIP ViT-L/14@336px beats the best existing model by an average of 2.6% on Kornblith's 12-dataset suite; linear probe on CLIP beats Noisy Student EfficientNet-L2 on 21 of 27 datasets; CLIP ViTs are about 3x more compute efficient than CLIP ResNets.
- Sec. 3.3 / Fig. 13: zero-shot CLIP cuts the robustness gap under natural distribution shift by up to 75%; e.g. ImageNet-A 77.1 vs ResNet-101's 2.7 at equal 76.2 ImageNet accuracy. Fitting a classifier on ImageNet raises ImageNet accuracy by 9.2% (to 85.4%) while average shift accuracy slightly falls (Fig. 14).

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors (Sec. 6):** zero-shot is only on average competitive with a ResNet-50 linear probe, now well below overall SOTA; they estimate about 1000x more compute is needed for zero-shot CLIP to reach SOTA. Weak on fine-grained classes (cars, flowers, aircraft), counting, and novel tasks such as distance to nearest car. Poor on truly out-of-distribution data: only 88% on MNIST, below logistic regression on raw pixels. Output restricted to a given set of classes (no generation). Poor data efficiency (12.8 billion images seen, about 405 years at one per second). Validation sets were repeatedly queried during development; the 27-dataset suite was "somewhat haphazardly assembled" and co-adapted with CLIP. Web data is unfiltered and encodes social biases. Zero-shot underperforms few-shot partly because naive adding of few-shot data did not help (Sec. 3.1.5).
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** The WIT dataset and training recipe were not released, so open reproductions (e.g. LAION-based) came later. Contrastive embeddings are known to behave like bag-of-concepts, weak on compositional/relational and spatial text. Large batches are needed for the objective; later sigmoid-loss variants relaxed this.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `vit2020` (image encoder), `transformer2017` (text encoder), `gpt3_2020` (zero-shot/prompt framing), `adam2014`, `resnet2015`; ConVIRT, InfoNCE/N-pair loss, Visual N-Grams.
- **Led to:** `flamingo2022` (CLIP-style contrastive pre-trained vision encoder, per its text), `llava2023` (CLIP ViT-L/14 as vision encoder), `ldm2021`-era text-to-image conditioning and `sd3_2024` (hindsight), and the vision-language grounding used in `rt2_2023`, `openvla2024`, `pi0_2024` (hindsight; those use SigLIP/DINO-style encoders descended from this idea).
- **Single idea added:** train on web-scale image-caption matching so natural language becomes the interface to a vision model, enabling zero-shot classifiers and robust transfer.
