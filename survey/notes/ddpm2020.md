# Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models
**Authors, venue, year:** Jonathan Ho, Ajay Jain, Pieter Abbeel (UC Berkeley); NeurIPS 2020 (34th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems) · **Key:** `ddpm2020` · **OpenAlex citations:** 21097 · **Era:** df
**Read status:** full text (extraction of the figure area is garbled by embedded LaTeX blobs, but all text, equations, tables and the appendix were readable)

## 1. Problem and motivation
Diffusion probabilistic models (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015) were defined and cheap to train, but the paper states there had been "no demonstration that they are capable of generating high quality samples" (Sec. 1). At the time GANs, autoregressive models, flows and VAEs, plus score-matching/energy-based models, owned image synthesis quality. The paper shows that a specific parameterization and a simplified loss make diffusion models competitive, and links them to denoising score matching.

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Take an image and add a little Gaussian noise, a thousand times, until only noise remains (the fixed "forward process"). Then train one neural network to undo a single step of noise. Generation starts from pure noise and applies the learned denoiser repeatedly. The paper's key practical insight is to have the network predict the noise $\epsilon$ that was added, and to train it with a plain unweighted mean-squared error. This turns out to be a re-weighted variational bound and is equivalent to denoising score matching at many noise levels; sampling then looks like Langevin dynamics with a learned gradient. Analogy: restoring a statue by repeatedly guessing and removing a thin layer of dust.

## 3. The mathematics
**(a) Forward process and its closed form (Eqs. 2, 4)**
$$q(x_t|x_{t-1})=\mathcal N(x_t;\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1},\beta_t I),\qquad q(x_t|x_0)=\mathcal N(x_t;\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0,(1-\bar\alpha_t)I)$$
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $x_0$ | data (image scaled to $[-1,1]$) |
| $x_t$ | noised latent at step $t$, same dimensionality as data |
| $\beta_t$ | fixed variance schedule |
| $\alpha_t=1-\beta_t$, $\bar\alpha_t=\prod_{s\le t}\alpha_s$ | cumulative signal-retention factor |

Meaning: noise compounds, so $x_t$ can be sampled directly from $x_0$ for any $t$ without simulating the chain; this makes training cheap.

**(b) Variational bound decomposed (Eq. 5) and tractable posterior (Eqs. 6-7)**
$$L=\mathbb E_q\Big[\underbrace{D_{KL}(q(x_T|x_0)\|p(x_T))}_{L_T}+\sum_{t>1}\underbrace{D_{KL}(q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0)\|p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t))}_{L_{t-1}}\underbrace{-\log p_\theta(x_0|x_1)}_{L_0}\Big]$$
with $q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0)=\mathcal N(\tilde\mu_t(x_t,x_0),\tilde\beta_t I)$, $\tilde\mu_t=\frac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\beta_t}{1-\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_t}(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})}{1-\bar\alpha_t}x_t$, $\tilde\beta_t=\frac{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\beta_t$. $p_\theta$ is the learned reverse Gaussian with mean $\mu_\theta$ and fixed variance $\sigma_t^2 I$. Meaning: every KL is between two Gaussians, so it has a closed form; $L_T$ is constant since $\beta_t$ are fixed.

**(c) Noise-prediction parameterization (Eqs. 8-12)** Writing $x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon$, matching $\tilde\mu_t$ reduces to
$$\mu_\theta(x_t,t)=\frac1{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\Big(x_t-\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\Big),$$
and each term becomes $\mathbb E\big[\frac{\beta_t^2}{2\sigma_t^2\alpha_t(1-\bar\alpha_t)}\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,t)\|^2\big]$ (Eq. 12). Symbols: $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ the injected noise; $\epsilon_\theta$ the network's prediction. Meaning: this is a weighted denoising score matching loss over noise scales indexed by $t$, so the VLB and score matching coincide (Sec. 3.2). The sampler (Algorithm 2) $x_{t-1}=\frac1{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t-\frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\epsilon_\theta)+\sigma_t z$ resembles Langevin dynamics.

**(d) Simplified objective (Eq. 14)**
$$L_{simple}(\theta)=\mathbb E_{t,x_0,\epsilon}\big[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,t)\|^2\big],\ t\sim U\{1..T\}$$
Meaning: it drops the weighting of Eq. 12, which down-weights small-$t$ terms and lets the network focus on harder, noisier denoising tasks (Sec. 3.4). Not a bound on likelihood any more, but gives the best samples.

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Training (Algorithm 1): sample $x_0$, $t\sim U\{1..T\}$, $\epsilon$; gradient step on Eq. 14.
2. Sampling (Algorithm 2): $x_T\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$; iterate $t=T..1$ with the update above ($z=0$ at $t=1$).
3. Choices (Sec. 4, App. B): $T=1000$; $\beta_t$ linear from $10^{-4}$ to $0.02$; $\sigma_t^2=\beta_t$ (also tried $\tilde\beta_t$, similar); U-Net backbone like unmasked PixelCNN++ with group norm, sinusoidal time embedding, self-attention at 16x16; discrete decoder for $L_0$ (Eq. 13). Adam, lr $2\times10^{-4}$ ($2\times10^{-5}$ for 256x256), EMA 0.9999, dropout 0.1 on CIFAR10. CIFAR10 model 35.7M parameters; LSUN/CelebA-HQ 114M; larger LSUN Bedroom about 256M (App. B). TPU v3-8.

## 5. Achievements
- Unconditional CIFAR10: IS 9.46, FID 3.17 (Table 1), better than most models including class-conditional ones; FID 5.24 vs the test set. Compare StyleGAN2+ADA (v1) 3.26 FID, NCSN 25.32, SNGAN-DDLS 15.42 (Table 1).
- Ablation (Table 2): predicting $\epsilon$ with $L_{simple}$ gives FID 3.17 vs 13.22 (true VLB, $\tilde\mu$ prediction, fixed variance); learned variances were unstable.
- LSUN 256x256 FID (Table 3, appendix): Bedroom 6.36 (4.90 for the large model), Church 7.89, Cat 19.75; "similar to ProgressiveGAN" (8.34/6.42/37.52) but behind StyleGAN2 on Church/Cat.
- CIFAR10 NLL $\le 3.70$ (L) / $\le 3.75$ (Lsimple) bits/dim, test, gap to train at most 0.03 (Sec. 4.3). Rate 1.78, distortion 1.97 bits/dim; more than half the codelength encodes imperceptible detail.
- Progressive lossy decoding, link to autoregressive models (Eq. 16), latent interpolation (Sec. 4.4).

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors:** log-likelihoods are not competitive with other likelihood-based models (Sec. 1, 4.3); progressive compression is only a proof of concept because minimal random coding is intractable in high dimension (App.); sampling needs $T=1000$ network passes (CIFAR10: 17 s for a batch of 256; 256x256: 300 s for a batch of 128, App. B); broader-impact section flags misuse (fake imagery) and dataset bias.
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** slow sampling motivated DDIM, ODE solvers and distillation; fixed variances and linear schedule were later improved; evaluated only on small/mid-resolution unconditional images and no text or class conditioning, with pixel-space compute that LDM later reduced.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015 diffusion probabilistic models; NCSN / denoising score matching (Song & Ermon 2019); U-Net / PixelCNN++; `vae2013` (variational bound), `gan2014` (the quality baseline it competes with).
- **Led to:** `scoresde2020` (continuous-time generalization), `ldm2021`, `cfg2022`, `dit2022`, `flowmatching2022`, `rectflow2022`, `sd3_2024`, and later diffusion-based policies and world models (`diffpolicy2023`, `diamond2024`).
- **Single idea added:** train a U-Net to predict the added noise with an unweighted MSE, which makes diffusion models both simple and high quality and ties them to score matching.
