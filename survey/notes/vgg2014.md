# Very Deep Convolutional Networks for Large-Scale Image Recognition
**Simonyan & Zisserman, ICLR 2015 (arXiv:1409.1556v6; Visual Geometry Group, Oxford)** · **Key:** `vgg2014` · **OpenAlex citations:** 124836 · **Era:** dl
**Read status:** partial: main paper (Sections 1-5, Tables 1-7) read fully; Appendices A (localisation) and B (generalisation) read only for headline numbers; Appendix C not read.

## 1. Problem and motivation
After `alexnet2012`, improvements to ConvNets had mostly changed the first layers (smaller receptive window and stride) or training/testing over scales (Sec. 1). Depth, a key architectural dimension, had not been studied in isolation. The paper asks how accuracy on large-scale image recognition changes with depth when everything else is held fixed.

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Fix a simple design (only 3x3 convolutions with stride 1, 2x2 max-pooling, three FC layers) and just stack more layers, from 11 to 19 weight layers. Small filters make depth affordable: stacked 3x3 layers see large regions, use fewer parameters than one big filter, and add more nonlinearities. Analogy: build a large lens from many tiny identical ones instead of one custom big piece.

## 3. The mathematics
The paper has no named equations; the key quantitative argument is the receptive-field and parameter count in Sec. 2.3:
- Two stacked 3x3 conv layers (no pooling between) have an effective 5x5 receptive field; three have 7x7.
- With $C$ input and output channels, a stack of three 3x3 layers has $3(3^2C^2)=27C^2$ weights, while one 7x7 layer has $7^2C^2=49C^2$, i.e. 81% more.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $C$ | channels per layer (input = output) |
| $3^2C^2$ | weights of one 3x3 conv layer |

Meaning: the deep stack has fewer parameters for the same receptive field and applies ReLU three times instead of once, making the decision function "more discriminative"; the authors also read it as regularising the 7x7 filter to factor through 3x3 filters.

Training objective (Sec. 3.1): multinomial logistic regression optimised by mini-batch SGD with momentum 0.9, batch size 256, L2 weight decay $5\cdot10^{-4}$, dropout 0.5 on the first two FC layers, initial learning rate $10^{-2}$ divided by 10 when validation accuracy stalls (3 times), stopped after 370K iterations (74 epochs).

Dense evaluation (Sec. 3.2): FC layers are converted to convolutions (first FC to 7x7 conv, last two to 1x1), applied to the whole uncropped image, producing a class score map that is spatially averaged. Scale jittering: training scale $S$ sampled from $[S_{min},S_{max}]=[256,512]$; test scale $Q$ may differ from $S$.

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Input 224x224 RGB, mean-subtracted; conv layers all 3x3, stride 1, padding 1; five 2x2 max-pools, stride 2; FC 4096, FC 4096, FC 1000, softmax; ReLU throughout (Sec. 2.1).
2. Six configurations A-E (Table 1): A 11 weight layers, B 13, C 16 (adds 1x1 convs), D 16 (all 3x3), E 19. Channels 64, 128, 256, 512, 512. Parameters: 133M (A) to 144M (E) (Table 2).
3. Initialisation: train shallow A from random init, then initialise first four conv layers and last three FC layers of deeper nets from A (Sec. 3.1). Authors note Glorot and Bengio initialisation later made this unnecessary.
4. Augmentation: random 224 crops from rescaled images, horizontal flips, RGB colour shift, scale jittering.
5. Test: dense + multi-crop evaluation and averaging of softmax outputs over several test scales and flips; model ensembling.
6. Implementation: Caffe-derived, 4-GPU data parallelism, 3.75x speed-up; 2-3 weeks per net on four Titan Black GPUs (Sec. 3.3).

## 5. Achievements
- Single-scale validation error (Table 3): A 29.6/10.4 (top-1/top-5), B 28.7/9.9, D 25.6/8.1 and E 25.5/8.0 with training scale $[256;512]$ (test $Q=384$). LRN (A-LRN: 29.7/10.5) did not help.
- Multi-scale (Table 4): best single net 24.8% / 7.5%; test top-5 7.3% for config E (Sec. 4.2).
- Dense plus multi-crop (Table 5): D and E 24.4 / 7.2 and 24.4 / 7.1.
- Ensembles (Table 6): 7-net ILSVRC submission 7.3% test top-5; post-submission 2-net (D+E) 6.8% test top-5.
- ILSVRC-2014: second place in classification (7.3%), first in localisation with 25.3% error (abstract, App. A). GoogLeNet won classification with 6.7% (7 nets); VGG single net 7.0% test vs single GoogLeNet 7.9% (Table 7).
- Depth ablations: a five-layer 5x5 net derived from B has 7% higher top-1 error (center crop) than B (Sec. 4.1); 3x3 everywhere (D) beats mixing 1x1 (C). Error saturates at 19 layers on ImageNet.
- Transfer (Table 11, App. B): VOC-2007 89.3 and VOC-2012 89.0 mAP for D/E (89.7/89.3 combined); Caltech-101 92.7 +/- 0.5, Caltech-256 86.2 +/- 0.3 for D&E combined, using features with a linear SVM.

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors:** error saturates at 19 layers on this dataset (deeper might help larger data); deep nets initially needed pre-initialisation from the shallow net because random init could stall; training takes 2-3 weeks; multi-crop evaluation is more costly than dense evaluation for small gains (Sec. 3.2); large number of parameters (144M, mostly in FC layers, compared with 144M in Sermanet's shallower net).
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** plain stacking beyond about 20 layers degrades (the problem addressed in `resnet2015`); huge FC layers are memory heavy and inefficient; 3x3 stack at full resolution is computationally expensive (compared with GoogLeNet and later efficient nets); no normalisation layers, so training is delicate.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `alexnet2012` (ReLU, dropout, augmentation), `lenet1998` (classic ConvNet), `dropout2014`, Ciresan et al. 2011 (small filters), Network-in-Network (1x1 convs).
- **Led to:** `resnet2015` (deeper nets with shortcuts, uses VGG-style 3x3 design and VGG as the plain baseline); widely used as a feature extractor and backbone for detection, segmentation and style transfer (hindsight).
- Single idea added: depth with uniform small 3x3 filters is the key lever, giving a simple, reusable design.
