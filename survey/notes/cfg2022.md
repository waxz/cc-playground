# Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance
**Authors, venue, year:** Jonathan Ho, Tim Salimans (Google Research, Brain team); arXiv 2207.12598 (v1, July 2022); a short version appeared in the NeurIPS 2021 Workshop on Deep Generative Models and Downstream Applications (per the paper's footnote) · **Key:** `cfg2022` · **OpenAlex citations:** 7474 · **Era:** df
**Read status:** full text

## 1. Problem and motivation
Diffusion models could not do the "low temperature" or truncation trick that GANs (BigGAN truncation) and flows (Glow) use to trade diversity for per-sample quality; naive attempts such as scaling scores or reducing noise gave blurry samples (Sec. 3). Classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol 2021) fixed this by adding the gradient of a separate classifier, but needed an extra classifier trained on noisy images, complicated the pipeline, and raised the question whether FID/IS gains simply came from adversarially attacking an image classifier (Sec. 1).

## 2. Core idea in one paragraph
Train one diffusion network that is sometimes given the condition (e.g. class label) and sometimes a "null" token, by randomly dropping the condition during training. At sampling time compute both the conditional and unconditional noise predictions and extrapolate from the unconditional one toward the conditional one. The extrapolation strength $w$ plays the role of the classifier-guidance weight: higher $w$ gives sharper, more prototypical but less diverse samples. No classifier is trained. Analogy: ask the model "what would you draw for this label?" and "what would you draw for anything?", then exaggerate the difference.

## 3. The mathematics
**(a) Classifier guidance (Sec. 3.1, for reference)**
$$\tilde\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda,c)=\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda,c)-w\sigma_\lambda\nabla_{z_\lambda}\log p_\theta(c|z_\lambda)\approx-\sigma_\lambda\nabla_{z_\lambda}[\log p(z_\lambda|c)+w\log p_\theta(c|z_\lambda)]$$
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $z_\lambda=\alpha_\lambda x+\sigma_\lambda\epsilon$ | noised data at log-SNR $\lambda$ ($\lambda=\log\alpha_\lambda^2/\sigma_\lambda^2$) |
| $\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda,c)$ | noise prediction, $\approx-\sigma_\lambda\nabla\log p(z_\lambda|c)$ |
| $w$ | guidance strength |
| $p_\theta(c|z_\lambda)$ | auxiliary classifier on noisy data |

Meaning: samples approximately from $\tilde p_\theta(z_\lambda|c)\propto p_\theta(z_\lambda|c)\,p_\theta(c|z_\lambda)^w$, up-weighting data a classifier recognizes.

**(b) Training objective (Eq. 5 with Algorithm 1)**
$$\mathbb E_{\epsilon,\lambda}\big[\|\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda)-\epsilon\|_2^2\big],\quad\text{with }c\leftarrow\varnothing\text{ with probability }p_{uncond}$$
Meaning: ordinary denoising score matching over noise levels; the same network learns $\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda,c)$ and $\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda)=\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda,c=\varnothing)$. $\lambda$ is drawn as $\lambda=-2\log\tan(au+b)$ with $u\sim U[0,1]$, a bounded hyperbolic-secant distribution inspired by the cosine schedule.

**(c) Classifier-free guided score (Eq. 6, Algorithm 2)**
$$\tilde\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda,c)=(1+w)\,\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda,c)-w\,\epsilon_\theta(z_\lambda)$$
Meaning: a linear extrapolation away from the unconditional score. Derivation sketch (Sec. 3.2): with exact scores, the implicit classifier $p^i(c|z_\lambda)\propto p(z_\lambda|c)/p(z_\lambda)$ has gradient $-\frac1{\sigma_\lambda}[\epsilon^*(z_\lambda,c)-\epsilon^*(z_\lambda)]$, and plugging it into (a) gives exactly (c). The authors stress that with a learned unconstrained network the difference need not be a gradient of any classifier (non-conservative field), so (c) is "inspired by" rather than equal to classifier guidance.

## 4. Method / architecture / algorithm
1. Train (Algorithm 1): sample $(x,c)$; with probability $p_{uncond}$ replace $c$ by $\varnothing$; sample $\lambda$, $\epsilon$; gradient step on the MSE.
2. Sample (Algorithm 2): at each of $T$ steps evaluate the network twice (conditional and unconditional), form Eq. 6, convert to $\tilde x_t=(z_t-\sigma_{\lambda_t}\tilde\epsilon_t)/\alpha_{\lambda_t}$, and take an ancestral step with variance interpolation hyperparameter $v$ (any other sampler, e.g. DDIM, can be used).
3. Setup (Sec. 4): same architecture/hyperparameters as ADM (Dhariwal & Nichol) but continuous-time training; ImageNet 64x64 (400K steps, $v=0.3$) and 128x128 (2.7M steps, $v=0.2$); $\lambda_{min}=-20$, $\lambda_{max}=20$; $w\in\{0,0.1,\dots,4\}$; FID/IS on 50000 samples.

## 5. Achievements
- Clear FID/IS trade-off controlled by $w$ (Tables 1, 2; Figs. 4, 5): FID decreases then increases, IS increases monotonically.
- ImageNet 64x64 (Table 1): FID 1.55 and IS 66.11 at $w=0.1$ ($p_{uncond}=0.1$), vs ADM FID 2.07 and CDM 1.48/67.95; IS 260.2 at $w=4.0$.
- ImageNet 128x128 (Table 2, $T=256$): FID 2.43 at $w=0.3$ (IS 158.47), beating ADM-G 2.97, CDM 3.52 and BigGAN-deep 5.7; at $w=4.0$ IS 421.03, beating BigGAN-deep's max-IS point (FID 25, IS 253). Authors call the 128x128 results state of the art in the literature.
- $p_{uncond}$ study (Sec. 4.2): 0.5 is consistently worse than 0.1 or 0.2, so only a small share of capacity is needed for the unconditional task.
- Shows a pure generative model can boost classifier-based metrics without classifier gradients.

## 6. Limitations
**Stated by the authors:** sampling is slower than classifier guidance because each step needs two forward passes (conditional and unconditional); a fair comparison to ADM-G is $T=128$, where the method does worse in FID (Sec. 4.3); guidance reduces diversity, which may harm underrepresented groups (Sec. 5); hyperparameters were tuned for classifier guidance and are possibly suboptimal; strongly guided samples display saturated colors (Fig. 3 caption); no theoretical guarantee since the implicit classifier from Bayes' rule can be inconsistent (Sec. 3.2). Experiments are a proof of concept on ImageNet at 64x64 and 128x128 only.
**Seen in hindsight (hindsight):** the technique became the default conditioning-strength knob in text-to-image systems (`ldm2021` already uses it), and later work on distilling the two passes into one; the null-token trick extends naturally to text prompts.

## 7. Place in the lineage
- **Builds on:** `ddpm2020`, `scoresde2020`, classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol 2021), GAN truncation (`gan2014`-era BigGAN).
- **Led to:** `ldm2021` (uses c.f.g. in Tables 2-3), `dit2022` (all headline results use it), `sd3_2024`, and conditional diffusion policies/world models; also the "guidance scale" in nearly all text-to-image diffusion.
- **Single idea added:** drop the condition at random during training and extrapolate conditional minus unconditional scores to get guidance with no classifier.

## 8. Verification notes
Figure 5's legend lists T = 128/256/512 whereas Table 2 and Sec. 4.3 list T = 128/256/1024; the table and text values were used. Best FID in Table 1 for $p_{uncond}=0.1$ at $w=0.1$ is 1.55; the paper says best FID at $w=0.1$ or 0.3 depending on dataset.
